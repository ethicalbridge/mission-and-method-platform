# Who this policy applies to

This policy applies to everyone who budgets, spends, approves, receives or records money for [[Organisation name]]: board members, staff, volunteers and contractors. It covers all our funds, whatever the source, and all accounts, cash, mobile money, cards and assets we hold. Partners that receive funds from us follow the financial terms in their Sub-grant Agreement.

**Law and standards.** We keep accounts as required by the law of [[country]] and by our [[regulator / registration body]]. We follow [[accounting standard or framework]]. Where a funder's financial rules are stricter than this policy, we follow them for that funder's money.

::: adapt Law, accounting standards and amounts
Ask your accountant which accounting rules apply to you: they depend on your legal form, size and country. For example, UK charities follow the Charities SORP, and many countries follow national standards based on International Financial Reporting Standards, with simpler versions for small entities. Check your tax registration, filing deadlines and any duty to file accounts with a regulator. Every amount in this policy is an example only. Set amounts that suit your income, your currency and your risks, and make them match your Delegation of Authority Matrix.
:::

# Policy

## 1. Roles and separation of duties

Separation of duties means that no single person controls a transaction from start to finish. We split these tasks between at least two people:

{widths: 50 50}
| Task | Who does it |
|---|---|
| Request a payment | Budget holder or staff member |
| Approve the payment | An approver named in the Delegation of Authority Matrix, never the requester |
| Make the payment (enter it in online banking or mobile money) | Finance lead |
| Authorise the payment in the bank or mobile money system | A second signatory, never the person who entered it |
| Record and reconcile | Finance lead |
| Review the reconciliation | Treasurer |

### When our team is too small

Where one person must do more than one of these tasks, we use compensating controls. These are extra checks that make up for the missing separation:

- the treasurer reviews every bank and mobile money statement each month, line by line, against the records;
- the treasurer has read-only online access to all our bank accounts;
- no one approves a payment to themselves, including salary, expenses or advances;
- payments to the director are approved by the treasurer or chair;
- our external accountant reviews a sample of transactions every [[quarter]];
- the board looks at a list of all payments above [[USD 500]] every [[quarter]];
- where the finance lead both enters and reconciles payments, the treasurer receives bank statements directly from the bank, not through the finance lead.

## 2. Budgets and budget holders

- The board approves an annual budget for the whole organisation before the start of each financial year, which runs from [[1 January to 31 December]].
- Each project or grant has its own budget, which follows the funder's agreement.
- The director names a budget holder for each budget. Budget holders approve spending within their budget, up to their limit, and explain variances.
- Any spending not in the budget, or above a budget line by more than [[10%]], needs approval as set out in the Delegation of Authority Matrix, and the funder's agreement where the funds are restricted.
- We prepare a cash flow forecast for at least the next [[6 months]] and update it every month.

## 3. Bank accounts

- All bank accounts are in the full legal name of [[Organisation name]]. We never hold our money in a personal account, even for a short time.
- The board approves the opening and closing of every account, and the list of signatories, by resolution.
- Every account has at least [[two]] signatories, and every payment needs [[two]] of them. Close associates may not be the only two signatories on any account.
- Online banking is set up so that one person enters a payment and a second person approves it.
- Cheque books, cards, tokens and passwords are kept securely. We never sign blank cheques. Passwords are never shared.
- When a signatory leaves, we remove them from the account within [[5 working days]].
- Where possible, we hold restricted grant funds in a separate account if the funder requires it.

::: adapt Bank accounts and mobile money
Name your bank accounts and signatories in an annex or board minute, not in this policy. Some countries have special rules: for example, in India foreign contributions must first be received in a designated "FCRA account" at the State Bank of India, New Delhi Main Branch, under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act 2010 as amended in 2020. Some funders require a separate bank account for their grant. If your bank only allows one signatory for online banking, ask about dual approval or add the treasurer as a second user. If you use mobile money, ask your provider about business accounts with more than one approver.
:::

## 4. Payments and approvals

1. Every payment must be in the budget and supported by an original invoice, receipt, contract or other evidence.
2. Every payment is approved in advance, within the limits in our Delegation of Authority Matrix. Approval is recorded by a signature, an email or the accounting system.
3. Purchases above set amounts need quotes or a tender, as set out in our Procurement Policy.
4. We pay suppliers by bank transfer or mobile money to an account in their own name. We pay cash only where there is no other practical option.
5. Before we pay new bank details, or changed details, we confirm them by calling the supplier on a number we already have, not one given in the email. Fraudsters often send fake bank details by email.
6. Travel costs, per diems and expense claims follow our Expenses and Per Diem Policy.
7. Advances to staff for activities are recorded, approved and accounted for (retired) within [[5 working days]] of the end of the activity or trip, as in our Expenses and Per Diem Policy, with receipts and any unspent cash returned. No new advance is given until the last one is accounted for.

## 5. Cash and petty cash

- We keep as little cash as possible. The petty cash float is no more than [[USD 300]], held by [[Name, role]] in a locked box.
- Single petty cash payments are no more than [[USD 50]]. Larger amounts are paid by transfer.
- Every payment from petty cash has a receipt or signed voucher.
- We top up the float only to its fixed level, against receipts for what was spent (this is called an imprest system).
- Petty cash is counted and reconciled at least [[monthly]], and the count is signed by the cash holder and a second person. The treasurer or director makes a surprise count at least [[twice a year]].
- Cash received, for example at events, is counted by two people, recorded, and banked within [[3 working days]].
- Cash carried for field activities is kept to the minimum needed, split between people where safe, and recorded when it is issued and returned.

## 6. Mobile money

Mobile money is often the safest, fastest and most transparent way to pay people in the communities we serve. We use it under these rules:

- Mobile money accounts are registered in the name of [[Organisation name]], as a business or organisation account, never on a staff member's personal number.
- Where the provider allows, payments need a second approver. PINs are never shared.
- We keep a record of each payment: date, recipient, phone number, amount, purpose and budget line, linked to a signed list or other evidence.
- We download the full statement at least [[monthly]] and reconcile it with our records, like a bank account.
- Transfers between mobile money and bank accounts are recorded on both sides.
- We record transaction charges as a cost, and include them in budgets.
- If a staff member must ever receive funds on their personal number in an emergency, the director approves it in writing in advance, and the staff member provides a statement and receipts within [[3 working days]].

## 7. Receipts and evidence

Every payment needs evidence that it was genuine, approved and for our work. We accept:

- a supplier's invoice and receipt, showing their name, date, items and amount;
- for small purchases where no receipt is available, such as local transport or market goods, a payment voucher signed by the person who paid and approved by their manager, explaining why there is no receipt;
- for cash or mobile money paid to participants, such as transport refunds or stipends, a list of everyone paid with their name, amount, date and signature or thumbprint, a witness signature and the attendance list for the activity.

Photocopied, altered or handwritten receipts where printed ones are normal are warning signs. We follow up on them, as set out in our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure.

::: adapt Paying participants
Set rules that respect people's dignity and privacy. Collect only the information you need on payment lists, and keep them securely under your Data Protection Policy. Where people cannot sign, use a thumbprint or a witness. Check funders' rules: some set limits on participant payments or require a specific form. In some countries payments to individuals above a certain amount must be reported for tax. Check your local tax rules.
:::

## 8. Income

- We record all income, from every source, in our accounts.
- Grant agreements are signed by those named in the Delegation of Authority Matrix and recorded in a grants register showing the amount, period, restrictions and reporting dates.
- Donations follow our Gift Acceptance Policy. We issue receipts where the law or the donor requires.
- Income from sales or services is invoiced promptly and followed up when overdue.

## 9. Payroll

- Everyone we pay a salary has a written contract and a personnel file.
- Pay rates and changes are approved as set out in the Delegation of Authority Matrix. Nobody approves their own pay.
- Payroll is prepared by [[Name, role / payroll provider]] and approved by a second person before payment.
- We deduct and pay income tax, social security and other statutory contributions on time, as required by the law of [[country]].
- Salaries are paid by bank transfer or mobile money, never in cash, unless the director approves an exception in writing.
- Staff whose time is charged to a grant complete timesheets, approved by their manager, so that we can show the time spent.
- When someone leaves, we remove them from the payroll from their last working day.

## 10. Monthly reconciliations

By the [[10th]] working day of each month, the finance lead reconciles every bank account, mobile money account and cash float with our records. Reconciliation means checking that our records match the statement and explaining any difference. The treasurer reviews and signs every reconciliation within [[5 working days]] and asks about anything unusual. Unexplained differences are investigated straight away.

## 11. Restricted and unrestricted funds

- **Restricted funds** can only be used for the purpose the funder or donor set. **Unrestricted funds** can be used for any of our purposes, as the board decides. **Designated funds** are unrestricted funds the board has set aside for a purpose; the board can change this.
- Every transaction is coded to a fund or grant and a budget line in our accounts.
- We do not use one restricted fund to pay for another fund's costs, even temporarily, unless both funders agree in writing.
- Shared costs, such as rent or the finance lead's time, are charged to grants using a fair, documented method, for example time spent or number of staff, and only as each funder allows.
- Unspent restricted funds at the end of a grant are handled as the agreement says: returned, carried forward, or reallocated with the funder's written approval.

## 12. Foreign currency and exchange rates

- We record transactions in [[reporting currency]].
- When we receive funds in another currency, we record the rate actually received from the bank on the date of conversion, with the bank advice as evidence.
- For reports to funders, we use the method their agreement requires. If there is none, we use [[the actual rate of each conversion / a weighted average of the conversions for that grant]], and say so in the report.
- We record exchange gains and losses separately. A loss on a restricted grant may need to be covered from unrestricted funds unless the funder agrees otherwise.
- We do not exchange money through informal dealers unless there is no alternative, the director approves it in writing, and we record the rate and the reason.

::: adapt Exchange rates
Choose one method and use it consistently. Funders often say which rate to use, so check each grant agreement. If you work in a country with more than one official rate, or a large gap between official and market rates, take advice from your accountant and tell funders how you will handle it before the grant starts. Ask your bank about holding a foreign currency account to reduce conversion costs.
:::

## 13. Assets register

- We record every item worth more than [[USD 300]], and all laptops, phones and vehicles whatever their value, in an assets register.
- The register shows the description, serial number, date bought, cost, funder, location and who uses it.
- Each asset is labelled with our name and a reference number.
- We check the register against the physical assets at least [[once a year]].
- Assets bought with grant funds are used and disposed of as the funder requires. Disposals and write-offs are approved by [[the director / the board above a set value]] and recorded.

## 14. Management accounts and reporting

Every [[quarter]], the finance lead prepares management accounts for the board. They include:

- income and spending against budget, with explanations for significant differences;
- the cash position and cash flow forecast;
- balances of restricted, designated and unrestricted funds, and our reserves position under our Reserves Policy;
- grant reporting deadlines and any funder concerns;
- any losses, irregularities or control weaknesses.

The director and finance lead also review a short monthly summary. We send financial reports to funders on time, as each agreement requires.

## 15. Annual accounts, audit and independent examination

- We prepare annual accounts within [[6 months]] of the end of our financial year.
- Our accounts are [[audited by a registered auditor / independently examined by a qualified person]], as the law, our governing document or our funders require. The board appoints the auditor or examiner.
- The board approves the annual accounts and the auditor's or examiner's report, and reviews any management letter and the action taken.
- We file the accounts with [[regulator / tax authority]] by [[deadline]], and share them with funders who ask.

::: adapt Audit and independent examination
The rules depend on your country, legal form and income. For example, in England and Wales, a charity's need for an independent examination or a full audit depends on its income and assets, and the thresholds change from time to time; check the current figures with the Charity Commission. In many countries every registered NGO must file audited accounts each year. Some funders also require a project audit of their grant. An independent examination is a lighter review than an audit. Check your local law on NGO or company accounts and your grant agreements, and budget for the cost.
:::

# Records, confidentiality and data

- We keep financial records, including receipts, vouchers, statements, payroll and contracts, for at least [[7 years]] after the end of the financial year, or longer if the law or a funder requires. Our Records Retention Schedule gives the details.
- We keep original paper documents in a locked place and scan them to [[our shared drive / accounting system]]. Electronic records are backed up at least [[weekly]].
- Every document is referenced so that it can be matched to its transaction.
- Payroll and participant payment records contain personal information. Access is limited to those who need it, under our Data Protection Policy.

# If this policy is breached

A breach of this policy, such as approving your own payment, using a personal account or skipping approval, may lead to disciplinary action, the end of a role or contract, and recovery of any loss. Report any breach or concern to the finance lead or the treasurer. If you suspect fraud, follow our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure.

# Monitoring and review

The treasurer reports to the board [[every quarter]] on whether these controls are working, including late reconciliations and any exceptions approved. The board reviews this policy [[every year]], and after any audit finding, loss or major change in our funding.
