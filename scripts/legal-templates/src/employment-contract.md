::: adapt Before you use this agreement
**Use it** when you hire someone as an employee: they work under your direction, as part of your team, for a salary. **Do not use it** for volunteers (use the Volunteer Agreement), for freelancers who run their own business (use the Independent Contractor Agreement), or for staff hired for you by an employer of record, who will issue its own contract.

**Employment law is mandatory and local.** The law of the country where the employee normally works usually applies, whatever this contract says. You cannot contract out of minimum rights, and a term that gives less than the law allows is usually void. Before you issue this contract, check the local rules on:

- **Written particulars:** many countries list what a contract must contain and when it must be given, for example the UK (Employment Rights Act 1996), the EU (Directive (EU) 2019/1152), Kenya (Employment Act 2007) and South Africa (Basic Conditions of Employment Act 1997).
- **Minimum wage, working-time limits and overtime pay.**
- **Statutory leave:** annual, public holidays, sick, maternity, paternity and parental leave.
- **Registration and payments:** social security, health insurance, pension and income tax withholding. Many countries require you to register as an employer before the first payday.
- **Probation, termination procedures, notice and severance.** Several countries protect employees against unfair dismissal and set severance formulas.
- **Language:** some places require the contract in the local language, for example France, Québec in Canada and Indonesia. Always give the employee a version they fully understand.
- **Collective agreements** for your sector.

**Hiring in another country** may require you to register as an employer there or use an employer of record. Do not call an employee a contractor to avoid this.

**Get local review** from an employment lawyer or labour adviser before you issue the contract, and delete every orange box.
:::

# Agreed terms

## 1. Definitions and interpretation

1. In this contract:
  1. **"Applicable law"** means the employment, tax, social security, data protection and other laws that apply to your employment in [[country]].
  2. **"Confidential information"** means information about our work, finances, funders, partners, staff, volunteers and the people we serve that is not public, in any form.
  3. **"Intellectual property"** means copyright, database rights, trademarks, designs, know-how and similar rights anywhere in the world.
  4. **"Policies"** means the policies and procedures we adopt from time to time, as listed in clause 19.
  5. **"Working day"** means a day other than a weekend day or public holiday in [[country or place of work]].
2. Headings are for convenience only. Where this contract conflicts with applicable law, applicable law prevails. Where a Policy conflicts with this contract, this contract prevails.

## 2. Start date and continuous service

1. Your employment starts on [[start date]].
2. Your period of continuous employment began on [[the start date above / an earlier date, when you started with us in another role]].
3. Your employment is [[permanent (open-ended) / fixed-term until end date, as set out in clause 21]].

## 3. Job title and duties

1. You are employed as [[job title]]. You report to [[manager's role]].
2. Your main duties are set out in the job description in Schedule 1. You will also carry out other reasonable duties that match your skills and experience.
3. We may update the job description after discussing it with you, but will not make major changes without your agreement unless applicable law allows it. You agree to work honestly, with care and skill, and to follow our reasonable instructions.

## 4. Place of work and remote work

1. Your normal place of work is [[our office at address / your home in city, country / a mix of our office and home]].
2. You may work remotely for up to [[number]] days a week, by agreement with your manager. When you work remotely, you agree to follow our IT and data security rules and to keep a safe working environment.
3. You may need to travel within [[country / region]] for your work. Travel outside [[country]] requires your agreement and follows our Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy.
4. You must not work from another country for more than [[10 working days]] in any [[12-month period]] without our written approval, because this can create tax and legal obligations.

::: adapt Remote work and working from abroad
Decide whether the role is office-based, remote or hybrid. If the employee will work mostly from another country, stop: that country's employment, tax and social security law will probably apply, and you may need to register there or use an employer of record. Some countries require employers to provide home-working equipment or an allowance, or give a right to request remote work. Check your local law on remote work.
:::

## 5. Probation

1. The first [[three / six]] months of your employment are a probation period.
2. During probation, your manager will review your work with you at least [[monthly]], tell you in writing of any concerns and give you a fair chance to improve.
3. We may extend probation once, by up to [[three months]], if we tell you in writing before it ends and explain why, where applicable law allows this.
4. During probation, either of us may end your employment by giving [[one week's]] written notice, or the minimum notice required by applicable law if longer.
5. We will confirm in writing when you have completed probation successfully.

::: adapt Probation limits
Many countries cap probation. Examples: the EU directive generally limits it to six months; Kenya allows six months, extendable by agreement to twelve (Employment Act 2007); the Philippines allows six months; Brazil allows an experience contract of up to 90 days; Indonesia allows three months and none in fixed-term contracts. Argentina extended its standard probation to six months in 2024 (Law 27.742) and passed a further labour reform in 2026; the UK Employment Rights Act 2025 cuts the qualifying period for unfair dismissal protection from two years to six months for dismissals from 1 January 2027. Check the current rules. A dismissal during probation must still be fair and non-discriminatory.
:::

## 6. Hours of work and overtime

1. Your normal working hours are [[40]] hours a week, [[Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:30]], with an unpaid lunch break of [[one hour]]. [[For part-time staff: number hours a week on named days.]]
2. You may sometimes need to work extra hours, for example before deadlines or in emergencies, but never beyond the maximum allowed by applicable law.
3. Overtime must be approved in advance by your manager. [[Choose one: We pay approved overtime at 1.5 times your hourly rate. / You may take time off in lieu of approved overtime within three months. / Your salary reflects the senior nature of your role, and no extra pay is due for additional hours, where applicable law allows.]]
4. You are entitled to rest breaks and daily and weekly rest periods of at least the length required by applicable law.

## 7. Salary and pay day

1. Your gross salary is [[amount and currency]] per [[year / month]], [[pro rata for part-time staff]]. Schedule 2 summarises your pay and benefits.
2. We pay your salary [[monthly in arrears]] on or before the [[last working day]] of each month, by bank transfer to the account you nominate in your name.
3. We give you a payslip each pay day, showing your gross pay, each deduction and your net pay.
4. We pay a [[thirteenth-month salary / annual bonus required by law]] of [[amount]] in [[month]]. [[Delete if not applicable.]]
5. We review salaries [[once a year in month]]. A review does not guarantee an increase.

## 8. Deductions, tax and social security

1. We deduct from your salary the income tax, social security, health insurance and pension amounts that applicable law requires, and pay them to the authorities on time.
2. We will make other deductions only where applicable law allows and you have agreed in writing beforehand, for example to repay a salary advance.
3. We register you with [[name of social security agency / pension fund / health insurer]] within [[the period required by law]] and pay the employer's share of these payments.

::: adapt Pay, overtime and deductions
Check that the salary meets the national or sector minimum wage for the hours worked. Overtime examples: the US Fair Labor Standards Act requires one and a half times the regular rate after 40 hours a week for non-exempt employees; the Philippines Labor Code sets a premium of at least 25% on ordinary days; Brazil's Constitution sets at least 50%. The EU Working Time Directive 2003/88/EC limits average weekly hours to 48. A thirteenth-month salary is mandatory in several countries, including the Philippines, Brazil and Argentina, and Indonesia requires a religious holiday allowance. Most countries strictly limit deductions from pay.
:::

## 9. Benefits

1. You are entitled to the benefits set out in Schedule 2, for example [[health insurance / pension / transport allowance / mobile phone allowance]].
2. Benefits provided through an insurer or other third party follow its rules. We may change or replace a benefit that is not required by law after consulting you and giving you [[one month's]] notice, as long as the overall value is not reduced without your agreement.

## 10. Expenses

1. We reimburse reasonable expenses you incur for our work, in line with our Expenses and Per Diem Policy.
2. Submit claims with receipts within [[30 days]].
3. A false or inflated claim is gross misconduct.

## 11. Annual leave and public holidays

1. You are entitled to [[number]] working days of paid annual leave in each leave year, [[pro rata for part-time staff and for part years]]. The leave year runs from [[1 January to 31 December]].
2. You are also entitled to paid leave on the public holidays observed in [[country or place of work]]. If you work on a public holiday at our request, you receive [[a day off in lieu / pay at the rate required by law]].
3. Please agree leave dates with your manager at least [[two weeks]] in advance. We will not refuse a request unreasonably.
4. You may carry over up to [[five]] days of unused leave to the next leave year, to be used by [[31 March]], unless applicable law allows more. We encourage you to take all your leave: rest protects your health and our work.
5. When your employment ends, we pay you for leave you have earned but not taken.

## 12. Sickness absence and sick pay

1. If you are unable to work because of illness or injury, tell your manager by [[10:00]] on the first day, or as soon as you reasonably can.
2. For absences of more than [[three]] consecutive days, please give us a medical certificate.
3. We pay [[your full salary for up to number working days in any 12-month period, and then statutory sick pay / statutory sick pay only]], in line with applicable law.
4. We treat health information as sensitive data. If you have a disability or long-term health condition, we will discuss reasonable adjustments with you.

## 13. Family leave

1. You are entitled to maternity, paternity, parental, adoption and other family leave and pay in line with applicable law. [[We offer enhanced family leave, as set out in Schedule 2.]]
2. Please give us the notice the law requires, so that we can plan cover. You will not be treated unfavourably because you are pregnant, take family leave or have caring responsibilities.
3. You are entitled to return to your job, or a suitable equivalent role where applicable law allows, on terms no less favourable than before your leave.

::: adapt Statutory leave: country notes
Set annual leave at or above the legal minimum. Some examples you can check against (rules change, so confirm the current law):

## 14. Other leave

1. You may take up to [[three]] days of paid compassionate leave on the death or serious illness of a close family member, and more by agreement.
2. You may take reasonable time off, [[paid / unpaid]], for public duties, voting, emergencies involving dependants and any other leave the law provides.
3. If you are affected by domestic violence, you may take [[up to five days of]] confidential paid leave to seek safety or support.

## 15. Confidentiality

1. You must keep confidential information confidential, during and after your employment. Use it only to do your job, and share it only with people who are authorised to receive it.
2. This does not stop you from:
  1. disclosing information when the law requires it;
  2. raising a concern under our Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure, or with a regulator, the police or another authority; or
  3. discussing your own pay and conditions, where applicable law protects this.

## 16. Data protection

1. We process your personal data to manage your employment, pay you, meet our legal obligations and protect our legitimate interests, as explained in our Privacy Notice [[for staff]] and our Data Protection Policy.
2. In your work, you must handle the personal data of others, including the people we serve, in line with our Data Protection Policy and our IT and Information Security Policy.
3. You must report any actual or suspected data breach to [[role]] within [[24 hours]] of becoming aware of it, following our Data Breach Response Procedure.

## 17. Intellectual property

1. Intellectual property in anything you create in the course of your employment, alone or with others, belongs to [[Organisation name]] from the moment it is created. Examples include reports, training materials, photographs, software, databases and designs.
2. Where the law does not give us ownership automatically, you assign these rights, including future rights, to us by this contract, and will sign any document reasonably needed to confirm this, at our cost.
3. Where applicable law allows, you waive your moral rights in this work, so that we can adapt, edit and publish it without naming you. We will credit you where it is practical and appropriate.
4. Things you create in your own time, without using our resources or confidential information, and that are not related to our work, remain yours.

::: adapt Intellectual property
In many countries the employer owns work an employee creates in the course of employment (for example under the UK Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 and US work-made-for-hire rules). In others, especially civil-law countries, the author keeps copyright and the employer gets usage rights, so the written assignment matters. Moral rights (to be named and to object to distortion) cannot be waived in some countries, such as France, Brazil and Argentina. If you publish open-licence materials, say so.
:::

## 18. Conflicts of interest and outside work

1. You must declare any actual, possible or perceived conflict of interest to [[role]] as soon as you become aware of it, in line with our Conflict of Interest Policy.
2. You may take on other paid or unpaid work outside your working hours, provided it:
  1. does not conflict with our interests or compete with our work;
  2. does not affect your performance, safety or rest; and
  3. does not use our time, equipment, name or confidential information.
3. Please tell your manager in writing before you start any other regular work. We will not object unreasonably.

## 19. Code of Conduct and policies

1. You agree to follow our Code of Conduct and our Policies, including:
  1. Safeguarding Policy and Procedure;
  2. Anti-Harassment, Intimidation and Bullying Policy and Procedure;
  3. Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure;
  4. Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure;
  5. Data Protection Policy and IT and Information Security Policy;
  6. Expenses and Per Diem Policy and Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy; and
  7. [[any other policies you have]].
2. The Policies are at [[location]]. They do not form part of this contract unless they say so, and we may update them, telling you about significant changes.
3. A serious breach of the Code of Conduct, including sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment, fraud or bribery, is gross misconduct and may lead to dismissal without notice.

## 20. Safeguarding and pre-employment checks

1. Your employment is conditional on:
  1. satisfactory references from [[two]] previous employers, including a check on safeguarding concerns, following the principles of the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme;
  2. proof of your identity and of your right to work in [[country]];
  3. [[a satisfactory criminal record or police clearance check appropriate to the role]]; and
  4. [[proof of the qualifications required for the role]].
2. If a check is not completed before your start date, you may start work under supervision, and we may end your employment with [[one week's]] notice if the result is not satisfactory.
3. You must tell [[the safeguarding lead]] promptly if, during your employment, you are charged with or convicted of any criminal offence, or become the subject of any investigation, that could affect your suitability to work with children, adults at risk or money.

::: adapt Safeguarding checks
Use criminal record checks only where the role justifies them, for example contact with children or adults at risk, or control of money. Systems differ: the UK uses Disclosure and Barring Service checks; Kenya issues a Certificate of Good Conduct through the Directorate of Criminal Investigations; South Africa has a National Register for Sex Offenders and a Child Protection Register; many countries issue a police clearance certificate. Some countries and US states restrict when you may ask about convictions. Treat this information as highly sensitive data. Where no reliable check exists, rely on careful references, a self-declaration and supervision.
:::

## 21. Grant-funded posts

1. [[Delete this clause if the post is not funded by a specific grant.]]
2. Your post is funded by a grant from [[funder name]] for the project [[project name]], which is currently due to end on [[date]].
3. **Option A, fixed-term:** your employment is for a fixed term starting on [[start date]] and ending on [[end date]]. It will end on that date without further notice, unless we agree in writing to extend it. We will tell you at least [[two months]] before the end date whether we expect to extend it.
4. **Option B, open-ended but funding-dependent:** your employment is open-ended. If the funding ends or is reduced, we will consult you, look for other funding and for suitable alternative roles, and, if your post must end, give you notice under clause 23 and any severance required by applicable law.
5. Either way, if the funding ends early, we will tell you as soon as we know, consult you and act fairly and in line with applicable law.

::: adapt Grant-funded posts: handle with care
The end of a grant does not automatically end an employment contract. In many countries it is a redundancy, which may require consultation, notice and severance. Repeatedly renewed fixed-term contracts can become permanent: the EU Fixed-term Work Directive 1999/70/EC requires limits on successive contracts, and in the UK four years or more of successive fixed-term contracts usually makes the employee permanent unless justified (Fixed-term Employees Regulations 2002). Some countries, such as Brazil and Indonesia, allow fixed-term contracts only in specific cases and for limited periods, and courts in several countries have treated repeated renewals as creating an expectation of permanent work. Choose Option A or B with local advice, delete the other, and budget for notice and severance in grant proposals.
:::

## 22. Disciplinary and grievance procedures

1. Our Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure, at [[location]], explains how we deal with concerns about conduct or performance. It does not form part of this contract.
2. Before any disciplinary action, we will tell you the concern in writing, hear your response at a meeting where you may bring [[a colleague or representative]], and give a written decision with a right of appeal.
3. We may suspend you on full pay while we investigate a serious concern. Suspension is not a finding of wrongdoing.
4. If you have a grievance about your work, raise it first with your manager. If that does not resolve it, or the grievance is about your manager, write to [[role, for example the chair of the board]]. We will meet you within [[ten working days]] and reply in writing.

## 23. Termination and notice

1. After probation, either of us may end your employment by giving written notice of:
  1. [[one month]] during your first [[two]] years of service; and then
  2. [[two months]], or the longer period required by applicable law.
2. We may dismiss you without notice for gross misconduct, after following a fair procedure. Examples of gross misconduct include sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment; violence; theft, fraud or bribery; serious breach of confidentiality or data protection; and working under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
3. We may end your employment for other fair reasons allowed by applicable law, such as redundancy or capability, following the procedure and paying any severance that the law requires.
4. Where the law allows, we may pay basic salary in place of notice.
5. On termination, we pay all amounts due, including earned untaken leave and any severance, within [[the period required by law / seven days]], and give you a certificate of service on request.

::: adapt Termination, notice and severance
Local law varies most here, so take advice before ending any employment. Examples: most US states allow at-will employment, though anti-discrimination protections still apply; South Africa requires a fair reason and procedure (Labour Relations Act 1995) and severance of at least one week's pay per completed year for operational dismissals; Kenya requires severance of at least 15 days' pay per completed year for redundancy (Employment Act 2007); the Philippines requires a just or authorised cause: for just causes, a two-notice procedure with a chance to be heard; for authorised causes such as redundancy, 30 days' written notice to the employee and the labour department, and separation pay; Brazil sets notice that grows with service. Check whether you must consult staff or notify a labour authority before redundancies.
:::

## 24. Garden leave (optional)

1. [[Delete this clause if you do not need it.]] During any notice period, we may ask you not to come to work, not to contact our partners, funders or the people we serve, and not to carry out some or all of your duties.
2. You remain our employee on full salary and benefits, your duties of confidentiality and loyalty continue, and you must remain available to help with handover. We will use this clause only for a good reason, such as protecting confidential information or the people we serve.

## 25. Return of property

1. When your employment ends, or earlier if we ask, you must return all our property, including laptops, phones, keys, ID cards, documents and files, in whatever form.
2. You must hand over passwords and access to our accounts, and must not keep copies of our confidential information or personal data. On request, you will confirm in writing that you have done so.

## 26. Governing law and disputes

1. This contract is governed by the law of [[country]]. Nothing in this contract removes any protection you have under the law of the place where you normally work.
2. If a dispute arises, we will first try to resolve it through the grievance procedure in clause 22. Either of us may then use [[the labour conciliation or mediation service, for example the national labour office]] before going to the [[labour court or tribunal of country]].

::: adapt Governing law and language
Choose the law of the country where the employee normally works. If you choose another, the employee usually keeps the mandatory protections of the place of work anyway (in the EU, under Article 8 of the Rome I Regulation 593/2008). Name the local conciliation body if one exists, for example the CCMA in South Africa. If the law requires a local-language contract, make that version the official one.
:::

## 27. Entire agreement and changes

1. This contract, including its schedules, is the entire agreement between us about your employment, and replaces any earlier offer letter or discussion.
2. We may change this contract only by written agreement signed by both of us, except for minor changes that this contract allows or applicable law permits. We will give you written notice of any change at least [[one month]] before it takes effect.
3. If any part of this contract is invalid, the rest remains in force. This contract is signed in [[number]] copies. [[It is written in language and in English. If they differ, the language version prevails.]]
