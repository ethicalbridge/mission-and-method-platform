::: adapt Setting your limits
Every amount in this matrix is illustrative. Set limits with your treasurer or accountant, in proportion to your income: a common approach is to let the director approve budgeted spending up to around [[2–5%]] of annual income, and keep anything larger, or anything unbudgeted, for the board. Use one currency. Check your governing document first: it may reserve some decisions, such as borrowing, buying property or changing the constitution, to the board or to members, and this matrix cannot override it. Also check each funder's agreement, which may require their approval for some changes.
:::

# How to use this matrix

- **Find the decision** in the tables below, then the amount. The person named in the **Approves** column, or anyone above them, may approve it.
- **Second approval** means a second person must also approve before the commitment is made. Where it says “Treasurer”, the chair can act if the treasurer is absent or conflicted.
- **Amounts include tax** and are the total value of the commitment, for example the full value of a 12-month contract, not the monthly cost.
- **Do not split** a purchase or contract into smaller parts to stay under a limit. That is a breach of this matrix.
- **Approval is in writing**: a signature, an email or an entry in the accounting system, kept with the records.
- **The budget comes first.** Spending must also be in an approved budget and follow our Financial Controls Policy and Procurement Policy.

Roles used: **Board** [[trustees / directors / committee]] acting as a body at a quorate meeting or by written resolution; **Chair**; **Treasurer**; **Director** [[executive director / CEO / coordinator]]; **Finance lead**; **Budget holder** (a manager named by the director for a budget).

::: adapt Employment decisions
Check local employment law before you fix who can dismiss staff. In some countries dismissal requires specific steps, notice or approval from a labour authority. If the director is the only manager, consider asking a board member to hear any appeal against a disciplinary decision.

# 5. Funding and partners

{widths: 30 18 20 32}
| Decision | Approves | Second approval | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submit a grant application up to [[USD 50,000]] | Director | Not applicable | Fits our strategy and budget |
| Submit a grant application above [[USD 50,000]], or for a new area of work | Director | Chair | Board informed |
| Accept and sign a grant up to [[USD 100,000]] | Director | Treasurer | Conditions checked against our policies |
| Accept a grant above [[USD 100,000]], or with unusual conditions | Board | Not applicable | Unusual conditions include co-funding, unlimited liability or rights over our name |
| Make a sub-grant to a partner up to [[USD 10,000]] | Director | Treasurer | After due diligence and a Sub-grant Agreement |
| Make a sub-grant above [[USD 10,000]] | Board | Not applicable | Due diligence report presented |
| Accept donations | As set in our Gift Acceptance Policy | As set in that policy | Donor checks first |

::: adapt Grants and sub-grants
Set grant limits in proportion to your size: a single grant worth a large share of your annual income carries real risk and deserves a board decision. Before signing, someone must read the whole agreement, including reporting, audit, repayment, intellectual property and termination clauses. Sub-grants pass both money and risk to partners; keep the limit for the director low until you have tested your Partner Due Diligence Questionnaire and Sub-grant Agreement.
:::

::: adapt Legal and public matters
Decide who can speak for you in a crisis before one happens. If you work on sensitive issues, such as human rights, elections or land, you may want the chair to approve any public statement on them. Check your local law on who can sign legal documents for your legal form: in some countries only registered directors or officers can bind the organisation, and their names must be filed with the registry.
:::

# Rules that always apply

### Absence

When an approver is away, the person named below acts for them. The deputy must be someone other than the person asking for the approval.

{widths: 50 50}
| Approver | Deputy |
|---|---|
| Director | [[Name, role]], up to [[USD 5,000]]; above that, the chair |
| Treasurer | Chair, or another board member named by the board |
| Chair | Vice-chair, or another board member named by the board |
| Budget holder | Director |

### Conflicts of interest

No one may approve a decision in which they, or a close associate, have an interest. The decision passes to the next level up, or to the chair. Our Conflict of Interest Policy applies.

### Emergencies

In a genuine emergency, such as a threat to someone's safety or the loss of our assets, the director may approve spending of up to [[USD 2,000]] outside these limits. They tell the chair within [[48 hours]] and the board at its next meeting.

### Further delegation

An approver may delegate their authority to another named person, in writing, for a set period and up to a set amount, but never beyond their own limit. The delegation is kept with the finance records.

::: adapt Rules for a very small team
If your team is two or three people, you may not have budget holders or a finance lead. Then combine the columns: for example, the director approves and the treasurer gives second approval above a lower limit. If the founder is the only staff member, make sure payments to the founder, and the founder's pay, are always approved by the treasurer or chair.
:::

# Guidance notes

- **Keep it short and current.** A matrix that nobody reads protects nobody. Share it with every approver and bank signatory, and keep it with your Financial Controls Policy.
- **Match your bank mandate.** The people who can approve payments in the bank should be the same people this matrix names, with the same limits where the bank allows.
- **Match your funders.** Some grant agreements require the funder's approval before you change the budget, key staff or partners. Note these in the table or in an annex.
- **Check it works.** Once a year, the treasurer checks a sample of approvals against this matrix and reports to the board.
- **Record exceptions.** Any decision taken outside the matrix, including in an emergency, is recorded and reported to the board.
