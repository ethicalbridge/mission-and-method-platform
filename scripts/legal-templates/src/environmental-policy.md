# Who this policy applies to

This policy applies to everyone who works with or for [[Organisation name]]: staff, volunteers, interns, board members, and contractors and consultants when they work for us. It covers our offices, travel, purchases, events, digital tools and, most importantly, our programmes and the work of partners we fund.

**Law.** We follow the environmental law of [[country]] and of the other countries where we work, including rules on waste, pollution, protected areas and environmental assessment. Where our partners or funders set stricter rules for a project, we follow those.

::: adapt Environmental law
Most small organisations are not directly regulated, but some activities are, such as construction, water points, agriculture, waste handling and work in protected areas. Examples to check: in Kenya, the Environmental Management and Co-ordination Act 1999 requires an environmental impact assessment licence from the National Environment Management Authority for listed projects; in South Africa, the National Environmental Management Act 1998 and its EIA regulations; in India, the EIA Notification 2006. Many countries also ban single-use plastic bags, as Kenya and Rwanda have done. If your programmes include any physical works, check your local law on environmental assessment before you start.
:::

# Our commitments

## 1. Measure what matters

Once a year, the environment lead collects simple figures for the previous [[12 months]]:

- **Travel:** distance flown (by short, medium and long flights), distance by car, motorbike and public transport, and nights in hotels.
- **Energy:** electricity used (from bills or meter readings), generator fuel, and gas or other fuel.
- **Purchases:** the main items we buy, such as paper, printing, IT equipment, vehicles and event supplies.
- **Waste and water:** a rough estimate, where we can measure it.

We turn the main figures into an estimate of greenhouse gas emissions using a free calculator or published conversion factors. We use the estimate to see where our biggest impacts are and whether they are falling, not as an exact number.

::: adapt How to measure
Keep it simple: a spreadsheet with a few rows is enough. The Greenhouse Gas Protocol is the common standard. It groups emissions into "scopes": scope 1 is fuel you burn (vehicles, generators), scope 2 is electricity you buy, and scope 3 is everything else, including flights and purchases. Free published conversion factors, such as those the UK government publishes each year, or tools from climate charities and networks, can turn distances and kilowatt-hours into emissions. If you share an office or work from home, estimate your share or leave energy out and say so.
:::

## 2. Travel

Travel is often a small organisation's largest source of emissions. We:

- ask first whether a trip is needed, and meet online where that works as well;
- combine trips, and stay longer and travel less often where it makes sense;
- take the train or bus for journeys under [[6 hours]] where it is safe, reasonably priced and practical;
- fly economy class, and choose direct flights where the price difference is reasonable;
- share vehicles, keep them well maintained and avoid idling engines;
- never ask anyone to take a less safe option to reduce emissions. Safety comes first, under our Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy.

We record travel in a way that makes the annual figures easy to collect, through our Expenses and Per Diem Policy.

::: adapt Travel rules
Set rules that fit your context. Where rail is limited or roads are unsafe, flying may be the only safe option; say so. You might set a target, such as cutting flights by [[20%]] in two years, or a rule that any long-haul flight needs approval from [[role]]. For field work, consider whether local staff or partners can do the work instead of visits from far away. This also strengthens local leadership.
:::

## 3. Energy and offices

- We switch off lights, equipment and air conditioning when not in use, and set air conditioning no lower than [[24°C]].
- We choose efficient equipment and LED lighting when we replace items.
- Where we can choose, we buy electricity from renewable sources or install solar power, especially where it also replaces a diesel generator.
- When we look for a new office, we consider energy use, access by public transport and accessibility.

## 4. Procurement

We follow our Procurement Policy, which includes environmental criteria. When we buy goods or services, we:

- buy only what we need, and repair, share or buy second-hand before buying new;
- prefer durable, repairable, energy-efficient and locally made products where quality and price are reasonable;
- avoid single-use plastic and disposable items, such as plastic water bottles at events;
- ask main suppliers how they reduce their environmental impact, and give it weight when we compare quotes for contracts above [[amount and currency]];
- buy recycled paper and print only when needed, double-sided.

## 5. Waste, water and resources

- We reduce, reuse and recycle, in that order. We provide separate bins where local recycling exists.
- We dispose of electronic waste, batteries and hazardous materials through approved collectors, and wipe data from devices before disposal under our IT and Information Security Policy.
- We give old equipment a second life, for example by donating working laptops to partners after wiping them.
- We use water carefully, especially in areas where water is scarce.

## 6. Digital

Digital tools save travel but also use energy. We:

- keep devices for as long as they work well, and buy refurbished equipment where suitable;
- delete files and emails we no longer need, in line with our Records Retention Schedule;
- turn cameras off in large online meetings where people have poor connections, which also helps inclusion;
- choose online service providers that publish their environmental commitments, where the choice is otherwise equal.

## 7. Events

When we organise events, workshops or training, we:

- choose venues close to participants or reachable by public transport, and offer online participation where possible;
- serve local, seasonal food, with vegetarian options as standard;
- avoid single-use plastic, printed packs and giveaways nobody needs;
- share materials digitally, while keeping printed copies for participants without devices or connections.

## 8. Climate and environmental risk in our programmes

Our programmes can affect the environment, and the environment affects our programmes. We apply the principle of **do no harm** to both.

**Before a new programme or major activity starts**, the programme lead answers the screening questions in Annex A with the community and partners. If the answers show a significant risk, the lead agrees changes with the [[Executive Director / founder]] and, for high risks, takes specialist advice and records the risk in the Risk Register.

We pay particular attention to:

- activities that involve construction, water, land, agriculture, energy, waste or the use of chemicals;
- work in or near protected areas, forests, wetlands, coasts and other fragile places;
- the use of natural resources such as firewood, water and timber, by us or by participants as a result of our work;
- whether climate hazards, such as floods, drought, heat or storms, could affect people's safety, our activities or what we build.

**Supporting adaptation.** Where it fits our mission, we help communities prepare for climate change, using their own knowledge of their environment alongside scientific information. We ask partners we fund to follow the same principles.

::: adapt Programme screening
Keep the screening proportionate: a training programme needs only the first few questions; a project that builds water points or supports farming needs all of them, and may need a formal environmental assessment under local law. Humanitarian and development organisations can sign the Climate and Environment Charter for Humanitarian Organizations, launched in 2021, and free tools such as NEAT+ help screen environmental risks in humanitarian settings. Some funders have their own environmental and social safeguards; follow them when you use their funds.
:::

## 9. Offsetting

Our priority is to **reduce** our emissions. **Carbon offsetting** means paying for a project elsewhere that is meant to reduce or remove emissions, such as tree planting or clean cookstoves, to balance out your own. Offsets have real limits: their quality varies, some projects would have happened anyway, and the benefits can be overstated or short-lived. Our position is:

- [[We do not currently buy offsets. We put our time and money into reducing our own emissions and into our programmes.]]
- [[Or: each year we set aside an amount, for example a share of our travel costs, as a "climate contribution" for verified projects, using credits certified under recognised standards such as Gold Standard or Verra, or for environmental work in the communities we serve.]]
- We do not describe ourselves as "carbon neutral" or "net zero" on the basis of offsets.

::: adapt Choosing your offsetting position
Choose one of the two options above and delete the other. Both are honest positions for a small organisation. If you buy credits, check the project carefully, prefer projects that also benefit local communities, and keep a record of the certificates. Be aware that rules on environmental claims in advertising are getting stricter in several places, including the European Union and the UK, and claims such as "carbon neutral" can be challenged.
:::

## 10. Honest communication

We describe our environmental efforts accurately and modestly. We do not exaggerate, and we report what we have not yet achieved as well as what we have. We follow our Social Media and Communications Policy.

# Records

The environment lead keeps the annual figures, the action plan and the programme screening records for at least [[5 years]], so that we can show progress over time.

# If this policy is not followed

Most problems are best solved by a conversation and a better way of working. Repeated or deliberate disregard of this policy, or any action that causes serious environmental harm or breaks environmental law, will be dealt with under our Code of Conduct and Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure, or the terms of a contract or partnership agreement.

# Monitoring and review

The board reviews progress on the action plan once a year. We review this policy every [[two years]], or sooner if our activities, the law or our funders' requirements change.
