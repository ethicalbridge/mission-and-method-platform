# The information we collect and where it comes from

What we collect depends on how you are connected to us. Read the section that applies to you.

## 1. If you take part in our programmes or use our services

We collect:

- your name, age or age range, gender, phone number and [[village, town or area]];
- information about your household, such as [[number of people, children in school]], where our programme needs it;
- your attendance and progress in our activities, and your feedback;
- [[health, disability or other sensitive information, only where the service needs it, such as at our clinic or for accessibility]];
- photos, recordings and stories, only if you agree separately.

Most of this comes from you, when you register or take part. Sometimes it comes from [[a partner organisation, a community leader or a referral from a local service]] who has told you they are sharing it with us.

## 2. If you donate or support us

We collect your name, contact details, donation history, payment reference (not your full card details, which our payment provider handles), tax information where you ask for a receipt, the messages you have agreed to receive and your communication preferences. This comes from you, or from [[the fundraising platform or payment provider]] you used.

## 3. If you work or volunteer with us, or apply to

We collect your application, CV and references; contact details and emergency contact; ID and right-to-work documents; bank and tax details for payment; contract, performance and training records; sick leave and health information needed to support you at work; and, for some roles, the outcome of a criminal record check. This comes from you, your referees, and [[the agency or official body that carries out checks]].

## 4. If you visit our website or contact us online

We collect what you send us through forms or email, and technical information such as your device, browser, approximate location and the pages you visit. Some of this is collected with cookies. Our Cookie Policy explains which ones we use and how to change your choices.

# Why we use your information and our lawful reasons

Data protection laws only allow us to use your information when we have a good reason, called a lawful basis. This table shows our reasons.

{widths: 40 22 38}
| What we use it for | Whose information | Our lawful reason |
|---|---|---|
| To deliver our programmes and services, and keep in touch about them | People we serve | [[Our agreement with you / legitimate interests / consent]] |
| To keep you safe and respond to safeguarding concerns | People we serve, staff, volunteers | Legitimate interests; vital interests in an emergency; legal obligation where the law requires us to report |
| To deliver health or other services that need sensitive information | People we serve | Your explicit consent, or [[another condition our law allows]] |
| To report to our funders, using totals and anonymous stories unless you agree otherwise | People we serve | Legitimate interests |
| To use your photo or story in our communications | People we serve, supporters | Your consent |
| To process donations and send receipts | Donors | Our agreement with you; legal obligation for tax records |
| To send newsletters and appeals | Supporters | Your consent, or [[legitimate interests for existing supporters, where our law allows]] |
| To recruit, employ, pay and support staff and volunteers | Staff, volunteers, applicants | Our agreement with you; legal obligation for tax, payroll and employment law |
| To run, secure and improve our website | Website visitors | Legitimate interests; consent for non-essential cookies |
| To keep financial and legal records and respond to authorities | Everyone, as needed | Legal obligation |

Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time by contacting us. This does not affect what we did before. Saying no, or changing your mind, will never affect the support or services you receive from us.

::: adapt Lawful reasons in your country
Copy the lawful reasons from your data inventory. The names in this table follow the GDPR. Other laws use different lists: for example, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 relies mainly on consent and a short list of "legitimate uses", and Brazil's LGPD has ten legal bases. Use the names in your own law. Never say you rely on consent where people cannot really refuse, for example when information is a condition of receiving aid: choose another lawful reason and explain it.

# Who we share your information with

We never sell your information. We share it only when we need to, and only the minimum:

- **Service providers** that help us run our work, such as [[email and cloud storage, our database, our payment provider, our accountant]]. They may use your information only on our instructions, under a written agreement.
- **Partner organisations** we work with to deliver a service to you, such as [[name or type of partner]], under a Data Sharing Agreement. If we want to refer you to another organisation, we ask you first, unless you or someone else is in danger.
- **Funders**, usually as totals or anonymous stories. If a funder needs to check names, for example during an audit, we share only what is needed, securely.
- **Authorities** such as police, courts, tax or labour authorities, only where the law requires it or to protect someone from serious harm.
- **Professional advisers**, such as lawyers, auditors or insurers, where needed, under a duty of confidentiality.

# Sending information to other countries

Messaging apps can also store messages abroad. When information leaves [[country]], we make sure it is protected in the way the law requires, for example through the provider's contract terms.

# How we keep your information safe

We protect your information with secure systems, strong passwords, two-step sign-in, encrypted devices and locked storage for paper files. Only people who need your information for their work can see it, and everyone in our team agrees to keep it confidential. If something goes wrong and your information is put at risk, we will tell you where the law requires it, or where it could affect you, and explain what we are doing about it.

# Your rights and how to use them

You have rights over your information. Depending on the law that applies to you, you can ask us to:

- **show you** the information we hold about you and give you a copy;
- **correct** information that is wrong or incomplete;
- **delete** your information, when we no longer need it or you withdraw consent;
- **stop or limit** how we use it, including for fundraising and newsletters, which we always stop when asked;
- **give you** your information in a format you can take elsewhere;
- **have a person review** any decision made about you by a computer alone. We do not make such decisions today.

To use any of these rights, tell any member of our team, in person, by phone or message, or write to [[privacy@yourorganisation.org]]. You do not need to use any special words or form. It is free. We may ask you to confirm who you are. We will reply within [[one month]]. If your request is complex, we may need longer, and we will tell you why within the first month.

Sometimes we cannot do everything you ask, for example when the law requires us to keep tax records. If so, we will explain why.

::: adapt Response times and rights by country
Check which rights and deadlines apply. The GDPR and UK GDPR give one month, which can be extended by two months for complex requests. Some laws set shorter periods: for example, Brazil's LGPD provides for a full access response within 15 days. Check your own law, and the Data Rights Request Procedure, and use the shortest deadline that applies to the people you serve. If your law gives extra rights, such as the right in India to nominate someone to act for you after death or incapacity, add them.
:::
