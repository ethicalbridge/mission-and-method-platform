# Purpose

This schedule tells everyone at [[Organisation name]] how long to keep each type of record and what to do with it at the end. It applies to records in any form: paper, email, shared drives, databases, messaging apps, photos and records held for us by service providers.

We keep records for as long as we need them for our work, to meet legal and funder requirements, and to defend legal claims. Then we delete or destroy them securely, or archive them where they have lasting value. Personal information must not be kept longer than necessary. This is a core principle of data protection law, including the GDPR.

# How to use this schedule

- **Owner:** the role responsible for keeping the record and for deleting it on time.
- **Retention period:** how long we keep the record after the trigger.
- **Trigger:** the event that starts the clock, for example the end of the financial year.
- **Action:** what happens at the end. Delete means secure deletion or destruction. Archive means keeping a permanent copy in our secure archive. Review means the owner decides whether to keep it for a stated reason or delete it.

All periods marked "typical" are sensible defaults used by many organisations. They are **typical; check local law and funder conditions.** Where a law or agreement requires a longer period, the longer period applies. Where a record falls into two categories, keep it for the longer period.

::: adapt Setting your periods
Go through each row with your accountant, and check your three or four biggest grant agreements. Change the periods to match your law and your funders, and delete rows you do not need. Add records specific to your work, for example clinical records, school records or microfinance loan files, which often have their own legal rules. Record the reason for any period that is unusually long or short.
:::

::: adapt Accounting and tax periods by country
These periods vary widely and are set by company, tax and charity law. Some examples to check: India's Companies Act 2013 requires books of account for eight years; Germany's commercial and tax rules require up to ten years for accounting records; South Africa's Companies Act 2008 requires seven years for company records; in the UK, charities generally keep accounting records for six years; Kenya's Tax Procedures Act 2015 generally requires five years. Tax periods can be extended where a tax audit or dispute is open. Check the law in your country with your accountant and set one period that covers all of them.
:::

Grant agreements often set retention periods that are longer than the law. # Disposing of records securely

When a record reaches the end of its period, the owner deletes or destroys it within [[3 months]] and notes it in the disposal log.

- **Paper:** shred with a cross-cut shredder, or use a disposal company that gives a certificate of destruction. Never put personal records in ordinary waste or reuse them as scrap paper.
- **Electronic files:** delete from shared drives, email, databases and the recycle bin. Remember copies on laptops, phones, USB drives and messaging apps.
- **Devices:** before reuse, sale or disposal, wipe phones, laptops and drives using a factory reset with encryption, or physically destroy the storage.
- **Service providers:** ask providers to delete our data at the end of a contract and confirm in writing.
- **Backups:** data in backups is deleted when the backup is overwritten. Until then, it must not be restored or used.

# Legal holds

A legal hold stops the deletion of records. The [[Director]] places a hold when a legal claim, investigation, audit, regulator inquiry or rights request is open or reasonably expected. They tell the record owners in writing what is held and why. Nobody may delete records under a hold, even if their period has ended. The hold is lifted in writing when the matter closes, and normal deletion resumes.

# Review

The [[data protection lead]] reviews this schedule every [[year]], and when a law changes, a new type of record is created, or a major grant has new requirements. At each review they check with owners that deletion has actually happened, and report the result to the board.

# Guidance notes

- **Start with the records you actually have.** Walk through your shared drive and filing cabinets and check each type against this schedule.
- **Do not keep everything "just in case".** Old personal data is a liability, not an asset.
- **Make deletion routine.** Set calendar reminders, for example after each financial year end.
- **Ask before deleting** anything you are unsure about. It is easier to delete later than to recover a lost record.
- **Tell people.** Our Privacy Notice explains, in summary, how long we keep information.
