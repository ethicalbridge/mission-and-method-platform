::: adapt Before you use this agreement
**Use it** when [[Organisation name]] gives funds to a partner to deliver a project the partner leads or co-designs, whether the money comes from a head donor (an institutional or foundation funder) or from your own funds.

**Do not use it** to buy services or goods from a partner (use the Services Agreement), or for collaboration without funds (use the Partnership Memorandum of Understanding).

**Before signing:** complete due diligence on the partner, proportionate to the amount and risk, using the Partner Due Diligence Questionnaire. Agree the budget and reporting dates with the partner, not for it.

**Donor conditions often prevail.** Most head grant agreements require you to pass specific clauses on to sub-grantees, sometimes word for word. Copy them faithfully into Schedule 3. Do not summarise or soften them, and do not add stricter terms that the donor does not require and the partner has no budget to meet.

**Local law:** several countries regulate foreign funding of non-profits. For example, India's Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act 2010, as amended in 2020, requires registration or prior permission to receive foreign contributions and, since 2020, forbids transferring them to other organisations. Check the law in the partner's country before you commit.
:::

# Background

1. The Grantor has received funding from [[Head Donor name]] under [[grant agreement reference and title]], dated [[date]] (the **Head Grant**) [[or: is funding this project from its own resources]].
2. The Partner [[e.g. is a community-based organisation working with young people in X district]] and has designed, with the Grantor, the project described in Schedule 1.
3. The Grantor wishes to fund the Partner to deliver that project on the terms below.

# Agreed terms

## 1. Definitions

1. In this agreement:
  1. **Approved Budget** means the budget in Schedule 1, as changed under clause 8.
  2. **Business Day** means a day other than a weekend or public holiday in [[the Partner's country]].
  3. **Eligible Costs** means costs that meet clause 5.
  4. **Grant** means the funds the Grantor provides under this agreement, up to [[amount and currency]].
  5. **Head Donor** means [[Head Donor name]], and **Head Grant** has the meaning in the Background.
  6. **Project** means the activities and results described in Schedule 1.
  7. **Project Period** means [[start date]] to [[end date]], as extended under clause 8.
2. "Clause 5.2" means paragraph 2 of clause 5. The schedules form part of this agreement. "In writing" includes email from the named contacts.

## 2. The Grant and the Project

1. The Grantor will provide the Grant to the Partner to deliver the Project during the Project Period, as described in Schedule 1.
2. The Partner will use the Grant only for the Project and in line with the Approved Budget and this agreement.
3. The Grant is a contribution towards the Project, not payment for services. The Partner keeps responsibility for how it delivers the Project, and is not the Grantor's agent.
4. The Partner will tell the Grantor of other funding it receives for the same activities, so that no cost is funded twice.

## 3. Head Donor conditions

1. Schedule 3 sets out the conditions of the Head Grant that apply to the Partner. The Partner will comply with them as if they were written into this agreement.
2. If Schedule 3 conflicts with the rest of this agreement, [[the stricter provision / Schedule 3]] prevails.
3. The Grantor will give the Partner the relevant parts of the Head Grant, any donor guidance referred to in them, and any changes to them within [[10]] Business Days of receiving them.
4. If the Head Donor suspends, reduces or ends the Head Grant, the Grantor may suspend, reduce or end the Grant to the same extent by written notice. The Grantor will explain the reason, share the Head Donor's notice where it is allowed to, and pay Eligible Costs the Partner has properly committed, as far as the Head Donor allows.

::: adapt Copying the donor's conditions
Read the Head Grant line by line and list every clause that says it must be passed on to sub-grantees or partners ("flow-down" clauses). Typical ones cover eligible costs, procurement rules, audit and access rights, record retention, anti-terrorism and sanctions, safeguarding, visibility, prohibited purchases, and reporting of irregularities. Copy them faithfully, with the donor's numbering, into Schedule 3. Where the donor uses its own terms (for example "recipient" or "implementing partner"), add a line explaining that they mean the Partner.

Translate Schedule 3 into the Partner's working language if needed, and go through it together at the start. If the Head Grant is in English and a translation is provided, state which version prevails. If you fund the Project from your own resources, delete clause 3 and Schedule 3.
:::

## 4. Payments

1. The Grantor will pay the Grant in tranches, as set out in Schedule 2, into a bank account in the Partner's legal name.
2. The first tranche is paid within [[10]] Business Days of the later of signature of this agreement and the Grantor's receipt of the Partner's bank details on letterhead.
3. Each later tranche is paid within [[10]] Business Days after the Grantor approves the reports due before it, and once the Partner has spent or committed at least [[70]]% of the funds it has already received.
4. The Grantor will review each report within [[10]] Business Days, and either approve it or explain in writing what is needed. The Grantor will not delay a tranche for minor issues that can be corrected in the next report.
5. The Grant is paid in [[currency]]. The Partner records the exchange rate it actually receives on each transfer and uses it in its financial reports. [[Exchange rate gains are used for the Project with the Grantor's approval; losses are shared as agreed in writing.]]
6. Any interest earned on the Grant is used for the Project or returned to the Grantor, as the Head Donor requires.
7. The Grantor will confirm by telephone, with the Partner's finance contact on the number in this agreement, any request to change bank details before making a payment.

::: adapt Payments that do not starve the Project
Small partners rarely have reserves to pre-finance activities. Make the first tranche large enough to cover the first period plus a buffer (often one quarter's costs), and set the spending trigger in clause 4.3 at a realistic level, so that the Project does not stop while a report is reviewed. Check that your own payment schedule from the Head Donor allows you to pay on time.

Agree how currency movements are handled. In countries with volatile currencies, a sharp fall can wipe out a budget: consider paying in hard currency, paying smaller tranches more often, or a contingency line.
:::

## 5. Eligible costs

1. A cost is eligible only if it:
  1. is necessary for the Project and included in the Approved Budget;
  2. is incurred during the Project Period, or paid within [[30]] days after it for activities carried out during it;
  3. is reasonable, and follows the Partner's own policies and the procurement rules in clause 7;
  4. is recorded in the Partner's accounts and supported by evidence such as invoices, receipts, contracts, payroll records and attendance lists; and
  5. is not funded by anyone else.
2. The following are not eligible: costs incurred outside the Project Period; debts, fines and penalties; costs of [[alcohol, entertainment and gifts]]; political or religious campaigning; items prohibited by the Head Donor; and any amount lost through fraud or theft, except as clause 15.4 allows.
3. **Indirect costs.** The Approved Budget includes a contribution to the Partner's running costs of [[X]]% of direct costs. The Partner [[does not need to show receipts for this contribution / supports it as the Head Donor requires]].

## 6. Financial management

1. The Partner will track the Grant separately from its other funds, using [[a separate bank account / a separate project code in its accounting system]], so that every income and cost item for the Project can be identified.
2. The Partner will keep complete, accurate books and the original supporting documents for every cost.
3. The Partner will, at minimum:
  1. require two authorised people to approve each payment, and keep the roles of ordering, approving and paying separate where its size allows;
  2. reconcile its Project bank account each month;
  3. pay by bank transfer or mobile money where possible, and keep cash payments below [[amount]] each, with a signed receipt;
  4. keep a register of equipment bought with the Grant worth more than [[amount]]; and
  5. tell the Grantor within [[5]] Business Days of any change of its legal status, senior leadership, bank signatories or auditors.

::: adapt Proportionate financial controls
Match the controls to the partner's size and the amount of the Grant. A partner with two staff can still have two people approve payments, for example a staff member and a board treasurer, even if one person does the bookkeeping. Where mobile money is the norm, accept mobile money statements as evidence. If the partner has gaps, agree a short improvement plan under clause 14 rather than refusing to fund it. Check the Head Donor's rules, which may set specific cash limits or require a separate bank account.
:::

## 7. Procurement

1. The Partner will buy goods and services for the Project fairly, transparently and for best value. It will follow its own procurement policy where that is at least as strict as this clause, and otherwise the minimum rules in the table below.
2. The Partner will not split purchases to avoid a threshold. Anyone with a conflict of interest in a purchase will declare it and take no part in the decision.
3. The Partner will check that suppliers above [[amount]] are not on the sanctions lists in clause 11.4, and keep a record of each purchase decision.
4. Equipment bought with the Grant belongs to the Partner [[unless the Head Donor requires otherwise]], is used for the Project, and is not sold during the Project Period without the Grantor's written approval.

{widths: 35 65}
| Value of the purchase | Minimum process |
|---|---|
| Up to [[amount]] | One quote or a price check, recorded |
| [[amount]] to [[amount]] | At least three written quotes, compared in writing |
| Above [[amount]] | Open or competitive tender, with the Grantor's prior written approval of the selection |

::: adapt Procurement thresholds
Set thresholds that fit local prices and the partner's size, and check the Head Donor's rules first: many donors set their own thresholds, and some add rules on where goods may come from. In remote areas, three quotes may not exist. Allow a recorded explanation instead, approved by a second person. The Procurement Policy template shows a fuller approach.
:::

## 8. Changes and budget flexibility

1. The Partner may move up to [[10]]% of any budget line to another existing line without prior approval, provided the total Grant does not change. It will explain the change in its next financial report.
2. The Partner needs the Grantor's prior written approval to move more than this, to add a new budget line, to change [[staff salary or equipment]] lines, or to make any significant change to the Project's activities, locations or expected results.
3. The Grantor will respond to a change request within [[10]] Business Days. It will not unreasonably refuse a change that helps the Project respond to changed conditions.
4. A request to extend the Project Period, with or without extra funds, must be made at least [[30]] days before it ends.

::: adapt Budget flexibility
Ten per cent between lines is a common limit, but your Head Donor's rule decides: limits vary, and some donors require approval for any change. Use the Head Donor's limit, or a stricter one only if you have a good reason. Flexibility helps partners respond to price rises and changes in context, which are common in long projects.
:::

## 9. Reporting

1. The Partner will send the reports set out in Schedule 2, by the dates set there, using the templates the Grantor provides [[or the Partner's own format, if it contains the same information]].
2. Narrative reports describe activities, progress against the results in Schedule 1, challenges, changes, risks, learning and feedback from the communities served.
3. Financial reports show income and spending by budget line, compared with the Approved Budget, with an explanation of any difference of more than [[10]]%, and the exchange rates used.
4. The Partner will tell the Grantor within [[5]] Business Days of any event that significantly affects the Project, such as a delay, a security incident or a serious complaint.

## 10. Monitoring, audit and records

1. The Grantor may monitor the Project through meetings, document reviews and visits. It will give at least [[5]] Business Days' notice of a visit, except where it has a safeguarding or fraud concern.
2. The Partner will give the Grantor, the Head Donor and their auditors or investigators access to its Project records, staff, sites and supporting documents, and allow them to take copies.
3. [[If the Grant exceeds amount, the Partner will provide an independent audit or expenditure verification of the Grant within 90 days after the Project Period. Its cost is included in the Approved Budget.]]
4. The Partner will keep all records relating to the Grant for [[7]] years after the end of the Project Period, or longer if the Head Donor or the law requires.

## 11. Compliance

1. **Safeguarding.** The Partner will have and apply a safeguarding policy that covers protection from sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (PSEAH), follows the six core principles of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC), and includes a safe way for communities to raise concerns. It will report to the Grantor's safeguarding contact, [[name and email]], within [[24 hours]] any concern involving the Project, its staff or volunteers, sharing only the information needed and protecting the identity of the people involved.
2. **Fraud and corruption.** The Partner will not tolerate fraud, bribery or corruption. It will report to the Grantor within [[48 hours]] any suspected fraud, theft, bribery or other misuse of the Grant, take immediate steps to protect funds and evidence, and cooperate with any investigation. The Grantor will report to the Head Donor as the Head Grant requires.
3. **Code of conduct.** The Partner's staff and volunteers working on the Project will follow a code of conduct at least as protective as [[the Grantor's Code of Conduct]].
4. **Sanctions and counter-terrorism financing.** The Partner will not make funds or support available, directly or indirectly, to any person or entity on the UN Security Council Consolidated List or [[the US OFAC, EU, UK or other lists required by the Head Donor]]. It will screen its board members, senior staff, suppliers above [[amount]] and any sub-recipients against those lists before contracting, keep a record of each check, and tell the Grantor at once of any match.
5. **Data protection.** The Partner will collect only the personal data the Project needs, keep it secure and comply with the data protection laws that apply, including [[e.g. the Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019]]. It will share personal data about the people it serves with the Grantor only where needed and under a Data Sharing Agreement. Reports will use anonymised data unless agreed otherwise.
6. **Conflicts of interest and labour.** The Partner will declare any conflict of interest connected with the Grant, and will not use forced or child labour.
7. **Sub-recipients.** The Partner will not pass any of the Grant to another organisation without the Grantor's prior written approval. If approved, it will pass on the duties in clauses 5 to 11 and Schedule 3 in writing.

::: adapt Sanctions and counter-terrorism screening
Screening should be risk-based, as FATF Recommendation 8 on non-profit organisations encourages. Screen the people and organisations who receive funds or decide how they are used. Do not screen the people you serve unless the Head Grant expressly requires it and you have taken advice: it can harm trust and humanitarian principles. Since 2022, UN Security Council Resolution 2664 has created a humanitarian exemption to UN asset freezes for humanitarian assistance, and some national and regional sanctions regimes have followed. Check which lists and exemptions your Head Donor and your country apply, and record your checks as the Partner Due Diligence and Sanctions Screening Policy explains.
:::

## 12. Visibility

1. The Partner will acknowledge the support of the Grantor [[and the Head Donor]] in Project materials, events and publications, as set out in Schedule 3.
2. The Partner may decide not to display logos where visibility could put staff, partners or communities at risk. It will tell the Grantor of this decision.
3. Neither party will use the other's name or logo except as this clause allows or with written approval.

## 13. Intellectual property

1. The Partner owns the materials it creates for the Project. It grants the Grantor [[and the Head Donor]] a free, non-exclusive, permanent licence to use, copy, adapt and share them for non-commercial purposes, crediting the Partner.
2. Images, stories and data about people may be used only within the consent they gave.

## 14. Capacity support

1. The Grantor will provide the support described in Schedule 1, such as [[training, mentoring on financial management, or support to develop a safeguarding policy]], at no cost to the Partner.
2. Where due diligence or monitoring identifies a gap, the parties will agree a realistic improvement plan with dates and resources. The Grantor will not suspend or end the Grant for a gap the Partner is actively addressing under that plan, unless people or funds are at serious risk.

## 15. Unspent funds and refunds

1. Within [[30]] days of the Grantor's approval of the final financial report, the Partner will return any part of the Grant that it has not spent on Eligible Costs, unless the Grantor agrees in writing that it may be used for an agreed purpose.
2. The Partner will refund any amount the Grantor shows is not an Eligible Cost, within [[30]] days of a written request with reasons. The Partner may first respond and provide evidence within [[15]] Business Days.
3. The Grantor may deduct amounts due under this clause from later tranches, after telling the Partner in writing.
4. The Grantor will not ask the Partner to refund a cost that it approved in writing in advance and that the Partner incurred as approved, even if the Head Donor later disallows it, unless the Partner gave false or incomplete information. Where a loss is caused by theft or fraud despite the Partner following this agreement, the parties will agree in good faith how to share it, considering what the Head Donor allows.

::: adapt Fair refund rules
Refunds are where risk is most often shifted unfairly to small partners. Clause 15.4 keeps the risk of the Head Donor changing its mind with the Grantor, which chose the donor and approved the cost. Check that your Head Grant permits this, and that you can cover such a loss from unrestricted funds. Losses from fraud by a partner's staff are a common donor concern: agree in advance how they will be handled, and record the steps taken to prevent them.
:::

## 16. Suspension and termination

1. The Grantor may suspend payments, or ask the Partner to suspend some activities, by written notice with reasons, if:
  1. it has a reasonable suspicion of fraud, corruption or a serious safeguarding failure;
  2. a required report is more than [[30]] days late, after a written reminder; or
  3. the Head Donor suspends the Head Grant.
2. Either party may end this agreement by written notice if the other commits a material breach and does not remedy it within [[30]] days of written notice describing the breach. The Grantor may end it at once if fraud, corruption or serious misconduct is confirmed, or under clause 3.4.
3. The Partner may end this agreement by giving [[60]] days' written notice.
4. Either party may end this agreement if events beyond either party's control, such as conflict or natural disaster, prevent the Project for more than [[60]] days.
5. When this agreement ends, the Partner will stop making new commitments, send final reports within [[45]] days and return unspent funds under clause 15. The Grantor will pay Eligible Costs properly incurred or committed before the notice, including the reasonable costs of closing the Project, such as staff notice periods required by law, as far as the Head Donor allows.
6. Both parties will agree an exit plan to tell communities what will happen and, where possible, hand over or responsibly close activities.
7. Clauses 10, 11, 13, 15, 16.5, 16.6 and 17 continue after this agreement ends.

::: adapt Ending fairly
Suspension is a protective step, not a finding of wrongdoing: say so in any notice, and keep it as short as possible. Closing costs such as staff severance may be required by local employment law: check what the Head Donor allows and budget for it. Some countries require specific procedures to end staff contracts. Check the Partner's local law.
:::

## 17. Liability, disputes and general terms

1. The Partner is responsible for its staff, volunteers, activities and compliance with the law. Neither party is liable to the other for indirect or consequential loss.
2. The parties will try to settle any dispute through their grant and project managers within [[10]] Business Days, and then between [[the heads of the two organisations]] within a further [[15]] Business Days. If that fails, they will consider mediation through [[mediator or centre]] before going to [[the courts of country / arbitration under rules of institution]].
3. This agreement is governed by the law of [[country]].
4. Notices must be in writing and sent by email to the contacts in the Parties table. Notices of suspension or termination must also be sent by [[courier / hand]].
5. This agreement is the whole agreement about the Grant. Changes are valid only in writing signed by both parties, except budget changes under clause 8.1. Neither party may transfer it without the other's consent. If any part is invalid, the rest continues.
6. This agreement may be signed in counterparts and electronically. If it is translated, the [[English]] version prevails.
