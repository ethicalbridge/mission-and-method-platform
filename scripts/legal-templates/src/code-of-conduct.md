# Who this code applies to

This code applies to everyone who works with or for [[Organisation name]]:

- **Staff:** everyone we employ, full-time, part-time or temporary, including during probation.
- **Volunteers and interns:** from the day they start, whether they help once or every week.
- **Board members** [[trustees / directors / committee members]] and advisers.
- **Contractors and consultants:** anyone we pay to deliver work for us. Their contract requires them to follow this code.
- **Visitors and partners' staff** while they take part in our activities, as far as we can reasonably expect.

It applies at work, at our activities and events, when travelling for us, online and on social media, and outside working hours whenever your conduct could affect the people we serve, our colleagues or our reputation. It sits alongside your contract or agreement and does not replace it. If a partner or funder's code is stricter, the stricter rule applies while you work on their project.

**Law.** [[Organisation name]] is registered in [[country]] and follows its laws. When we work in other countries, we also follow the law that applies there. Where rules differ, we follow the one that gives people more protection.

# Our commitments

## 1. Respect and dignity for everyone

- Treat everyone with dignity, courtesy and respect: colleagues, volunteers, the people we serve, partners, funders and the public.
- Never harass, bully, intimidate, humiliate or abuse anyone, in person, in writing or online. Our Anti-Harassment, Intimidation and Bullying Policy and Procedure explains what this means and how to raise a concern.
- Never discriminate on grounds including age, disability, sex, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, marital or family status, pregnancy, race, colour, ethnicity, caste, nationality, national or social origin, language, religion or belief, political opinion, health or HIV status, or socio-economic status.
- Make reasonable adjustments so that everyone can take part fully.
- Respect the culture, customs and choices of the communities we work with, unless they cause harm.

## 2. Put the people we serve first

- The people and communities we work with define their own needs and priorities. We listen, involve them in decisions that affect them, and do not take over.
- Never use your position to obtain any advantage for yourself, your family or friends.
- Never promote religious, political or commercial views through our work, or make our support conditional on them.
- Do not create dependency: share skills and information so that people and partners can carry on without us.

## 3. Safeguarding first

### Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

[[Organisation name]] follows the six core principles of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC):

1. Sexual exploitation and abuse are gross misconduct and grounds for dismissal or ending any agreement with us.
2. No sexual activity with anyone under 18, whatever the local age of consent. Mistaken belief about someone's age is no defence.
3. Never exchange money, employment, goods, services or assistance for sex or sexual favours, or for any other humiliating, degrading or exploitative behaviour. This includes assistance due to the people we serve. [[We also ban paying for sex while working for us, even where it is legal.]]
4. Sexual relationships with the people we serve that involve improper use of rank or position are prohibited, because of the unequal power involved. Any relationship with a person we serve must be declared to the safeguarding lead.
5. Anyone who has a concern or suspicion about sexual exploitation or abuse by anyone connected to us must report it.
6. Everyone is responsible for creating and keeping an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse.

### Working with children and adults at risk

- Follow our Safeguarding Policy and Procedure at all times.
- Avoid being alone with a child or adult at risk where you cannot be seen by others. If it cannot be avoided, tell your manager or the safeguarding lead beforehand and record it.
- Never hit, shake, threaten or physically punish anyone. Never use language or behaviour that is sexual, abusive, shaming or degrading.
- Never contact children or adults at risk through personal phones or social media accounts unless the safeguarding lead has agreed a safe way to do so.
- Never take, keep or share images of children or adults at risk without informed consent and a clear purpose that follows our Photo and Story Consent Form.
- Never create, view, store or share sexual or abusive material, including child sexual abuse material, on any device.

If your organisation has no direct contact with children or adults at risk, say so here, and keep the duty to report.

### Reporting concerns

If you see, suspect or are told about harm, abuse, exploitation, harassment, fraud or any other breach of this code, report it within [[24 hours]]. You do not need proof.

- Report safeguarding concerns to the safeguarding lead, [[Name]], at [[email / phone]]. Report other breaches, such as fraud or lost data, to [[Name, role]] under our Incident Reporting Procedure. If unsure, tell the safeguarding lead.
- If the concern is about the safeguarding lead, or they are involved, report to [[Name, role]] at [[email / phone]].
- If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first on [[number]], then tell us.
- You can also use our Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure, including [[an anonymous route, if you have one]].

We acknowledge every report within [[2 working days]], and the same day if someone may be at risk. Do not investigate yourself, and do not confront the person concerned. Keep the matter confidential and cooperate with any review. No one is penalised for raising a genuine concern, even if it proves unfounded. Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern is itself a serious breach of this code.

## 4. Honesty and integrity

- Be truthful about who you are, your qualifications and experience, and the work you have done.
- Report results honestly. Never invent, exaggerate or hide information in reports, proposals, timesheets, expense claims or communications.
- Never offer, give, request or accept a bribe, a facilitation payment or any other improper advantage. Our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure explains what to do.
- Use the organisation's money, equipment and time only for its purposes, and follow our financial procedures.
- Do not accept gifts or hospitality connected to your work except small tokens worth less than [[amount and currency]]. Declare anything above that to [[role]], who records it in the gifts register.

::: adapt Gifts threshold
Set a realistic amount for your context, for example the price of a meal or a small locally made gift. Some organisations ban all gifts from people they serve. If you receive gifts from partners or suppliers at certain times of year, say how they are handled (for example, shared with the team or politely returned).
:::

## 5. Conflicts of interest

Declare any actual, possible or perceived conflict of interest to [[role]] as soon as you become aware of it, and before any related decision. Examples:

- A family member, partner or close friend applies for a job, a contract or support from us.
- You, or someone close to you, has a financial interest in a supplier, partner or funder.
- You work for, or advise, an organisation that competes with us or funds us.

Step out of any decision where you have a conflict. Our Conflict of Interest Policy explains how conflicts are recorded and managed.

## 6. Information, privacy and technology

- Collect only the personal information we need, use it only for the reason we collected it, and keep it secure. Our Data Protection Policy sets out the details.
- Keep confidential information confidential, during and after your time with us.
- Never share passwords. Use your own account, and lock devices that hold our information.
- Report any loss, theft or unauthorised sharing of information to [[role]] within [[24 hours]].
- Do not put personal, confidential or safeguarding information into AI tools or other online services that we have not approved. Check anything AI produces for accuracy and bias: you remain responsible for your work.

## 7. Health, safety and wellbeing

- Look after your own safety and that of others. Report hazards, accidents and near misses.
- Do not work under the influence of alcohol or drugs that affect your judgement or safety.
- Follow our security and travel guidance when working away from the office.
- Take the rest you are entitled to. Nobody is expected to work beyond what is reasonable, or to continue if they feel unsafe.

# Talking about our work publicly

- Only [[Name, role]], or someone they authorise, speaks to the media or makes public statements for [[Organisation name]].
- When you post about your work on personal accounts, make clear that the views are your own, and never share confidential information.
- Never share the identity, images, stories or personal details of the people we serve without their informed consent, given through our consent process. Never use images that could identify children at risk or put anyone in danger.
- Tell people the truth about our work. Do not exaggerate results, and do not present people as helpless or use images that undermine their dignity.

# Extra standards for leaders and the board

People with authority have more power, and so more responsibility. Managers, the safeguarding lead and board members also:

- model this code and make sure the people they manage know it;
- take every concern seriously, act on it promptly and keep it confidential;
- never discourage or punish anyone for raising a concern;
- declare their own interests every year and step out of decisions where they are conflicted;
- make sure that safeguarding, fraud and other serious incidents are reported to the board and, where required, to funders and regulators.

# If the code is breached

Report breaches as described in section 3. We take every report seriously and act in proportion to the risk. Depending on the case, we may:

- give guidance, a reminder or a written warning;
- suspend someone from their role while we look into a concern (this is not a finding of wrongdoing);
- end a contract, volunteer role or board membership, or dismiss an employee, following our disciplinary procedure and the law;
- report to the police or other authorities where the law requires it, or where someone is in immediate danger;
- inform funders, regulators or partners where our agreements or the law require it.

For serious concerns, including sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment, we may act straight away to keep people safe. For serious or complex cases we may bring in an external investigator. Where the law allows, we may share confirmed findings of sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment with future employers who ask, following the principles of the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme.

Anyone affected by a decision under this code can ask for it to be reviewed within [[30 days]], unless the policy that applies sets a different limit, by writing to [[Name, role]], who was not involved in the original decision.
