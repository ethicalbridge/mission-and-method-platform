Name real people. One person may hold two roles in a very small board, but the chair and the treasurer should be different people, and neither should be the founder if the founder is paid staff. The safeguarding lead board member must not be managed by, or closely related to, the founder. If no one can take this role yet, name an independent contact (see the Independent Contact Role Note) and plan to recruit someone with safeguarding experience.

# Status of these terms

These terms sit under the [[constitution / articles of association / trust deed / statute]] of [[Organisation name]] (the "governing document") and the law of [[country]]. If anything here conflicts with the governing document or the law, the governing document or the law prevails, and the board corrects these terms at its next meeting.

::: adapt Your governing document and local law
Read your governing document first and copy its rules on numbers, terms, quorum and voting into the sections below. Local law often fixes some points. Examples to check:

- **England and Wales:** charity trustees' duties come from the Charities Act 2011 and Charity Commission guidance ("The essential trustee"). Trustees cannot normally be paid unless the governing document, the Commission or the law allows it.
- **South Africa:** a non-profit company under the Companies Act 2008 needs at least three directors. Organisations registered under the Nonprofit Organisations Act 1997 report to the NPO Directorate.
- **Nigeria:** incorporated trustees are registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission under Part F of the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020.
- **Kenya:** check your registration category and the Public Benefit Organizations Act 2013, which came into force in 2024 and replaces the NGO Coordination Act. Societies and companies limited by guarantee follow other rules.
- **India:** trusts, societies and Section 8 companies (Companies Act 2013) have different board rules.
- **Brazil and much of Latin America:** associations usually have a general assembly, a board (diretoria) and often a fiscal council, set by the statute and the Civil Code.

If your highest body is a general assembly of members, add a short section on its powers (usually electing the board, approving the accounts and changing the statute).
:::

# Purpose and duties

The board holds [[Organisation name]] in trust for its mission and the people it serves. It makes sure that the organisation:

- pursues the purposes in its governing document;
- stays solvent, uses its money and assets properly and keeps proper accounts;
- complies with the law, its registration conditions and its funding agreements;
- keeps people safe, especially children and adults at risk;
- manages its risks and protects its reputation;
- has a [[Executive Director / founder]] who is supported and held to account.

The board acts collectively. No individual member can act for the board unless the board has delegated that authority in writing. Each member must act in the best interests of the organisation, not their own or those of anyone who nominated them; act within the governing document and the law; use reasonable care and skill; declare conflicts of interest; keep board business confidential; prepare for and attend meetings; and follow our Code of Conduct.

# Powers and reserved matters

The board delegates day-to-day running to the [[Executive Director / founder]] through the Delegation of Authority Matrix. The following decisions are **reserved to the board**. Only the board, at a quorate meeting or by written resolution, may take them.

{widths: 28 72}
| Area | Decisions only the board takes |
|---|---|
| Mission and strategy | Approve the strategy and any major change of direction; start or close a programme or country of operation |
| Legal form | Propose changes to the governing document; change legal form; merge with or create another entity; dissolve the organisation |
| Money | Approve the annual budget and any change of more than [[10%]]; approve the annual accounts; appoint the auditor or examiner; approve the Reserves Policy |
| Commitments | Approve any contract, grant or loan above [[amount and currency]] or longer than [[3 years]]; any borrowing or guarantee; buying, selling or leasing property |
| People | Appoint, set the pay of, appraise and dismiss the [[Executive Director / founder]]; approve the staff structure and pay policy |
| Policies and risk | Approve core policies, including the Code of Conduct, Safeguarding Policy and Procedure, Financial Controls Policy and these terms; approve the Risk Register each year |
| Delegation and banking | Approve the Delegation of Authority Matrix; set up committees; open or close bank accounts and appoint signatories |
| Governance | Appoint and remove members and officers within the governing document; approve related-party transactions; decide on serious incident reports to regulators and funders |

::: adapt Reserved matters and thresholds
Set thresholds against your annual budget, so the board sees big decisions without approving every purchase. A common rule for a small organisation is that any commitment above 5–10% of annual income, or any contract longer than the current funding, goes to the board. Make sure the thresholds match the Delegation of Authority Matrix exactly.
:::

# Composition

## 1. Size and skills

The board has at least [[3]] and no more than [[9]] members, as the governing document allows. We aim for [[5 to 7]]: enough to share the work and hear different views, few enough to decide quickly. Together, members should have the skills in Annex A. Each year the chair updates the skills matrix and uses it to plan recruitment and training.

## 2. Independence

Most members, and always the chair and treasurer, must be **independent**. Independent means the person:

- is not, and has not been in the last [[3]] years, paid by [[Organisation name]] as staff or contractor;
- is not a close family member or partner of a staff member, especially the [[Executive Director / founder]];
- has no business link with us, our main funders or suppliers that could affect their judgement.

Members who are not independent may sit on the board if the governing document allows. They do not take part in decisions about their own pay, role or performance.

## 3. Diversity and community representation

We want a board that reflects the communities we serve, with different experience, ages, genders and backgrounds. At least [[one / two]] members should have lived experience of the issues we work on or come from the communities where we work. We make taking part practical: meetings in [[language(s)]], plain-language papers, interpretation where needed, suitable meeting times and costs paid in advance where needed. Our Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy applies to board recruitment.

::: adapt Local and community voice
Decide how community voice reaches the board: reserved seats for people from the communities you serve; a community advisory group whose chair attends board meetings; or participants invited to one meeting a year. Avoid token seats: give these members the same papers, induction and vote as everyone else. If you work in several countries, consider a member based in each main country of operation.
:::

# Appointment, terms and rotation

## 4. Appointment

1. The board identifies gaps using the skills matrix and agrees a role description.
2. We advertise vacancies openly [[through our networks, website and partners]], not only through personal contacts.
3. At least two board members meet each candidate, who also meets the [[Executive Director / founder]] and receives these terms, the governing document, the latest accounts and the strategy.
4. Before appointment, the candidate completes a Board Interest Declaration and confirms they are not disqualified by law. We carry out [[references / safeguarding checks where lawful]] under our Safeguarding Policy and Procedure.
5. The board appoints by [[majority vote / election at the general assembly]]. The secretary records the appointment and files any notice the regulator requires within [[number]] days.

## 5. Terms of office and rotation

- Each member serves [[3]] years and may be reappointed for up to [[two]] further terms, a maximum of [[9]] years in a row. A member may return after a break of at least [[one year]].
- About [[one-third]] of members finish their term each year, to keep continuity.
- The chair serves for [[3]] years and may be reappointed once.
- A member leaves the board if they resign in writing, are disqualified by law, miss [[three]] meetings in a row without reasons the board accepts, or are removed under the governing document.

::: adapt Terms and eligibility
Copy term lengths from your governing document. Nine years in total is a widely used good-practice limit. Founding boards sometimes keep founding members longer while recruiting successors; if so, set a date for the first rotation. Check who is disqualified by law in your country (for example people with certain convictions or disqualified company directors) and whether you must check for this.
:::

# Officers

## 6. Chair

The chair leads the board. The chair agrees agendas, chairs meetings so that every member is heard and decisions are clear, and is the main link with the [[Executive Director / founder]]. They meet the [[Executive Director / founder]] at least [[monthly]], lead their annual appraisal, and lead the board evaluation and succession planning. Between meetings the chair may take urgent decisions only within section 10.

## 7. Treasurer

The treasurer leads the board's oversight of money. They review the management accounts at least [[quarterly]] and present them in plain language; lead the review of the budget, the Reserves Policy and the annual accounts; are the board's contact with the auditor or examiner; and check that our Financial Controls Policy is followed. The treasurer does not keep the books or make payments: that is a management task.

## 8. Secretary

The secretary sends notice and papers on time, makes sure minutes are taken, and keeps the registers of members, decisions and interests. With the [[Executive Director / founder]], they file the returns and notices the regulator requires.

::: adapt Officers in your governing document
Some laws or governing documents require named officers, for example a president, secretary and treasurer for many associations and societies. Use the titles your governing document uses. If a staff member takes minutes or acts as company secretary, say so and make clear they do not vote.
:::

# Meetings

## 9. Frequency, notice, quorum and decisions

- The board meets at least [[four]] times a year, following the calendar in Annex B, and when the chair or [[two]] members ask.
- The secretary sends notice at least [[14 days]] before, and papers at least [[7 days]] before. Each paper says what decision is needed. Urgent meetings may be called with [[48 hours']] notice.
- The [[Executive Director / founder]] attends but [[does not vote]]. The board meets without staff for part of at least one meeting a year.
- A meeting is quorate when [[3 members / one-third of members, whichever is greater]] are present, most of them independent.
- The board aims for consensus. Otherwise decisions need a simple majority of members present and voting. If votes are equal, the chair [[has / does not have]] a casting vote.

## 10. Decisions between meetings

The board may decide by **written resolution**, for example by email, if [[all members / the majority required by the governing document]] agree in writing. In an emergency, such as a safeguarding crisis or an immediate risk to funds, the chair and [[one other officer]] may take a decision that cannot wait. They consult as many members as they can, record the reasons and report at the next meeting.

## 11. Conflicts of interest

At the start of each meeting the chair asks for declarations. A member with a conflict declares it before the item, leaves or stays silent as the board decides under the Conflict of Interest Policy, and does not vote or count in the quorum for that item. The minutes record each declaration and how it was managed. Every member completes a Board Interest Declaration on appointment and each year, and updates it within [[28 days]] of any change.

## 12. Virtual meetings and minutes

Members may join by video or phone, and count as present, if everyone can hear each other and take part. Before any vote the chair asks each remote member in turn. We use secure links and do not record meetings unless the board agrees.

The secretary circulates draft minutes within [[14 days]]. Minutes record attendance, declarations of interest, each decision with the main reasons, and actions with owners and dates. The board approves them at the next meeting and the chair signs them. We keep them as set out in our Records Retention Schedule.

::: adapt Virtual meetings and signatures
Check that your governing document allows virtual meetings and email resolutions. Many older constitutions do not, and some regulators and banks still want signed minutes for certain decisions, such as changing bank signatories. If yours does not allow them, propose an amendment.
:::

# Committees and lead roles

The board may set up committees with written terms of reference. Committees report to the next board meeting and cannot take reserved decisions unless the board delegates them in writing. While we are small we [[have no standing committees / have the following]]:

- **Finance and audit committee** [[if needed]]: chaired by the treasurer, it reviews budgets, accounts, audit findings, financial controls, the Risk Register and fraud reports.
- **Safeguarding lead board member:** [[Name]] receives a summary of every safeguarding concern, checks each is handled under the Safeguarding Policy and Procedure, and reports to every meeting without naming people.
- **Nominations and people** [[optional]]: plans recruitment, and with the chair leads the [[Executive Director / founder]]'s appraisal and pay review.
- **Working groups** for a specific task, such as a strategy review.

::: adapt Committees for your size
A board of five rarely needs standing committees; the whole board can review finance at every meeting. Add a finance and audit committee when income or the number of grants grows, or a funder requires one. Some funders ask for a named board member for safeguarding and for fraud; one person may hold both.
:::

# Relationship with the [[Executive Director / founder]]

## 13. Governance and management

The board governs. The [[Executive Director / founder]] and staff manage.

{widths: 50 50}
| The board | The Executive Director or founder |
|---|---|
| Agrees the mission, strategy and annual budget | Proposes the strategy and budget, and delivers them |
| Sets policies and the limits of delegated authority | Runs the organisation within those policies and limits |
| Appoints, supports and appraises the [[Executive Director / founder]] | Recruits, manages and develops staff and volunteers |
| Asks questions, checks results and risks, holds management to account | Reports honestly on progress, finances, risks and incidents |

The Delegation of Authority Matrix sets out which decisions the [[Executive Director / founder]] and staff may take, and up to what amount. The board reviews it [[once a year]]. The [[Executive Director / founder]] may sub-delegate within it but stays accountable.

For each meeting the [[Executive Director / founder]] reports on: progress against plan; management accounts and cash forecast; the top risks; safeguarding, fraud, data and serious complaint incidents (anonymised); funder and regulatory matters; and decisions needed. They tell the chair within [[24 hours]] of any serious incident.

## 14. Founder-led organisations

Many small organisations are led by their founder, who may also sit on the board. This brings energy and commitment. It also brings risks: too much depends on one person, the board may find it hard to challenge the founder, and it can be unclear when the founder acts as staff and when as a board member. So:

- if the founder is paid staff, they do not chair the board or act as treasurer;
- the founder steps out of decisions on their own pay, appraisal, contract or conduct;
- concerns about the founder go to the chair or the safeguarding lead board member, never to the founder;
- the board agrees a written plan for what happens if the founder suddenly cannot work;
- once a year the board and founder review together how decisions are shared.

::: adapt If the founder sits on the board
Decide whether your founder is a voting member, a non-voting attendee, or staff only. In some countries paid staff cannot be voting board members of a charity or public benefit organisation; in others it is common. Many funders prefer that a paid founder does not vote, or at least that independent members are a clear majority. If the founder is currently the only director in law, recruiting independent members is your first governance action: target date [[date]].
:::

# Induction, training and evaluation

Within [[3 months]] of joining, each new member receives an induction: the governing document, these terms, the strategy, accounts and budget, the Risk Register and core policies, a meeting with the chair and the [[Executive Director / founder]], and where possible a visit to our work. Each signs the Code of Conduct and completes safeguarding training, refreshed every [[2]] years.

Once a year the chair leads a short board evaluation: a one-page questionnaire for members and the [[Executive Director / founder]], then a discussion. It covers focus, papers, attendance, skills and diversity, and the relationship with management. The board agrees up to [[three]] improvements and checks them a year later. Every [[three]] years someone independent helps.

# Confidentiality

Board members keep confidential all non-public information they receive as members, including personal information about staff, volunteers and the people we serve, safeguarding cases, and funder information. This continues after they leave. They follow our Data Protection Policy, keep papers secure and return or delete them on leaving. Confidentiality never prevents a member from raising a concern under our Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure or with a regulator.

# Expenses and payment

Board members are [[unpaid volunteers]]. We reimburse reasonable costs of board business, such as travel, data or phone credit and care costs, under our Expenses and Per Diem Policy, and pay in advance where that makes taking part possible. A member may be paid for other services only if the governing document and the law allow it, the board approves in advance with that member absent, and the payment is recorded in the register of interests and the accounts.

::: adapt Paying board members
In many countries non-profit board members are expected to be unpaid, and some laws forbid payment without the regulator's approval. In others, such as some co-operatives, a modest allowance is usual. Check your local law, governing document and funders' rules. Keep any payment small, approved in advance and disclosed in your accounts.
:::

# Review of these terms

The board reviews these terms every [[two years]], and when the governing document, the law or our size changes significantly.
