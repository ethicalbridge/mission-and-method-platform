::: adapt Before you use this agreement
**Use it** when one organisation delivers defined services to another for a fee: an evaluation, training, IT work, research, or a social enterprise selling its consultancy. "The Client" receives and pays for the services; "the Provider" delivers them. Put [[Organisation name]] in the right column. Later boxes show where each side usually pushes.

**Do not use it** for one individual working mostly for you (use the Independent Contractor Agreement) or for passing grant funds to a partner (use the Sub-grant Agreement).

**Local law:** some clauses, such as limits on liability, may not be enforced everywhere. For contracts above [[amount and currency]] or with high risk, ask a lawyer in [[country]] to review the final version, using the Legal Review Checklist.
:::

# Agreed terms

## 1. Definitions and interpretation

1. In this agreement:
  1. **Acceptance Criteria** means the criteria for each Deliverable in Schedule 1.
  2. **Business Day** means a day other than a weekend or public holiday in [[country or city]].
  3. **Charges** means the fees and reimbursable expenses in Schedule 2.
  4. **Confidential Information** means information shared under this agreement that is marked confidential or that a reasonable person would see as confidential, including personal data.
  5. **Data Protection Law** means all laws on personal data that apply to either party, including [[e.g. the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 / the EU or UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)]].
  6. **Deliverables** means the outputs listed in Schedule 1.
  7. **Intellectual Property Rights** means copyright, trade marks, designs, database rights, patents, rights in know-how and similar rights anywhere in the world.
  8. **Personnel** means a party's staff, volunteers and subcontractors.
  9. **Services** means the services in Schedule 1, as changed under clause 6.
2. "Clause 6.3" means paragraph 3 of clause 6. The schedules form part of this agreement. If a clause conflicts with a schedule, the clause prevails, except that Schedule 3 prevails on personal data.
3. "Including" means including without limitation. "In writing" includes email, but not [[instant or text messages]].

## 2. Term

1. This agreement starts on [[the date of the last signature below / date]] (the **Effective Date**). It continues until [[end date]], or until the Services are complete and paid for, unless it ends earlier under clause 19 or is extended by a change note.

## 3. The Services

1. The Provider will provide the Services and Deliverables in Schedule 1, by the dates set out there, using its own equipment unless Schedule 1 says otherwise.
2. The Provider will work with reasonable skill, care and diligence, in line with good practice in [[sector, e.g. evaluation / IT services]], using suitably qualified Personnel.
3. The Provider will not replace key Personnel named in Schedule 1 without the Client's consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.
4. The Provider will tell the Client in writing as soon as it expects a delay, with the cause and a likely new date.

## 4. The Client's responsibilities

1. The Client will provide on time the information, access, decisions, staff time and facilities listed in Schedule 1 as Client inputs, and make sure they are accurate as far as it knows.
2. The Client will name a contract manager who can make day-to-day decisions.
3. If a Client delay prevents the Provider from performing, the Provider is not in breach and gets a reasonable extension and any extra costs agreed under clause 6.

## 5. Acceptance

1. Within [[10]] Business Days of receiving a Deliverable, the Client will accept it in writing or reject it with written reasons that refer to the Acceptance Criteria.
2. If the Client rejects it, the Provider will correct and resubmit it within [[10]] Business Days at no extra charge, and clause 5.1 applies again.
3. If the Client rejects the same Deliverable [[twice]] for substantially the same reasons, it may accept it with a fair, agreed reduction in the Charges, or end this agreement under clause 19.3.
4. A Deliverable is treated as accepted if the Client does not respond within [[5]] Business Days of a written reminder sent after the period in clause 5.1, or when the Client uses it in its work other than for review.
5. Acceptance does not affect the Client's rights under clause 10.4 for hidden defects.

## 6. Changes

1. Either party may ask in writing for a change. Within [[5]] Business Days, the Provider will set out its effect on the Deliverables, timetable and Charges.
2. A change is binding only when both parties sign a change note in the form in Schedule 2. Neither party must perform, or pay for, out-of-scope work without one.
3. The contract managers may agree by email small changes that do not affect the Charges, the end date or the Acceptance Criteria.

::: adapt Acceptance and change control
Make the Acceptance Criteria in Schedule 1 concrete. **If you are the Client,** allow enough review time for your team and any funder that must approve. **If you are the Provider,** keep deemed acceptance in clause 5.4; without it, a client can delay payment by not replying. Limit the review rounds included in the price (two is common) and price extra rounds through clause 6.
:::

## 7. Charges and payment

1. The Client will pay the Charges in Schedule 2. They are [[a fixed price / daily rates up to a maximum]], and the total will not exceed [[amount and currency]] without a signed change note.
2. The Provider will invoice [[on acceptance of each Deliverable / monthly in arrears]], quoting this agreement's reference and [[the purchase order number]].
3. The Client will pay each correct, undisputed invoice within [[30]] days of receipt, in [[currency]], into the Provider's account in Schedule 2. Each party pays its own bank charges.
4. If the Client disputes an invoice in good faith, it will say why in writing within [[10]] Business Days and pay the undisputed part on time. Late undisputed payments carry interest at [[X% a year above the central bank rate / the rate set by law]].
5. [[Optional: The Client will pay an advance of X% of the Charges within 10 Business Days of the Effective Date, deducted from later invoices.]]
6. The Client will confirm any change of bank account by telephone with the Provider's contract manager, on the number in this agreement, before paying into it.

## 8. Expenses

1. The Charges include all the Provider's costs, except expenses that Schedule 2 lists as reimbursable. The Client will reimburse those only if they are necessary, approved in writing in advance [[when above amount]], in line with [[the Client's Expenses and Per Diem Policy]], supported by receipts and invoiced within [[60]] days, with no mark-up.

## 9. Taxes

1. The Charges are [[exclusive / inclusive]] of value added tax (VAT) or similar taxes. Where VAT is due on top, the Client will pay it against a valid tax invoice.
2. The Provider is responsible for the income tax, social security and other payments due for itself and its Personnel.
3. If the law requires the Client to withhold tax from a payment, the Client will pay it to the tax authority on time and give the Provider the official receipt within [[30]] days. The Charges [[will / will not]] be increased to cover it.

::: adapt Payment, expenses and tax
**If you are the Client:** tie payments to accepted Deliverables, hold back [[10–20]]% until the last one, and check that your funder allows advances. **If you are the Provider:** ask for an advance (20–30% is common for small providers) and interest on late payment.

**Country notes:** some laws set interest on late business payments, for example the UK Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998. Many countries, including Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, India and Peru, require the payer to withhold tax from service fees. Rates, non-profit exemptions and treaty rates change often: check with a tax adviser, and agree whether fees are "gross" (tax comes out of them) or "net" (paid on top).
:::

## 10. Warranties

1. Each party confirms that it has the authority to enter into this agreement.
2. The Provider confirms that it holds the licences needed for the Services; that the Deliverables will not infringe anyone's Intellectual Property Rights; and that the information it gave during selection was true.
3. The Client confirms that it may lawfully share the materials it provides.
4. If, within [[3 months]] after acceptance, a Deliverable is found not to meet this agreement, the Provider will correct it at its own cost.

## 11. Confidentiality

1. Each party will keep the other's Confidential Information confidential and use it only for this agreement.
2. A party may share it only with Personnel, advisers, auditors [[and funders]] who need it and who are bound by similar duties of confidentiality.
3. This clause does not cover information that becomes public through no fault of the receiving party, or that it already held, develops independently or receives lawfully from someone else. A party may disclose what the law or a regulator requires, telling the other first where lawful.
4. The Client may share this agreement and the Deliverables with its funders, auditors and regulators, and publish the Deliverables as stated in Schedule 1.
5. These duties continue for [[5]] years after this agreement ends, and without time limit for personal data, safeguarding information and trade secrets.

## 12. Data protection

1. Each party will comply with Data Protection Law.
2. Schedule 1 states whether the Provider will handle personal data for the Client. Where the Provider processes personal data on the Client's behalf and on its instructions, the Provider is a processor and Schedule 3 applies.
3. Where each party decides for itself why and how it uses personal data, such as staff contact details, each is an independent controller. Neither party will share personal data the other does not need.

::: adapt Data protection roles
**A processor** only follows instructions: for example, a firm that hosts your database, runs payroll or transcribes interviews you designed. Most data protection laws require a written contract with a processor. GDPR Article 28 lists the required terms, and Schedule 3 follows it. South Africa's POPIA (sections 20 and 21) sets similar duties for an "operator". Check your local law on processor contracts.

**An independent controller** decides its own purposes: an evaluator that designs and publishes its own survey may be one. If you decide purposes together, you may be joint controllers, which needs a different arrangement. If unsure, use the Data Sharing Agreement or take advice.
:::

## 13. Intellectual property

1. Each party keeps its Intellectual Property Rights in materials that existed before this agreement or that it develops independently ("Background IP").
2. **Option A: the Client owns the Deliverables.** On [[payment in full for each Deliverable]], the Provider assigns to the Client all Intellectual Property Rights in the Deliverables, except the Provider's Background IP and third-party materials.
3. **Option B: the Provider owns the Deliverables.** The Provider keeps the Intellectual Property Rights in the Deliverables and grants the Client a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual and irrevocable licence to use, copy, adapt, translate and share them for the Client's [[organisational and non-commercial]] purposes, including with its funders, partners and communities.
4. Where a Deliverable includes the Provider's Background IP or third-party materials, the Provider grants or obtains a licence on the terms in clause 13.3 for their use as part of it. The Provider may reuse its general know-how and tools, but not the Client's Confidential Information.
5. The Provider will obtain from its Personnel the rights it needs for this clause and, as far as the law allows, waivers of moral rights (the author's right to be named and to object to changes). The Client will credit authors when it publishes [[if asked]].
6. Images, stories and data about people collected during the Services may be used only within the consent those people gave.

::: adapt Who owns the work
**If you are the Client:** Option A is usual for bespoke work you will reuse. Check your grant agreement first: many funders require a licence for themselves, or open publication, for example under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC BY 4.0).

**If you are the Provider** with your own toolkits, choose Option B, or list your tools as Background IP.

**Country notes:** a copyright assignment usually must be in writing and signed. In many civil-law countries, including Brazil and much of Latin America and continental Europe, moral rights cannot be waived, so clause 13.5 applies only as far as the law allows. Check your local copyright law. Delete the option you do not use.
:::

## 14. Compliance and conduct

1. Each party will comply with all laws that apply to this agreement, including on bribery, money laundering, sanctions, labour, health and safety and data protection.
2. **Bribery and fraud.** Neither party, nor its Personnel, will offer, give, request or accept a bribe, facilitation payment or other improper advantage. Each will report any suspected bribery or fraud connected with this agreement to the other within [[48 hours]]. Laws with reach beyond borders, such as the UK Bribery Act 2010 and the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, may apply.
3. **Sanctions.** Each party confirms that neither it, nor its owners, board members, key Personnel or subcontractors, is on the UN Security Council Consolidated List or [[the US OFAC, EU, UK or national sanctions lists]], and that no money under this agreement will reach anyone on them. Each will tell the other at once if this changes.
4. **Safeguarding.** Where the Provider's Personnel may have contact with children or adults at risk, or their data, they will follow [[the Client's Safeguarding Policy and Procedure / the Provider's own policy, if at least as protective]], including the six core principles of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) on sexual exploitation and abuse. The Provider will vet them, [[with references and, where lawful, criminal record and Misconduct Disclosure Scheme checks]]. Each party will report any safeguarding concern connected with this agreement to the other, at [[email]], within [[24 hours]].
5. **Conduct.** The Provider's Personnel will follow [[the Client's Code of Conduct]] and declare any conflict of interest. The Provider will not use forced or child labour.
6. **Records and audit.** The Provider will keep accurate records of the Services, time and expenses for [[6]] years after this agreement ends, and let the Client, [[its funders]] and their auditors inspect them on [[10]] Business Days' notice.
7. A serious breach of this clause is a material breach that cannot be remedied, for the purpose of clause 19.3.

::: adapt Compliance clauses
Keep this clause mutual. **If you are the Client,** share the policies you name before signing, and check your grant agreement for clauses you must pass on, such as audit rights or specific sanctions lists: copy them accurately. **If you are the Provider,** check you can meet the vetting, reporting and audit duties. Criminal record checks are unavailable or restricted in some countries: use what is lawful locally and always take references. Record sanctions screening as the Partner Due Diligence and Sanctions Screening Policy explains.
:::

## 15. Liability

1. Nothing in this agreement limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud, for [[wilful misconduct]], or for anything that cannot be limited by law.
2. Neither party is liable for loss of profits, revenue or goodwill, or for indirect or consequential loss, [[except that the Client may recover any amount it must repay to a funder as a direct result of the Provider's breach]].
3. Subject to clauses 15.1 and 15.4, each party's total liability under this agreement is limited to [[100% / 150%]] of the total Charges paid and payable.
4. This limit does not apply to the Client's duty to pay the Charges. [[For breach of clause 11 or clause 12 and Schedule 3, the limit is amount and currency.]]

## 16. Indemnities

1. The Provider will indemnify the Client against losses, costs and reasonable legal fees arising from any third-party claim that a Deliverable infringes their Intellectual Property Rights, except where the claim arises from the Client's materials or changes.
2. [[Optional: The Provider will indemnify the Client against third-party claims for personal injury or damage to property caused by the negligence of the Provider's Personnel.]]
3. A party claiming under an indemnity must tell the other promptly, not admit liability, and let the other control the defence.
4. These indemnities are subject to the limit in clause 15.3.

## 17. Insurance

1. During this agreement and for [[2]] years after it, the Provider will hold professional indemnity insurance of at least [[amount]] per claim, public liability insurance of at least [[amount]], and any insurance the law requires for its employees. It will show evidence on request.

::: adapt Liability, indemnities and insurance
**The cap:** 100% of the fees is common for small contracts; clients with more at stake may ask for 150–200%. **If you are the Client,** keep the funder-repayment wording in clause 15.2: a grant you must repay might otherwise count as excluded "lost revenue". **If you are the Provider,** never accept unlimited liability, and match the cap to your insurance.

**Indemnities** are promises to pay specific losses, often whether or not they could be claimed as damages, so they can cost far more than the contract is worth. Keep them narrow and under the cap. Refuse wording such as "indemnify against all losses arising from this agreement", and check whether your insurance covers liability taken on by contract: often it does not. If a small provider cannot buy professional indemnity insurance, reduce or delete clause 17.

**Country notes:** in the UK, limits in standard business terms must pass the reasonableness test of the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977. In many civil-law countries, liability for wilful misconduct or gross negligence cannot be limited, and indemnities may be treated differently from English-style contracts. Check your local law.
:::

## 18. Personnel and subcontracting

1. The Provider is an independent contractor. This agreement creates no employment, partnership, agency or joint venture. The Provider alone is responsible for its Personnel's pay, taxes, social security, insurance and benefits.
2. The Provider will not subcontract any part of the Services without the Client's prior written consent. It remains responsible for subcontractors and will pass on to them the duties in clauses 11 to 14.

## 19. Force majeure and termination

1. **Force majeure.** Neither party is in breach for a delay caused by events beyond its reasonable control, such as natural disasters, epidemics, war, civil unrest or government action, but not a shortage of money. The affected party will tell the other within [[5]] Business Days and limit the effect; deadlines are extended accordingly. If the event lasts more than [[60]] days, either party may end this agreement by [[10]] days' written notice.
2. **For convenience.** [[Either party / The Client]] may end this agreement by [[30]] days' written notice.
3. **For cause.** Either party may end this agreement at once by written notice if the other party:
  1. commits a material breach that cannot be remedied, or that it does not remedy within [[15]] days of written notice;
  2. becomes insolvent, enters liquidation, is deregistered or stops operating; or
  3. commits a serious breach of clause 14.
4. **Loss of funding.** If the Client's funding for the Services is withdrawn, suspended or reduced, the Client may end this agreement or reduce the Services by [[15]] days' written notice.
5. **Non-payment.** If an undisputed invoice is unpaid [[30]] days after a written reminder, the Provider may suspend the Services on [[7]] days' written notice until it is paid.
6. **On ending.** The Client will pay for Services properly performed up to the end date, plus approved costs the Provider cannot cancel, but not for rejected work. The Provider will refund any unearned advance within [[30]] days, hand over all work, and return or delete the Client's materials and personal data.
7. Clauses 11 to 16, 19.6, 20 and 21 survive the end of this agreement.

::: adapt Ending the agreement
**If you are the Client:** keep the loss-of-funding clause. Grant-funded organisations often cannot pay when a funder pulls out, and this lets you end cleanly while paying for work done.

**If you are the Provider:** ask for a cancellation fee (for example [[10]]% of the remaining Charges) for time you have reserved. Some countries treat a long contract with one main client as employment or agency, with notice rights. Check your local law.
:::

## 20. Disputes and governing law

1. The contract managers, and then [[the heads of the two organisations]], will each try to settle any dispute within [[10]] Business Days. If it is still unresolved, the parties [[will / may]] try mediation through [[mediator or mediation centre]], sharing the costs equally.
2. If mediation fails, the dispute will be settled by [[the courts of country or city, which have exclusive jurisdiction / arbitration under the rules of named institution, with one arbitrator, seated in city, in English]]. Either party may still ask a court for urgent interim relief.
3. This agreement is governed by the law of [[country or state]].

## 21. Notices and general terms

1. Formal notices must be emailed to the other party's contract manager. Notices of breach, termination or dispute must also go by hand or courier to the registered address. An email sent in the recipient's business hours is received when sent; otherwise on the next Business Day.
2. This is the whole agreement about the Services. Changes are valid only in writing signed by both parties.
3. Neither party may transfer its rights or duties without the other's written consent. Failing to enforce a right does not waive it. If any part is invalid, the rest continues.
4. No one else has rights under this agreement, [[except the Client's funders and their auditors under clause 14.6]].
5. Neither party will use the other's name or logo without consent, [[except that the Provider may list the Client as a client]].
6. This agreement may be signed in counterparts and electronically. If translated, the [[English]] version prevails.

::: adapt Law, disputes and signing
**Governing law:** usually the law where one of you is registered and the Services are delivered. **Courts or arbitration:** a court judgment is often hard to enforce abroad. Arbitration awards are enforceable in most countries under the 1958 New York Convention, but arbitration can cost more than a small contract is worth. UN agencies usually require arbitration under the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules.

**Formalities:** check whether the contract needs a stamp or seal, witnesses or stamp duty to be valid or used as evidence (for example, stamp duty in many Indian states, or the meterai duty stamp in Indonesia), and whether electronic signatures are accepted.
:::

#### PART B: THE PROVIDER'S DUTIES AS PROCESSOR

When it processes personal data for the Client, the Provider will:

1. process it only on the Client's documented instructions (this agreement and this schedule), including on transfers, unless the law requires otherwise, in which case it will tell the Client first if lawful;
2. make sure everyone authorised to process the data is bound by confidentiality;
3. take appropriate technical and organisational security measures, including at least [[access limited to named Personnel, multi-factor authentication, encrypted devices and transfers, and secure backups]];
4. not use a sub-processor without the Client's prior written authorisation, give it the same duties in writing, and remain responsible for it;
5. help the Client respond to people exercising their data rights, forwarding any request within [[5]] Business Days;
6. help the Client with security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments and consultation with regulators;
7. tell the Client of any personal data breach without undue delay, and within [[24 hours / 48 hours]] of becoming aware of it, with the information the Client needs;
8. at the end of the Services, at the Client's choice, delete or return all the personal data and delete copies, unless the law requires it to keep them, and confirm this in writing;
9. show compliance on request, and allow audits by the Client or its auditor on [[10]] Business Days' notice;
10. tell the Client at once if it believes an instruction breaks Data Protection Law;
11. not transfer the data outside [[country or region]] without the Client's written consent and the safeguards Data Protection Law requires; and
12. not use the data for its own purposes, such as marketing or training AI models.
