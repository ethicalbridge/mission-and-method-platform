# Purpose and scope

These procedures explain how [[Organisation name]] handles money day to day, for all our funds, accounts, cash, mobile money and assets. Our Financial Controls Policy sets the rules and our Delegation of Authority Matrix sets who approves what. Our Procurement Policy and Expenses and Per Diem Policy cover buying, travel, per diems and allowances. This manual tells you what to do, in what order. Where a grant agreement sets stricter rules, we follow them for that funder's money.

**Words we use.** A **payment voucher** is the cover sheet for all documents of one payment. **Reconciliation** means checking that our records match the bank, mobile money or cash balance, and explaining any difference. **Restricted funds** may only be used for the purpose a funder or donor set.

::: adapt Accounting software or spreadsheets
**Accounting software** is worth it once you have several grants or more than about [[50]] transactions a month: it gives an audit trail, grant codes and user roles. Set rights so that the person who enters payments cannot approve them, and give the treasurer read-only access. **Spreadsheets** can work for a very small organisation: keep one cashbook per account, save a final numbered version each month and never edit a closed month. Either way, use the same codes: account, fund or grant, and budget line.
:::

# Compensating controls for very small teams

Separation of duties means no one person controls a transaction from start to finish. Where our team is too small for this, we add checks:

{widths: 45 55}
| If one person must… | …we add this check |
|---|---|
| request and approve a payment | The treasurer reviews every such payment within [[5 working days]] |
| enter and reconcile bank payments | The treasurer receives statements directly from the bank and reviews them line by line each month |
| approve payments and hold the bank token | Bank alerts for every payment go to the treasurer |
| hold and record petty cash | Monthly counts with a second person, and surprise counts at least [[twice a year]] |
| prepare and approve payroll | The treasurer approves the payroll summary before payment |

Nobody approves a payment to themselves. Every [[quarter]], our accountant checks a sample of transactions and the board sees all payments above [[USD 500]].

# The procedures

## 1. Annual budget preparation and approval

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Agree income and spending before the year starts, so every payment has an approved budget line. |
| Who | Director leads; budget holders draft; finance lead consolidates; treasurer reviews; board approves. |
| When | Start [[3 months]] before the financial year ([[1 January to 31 December]]); approved before it starts. |
| Records | Budget versions, assumptions, cash flow forecast, board minute. |

1. The director sends budget holders a timetable and assumptions: salary changes, inflation, exchange rates and expected funding.
2. Budget holders draft budgets from last year's actual spending, signed grant budgets and planned activities.
3. The finance lead combines them, showing income by funder and status (confirmed, likely, possible), costs by line, each grant's contribution to core costs, any planned addition to reserves under our Reserves Policy, and a [[12-month]] cash flow forecast.
4. The director and treasurer review the draft at least [[6 weeks]] before the year starts. Spending is budgeted only against confirmed income, unless the board agrees a plan for the gap.
5. The board approves the budget in a minute. The finance lead loads it, with codes, and sends budget holders their budgets.
6. At [[mid-year]] the finance lead reforecasts. Changes above [[10%]] of a line, or spending outside the budget, are approved under the Delegation of Authority Matrix, and by the funder where required. A change of more than [[10%]] to the total budget goes to the board, as our Governing Body Terms of Reference require.

## 2. Raising and approving a payment

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Make sure every payment is genuine, budgeted, approved within limits and documented. |
| Who | Requester; approver named in the Delegation of Authority Matrix; finance lead; second signatory. |
| When | Approval before we commit. Suppliers paid within [[30 days]] of a correct invoice. |
| Records | Payment request form (Annex A), quotes, order or contract, delivery note, invoice, proof of payment. |

1. **Request.** The requester completes a payment request form (Annex A) and attaches the quotes our Procurement Policy requires.
2. **Check.** The finance lead checks the budget, the procurement process and the supplier checks.
3. **Approve.** The approver signs or approves in the system, within these illustrative limits:
  - up to [[USD 500]]: budget holder, with the finance lead's check before payment;
  - [[USD 501]] to [[USD 5,000]]: director, with the finance lead;
  - [[USD 5,001]] to [[USD 20,000]]: director, with second approval by the treasurer;
  - above [[USD 20,000]]: the board, recorded in a minute.
4. **Order and receive.** Above [[USD 500]], we issue a purchase order or contract before delivery, as our Procurement Policy requires. Someone other than the approver, where possible, signs the delivery note.
5. **Match.** The finance lead matches order, delivery note and invoice. The invoice must be in our name, and not a copy or a statement.
6. **Pay** under procedure 3. Mark every document "PAID" with the date and reference, so it cannot be paid twice, and file it.

Nobody approves a payment they requested, or one to themselves or a close associate. Never split a purchase to stay under a limit.

## 3. Bank and mobile money payments with two approvers

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Make sure no single person can move our money. |
| Who | Finance lead enters ("maker"); a second signatory or approver authorises ("checker"). |
| When | Payment runs on [[Tuesdays and Fridays]]; urgent payments the same day, still with two people. |
| Records | Voucher packs, payment run list, bank or mobile money confirmations, monthly statements. |

1. **New payees.** The account must be in the payee's own name. Confirm any new or changed details by phone, on a number we already hold, never one given in the email.
2. **Enter.** The finance lead enters each payment, quoting the voucher number as the reference.
3. **Authorise.** The second approver checks each voucher pack, confirms amount and payee, and authorises. If anything is missing, they reject it.
4. **Bulk mobile money.** The second approver checks the file total and a sample of names and numbers against the approved list before authorising.
5. **Confirm.** Save confirmations. Follow up failed transactions within [[2 working days]], and never resend until the first is confirmed as failed.
6. **Own-account transfers** and **cheques** also need two people. Never sign a blank cheque.

Tokens, PINs and passwords are never shared. When an approver is away, the deputy in the Delegation of Authority Matrix acts. When a signatory leaves, the chair asks the bank to remove them within [[5 working days]] and reports it to the board, as our Delegation of Authority Matrix allows. Only the board adds signatories.

::: adapt Bank and mobile money set-up
Ask your bank for dual authorisation: one user enters, another approves. If your bank or mobile money provider allows only one user, add payment alerts to the treasurer, a low daily limit and a weekly statement check. Ask mobile money providers about business accounts with a separate approver role. List accounts and signatories in a board minute, not here.
:::

## 4. Petty cash

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Pay small everyday costs safely, with evidence for every payment. |
| Who | Holder [[Name, role]]; approver [[Name, role]]; second counter; treasurer or director. |
| When | Top up when the float falls to about [[USD 100]], or monthly. Count monthly; surprise counts at least [[twice a year]]. |
| Records | Vouchers and receipts, petty cash book, signed reconciliation sheets (Annex B). |

1. **Float.** The float is fixed at [[USD 300]], in a locked box kept in a safe or locked drawer. Only the holder has the key.
2. **Pay out.** Single payments are no more than [[USD 50]]. Each needs a voucher (Annex B) signed by the recipient and approved by [[Name, role]], with the receipt attached, recorded in the petty cash book the same day.
3. **Not allowed:** IOUs, personal loans, cashing personal cheques, or splitting one purchase across vouchers.
4. **Top up.** The finance lead checks the vouchers and the approver authorises a top-up equal to the amount spent, returning the float to [[USD 300]] (an imprest system).
5. **Count.** At month end the holder and a second person count the cash and both sign the reconciliation sheet. Cash plus vouchers must equal the float. The treasurer or director also counts without notice at least [[twice a year]], and whenever the holder changes.
6. **Differences** above [[USD 5]] are reported to the director the same day and handled under procedure 15.

## 5. Cash and in-kind payments to participants

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Pay transport refunds, allowances or in-kind support fairly, safely and with evidence. |
| Who | Activity lead; a second staff member; a witness who is not paying out; finance lead. |
| When | Rates agreed before the activity; reconciled within [[3 working days]] after it. |
| Records | Attendance list, participant payment list (Financial Controls Policy, Annex B), cash issue record, distribution list. |

1. **Plan.** Confirm rates under section 11 of our Expenses and Per Diem Policy, and the expected number of people. Pay by mobile money where possible.
2. **Draw cash** through procedure 2, against a signed cash issue record. Carry no more than [[amount]], split between two people where safe.
3. **Pay.** Two staff are present. Each person is paid individually, as privately as possible, and signs or thumbprints the list beside their name and amount.
4. **Witness.** A witness who is not paying, ideally a partner or community representative, countersigns the list.
5. **In kind.** For food, kits, vouchers or airtime, record the item and quantity per person on a signed or thumbprinted distribution list, and keep a stock record.
6. **Children** are never paid cash. Allowances go to a parent or guardian, or are given in kind.
7. **Reconcile.** Return unspent cash within [[1 working day]]. The finance lead checks that lists, totals and returned cash match. Lists are kept securely under our Data Protection Policy.

::: adapt Cash-heavy contexts
Where banking and mobile money are limited, cash may be unavoidable. Plan cash movements, vary times and routes, never announce when cash will arrive, and move it with two people. Ask your insurer about cover for cash in transit and held overnight. Consider mobile money agents, local banks or partners instead. Some funders limit cash payments or must approve rates: check each agreement. Our Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy covers personal safety.
:::

## 6. Receiving income and donations

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Make sure all income reaches our accounts, is recorded and is coded to the right fund. |
| Who | Finance lead; anyone who receives cash or cheques; director for unusual donations. |
| When | Cash and cheques banked within [[3 working days]]; all income recorded within [[5 working days]]. |
| Records | Receipt copies, paying-in slips, grants register, donor correspondence and checks. |

1. **Our accounts only.** All income goes into accounts in the name of [[Organisation name]], never a personal account.
2. **Cash and cheques.** Two people count cash and sign the count sheet. Issue a receipt from a pre-numbered book or our system and keep the copy. Bank within [[3 working days]].
3. **Transfers.** The finance lead checks accounts at least [[weekly]] and records each receipt, matching grant instalments to the grants register.
4. **Code** every receipt as restricted or unrestricted, with its grant or fund code. If the donor names a purpose, the gift is restricted. If unsure, ask the director.
5. **Check donors.** Large, unusual or anonymous gifts are checked under our Gift Acceptance Policy. Issue receipts where the donor or the law requires.
6. **In-kind gifts** are recorded at fair value where our accounting rules require.
7. **Unidentified money** is held in a suspense code and investigated within [[10 working days]]. Refunds go only to the account the money came from, with the director's approval. A request to refund an "accidental" overpayment is a common fraud warning sign.

::: adapt Donation receipts and tax
Check your local law on donation receipts. In some countries only organisations with a special tax approval can issue receipts that give donors tax relief, with set wording or a registration number. Ask your accountant whether sales income must be invoiced with tax and counts towards a VAT or sales tax threshold. Warn your bank about large foreign grants: it may ask for the agreement before releasing funds.
:::

## 7. Payroll

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Pay the right people the right amount on time, and pay tax and contributions on time. |
| Who | Director authorises changes; finance lead or provider prepares; second person reviews; treasurer approves the director's pay. |
| When | Changes by the [[20th]]; pay on the [[last working day]]; statutory deductions by the legal deadline. |
| Records | Contracts, change forms, timesheets, payroll report, payslips, tax and contribution returns. |

1. **Master list.** The finance lead keeps each person's pay rate, account details and deductions, matching their contract.
2. **Changes.** Starters, leavers, pay changes, new bank details and deductions are made only on a written change form approved under the Delegation of Authority Matrix. Nobody approves a change to their own pay.
3. **Timesheets** for staff charged to grants are approved by their manager by month end.
4. **Prepare and review.** The finance lead or provider calculates pay and deductions. A second person compares it with last month, matching every difference to a change form and every name to the staff list.
5. **Approve and pay.** The director approves, and the treasurer approves the director's own pay. Pay by transfer or mobile money under procedure 3, and give everyone a payslip.
6. **Pay the authorities.** Pay income tax, social security and other statutory deductions by the legal deadline, and file the returns. Late payment can bring penalties and interest.

::: adapt Payroll and tax obligations
Check your local law with your accountant or tax authority: employer registration; income tax withholding; social security, pension, health and other contributions; monthly and annual deadlines; payslip contents; staff tax certificates; and withholding on payments to individual consultants. Add each deadline to the calendars. A payroll provider does not remove your duty to check deductions were paid.
:::

## 8. Advances and their reconciliation

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Give staff funds for activities and travel, and account for every unit. |
| Who | Staff member; approver under the Delegation of Authority Matrix; finance lead. |
| When | Request at least [[5 working days]] before; reconcile within [[5 working days]] of the end of the activity or trip. |
| Records | Advance request, advance register, reconciliation with receipts and lists, proof of returned cash. |

1. The staff member requests the advance with a cost breakdown and budget line. Advances to the director are approved by the treasurer.
2. The finance lead checks no earlier advance is outstanding, pays it, preferably by transfer or mobile money, and records it in the advance register.
3. Within [[5 working days]] of the end of the activity or trip, the staff member submits receipts and participant lists, and returns unspent money.
4. The finance lead checks the reconciliation within [[5 working days]] and pays any approved overspend.
5. If it is late, the finance lead reminds the staff member and tells the director at [[15 days]]. After [[30 days]], our Expenses and Per Diem Policy applies, including any deduction the law and the contract allow.

## 9. Monthly close

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Check our records are complete and correct, and give leaders timely information. |
| Who | Finance lead prepares; budget holders explain variances; treasurer reviews. |
| When | By the [[10th]] working day of the next month; treasurer review within [[5 working days]]. |
| Records | Signed reconciliations (Annex C), cash counts, budget versus actual, forecast, checklist (Annex D). |

1. **Complete the books:** all payments, receipts, petty cash, payroll and charges, with codes.
2. **Reconcile** every bank and mobile money account using Annex C, explaining every difference.
3. **Count** petty cash and any field floats (procedure 4), and **review** the advance register (procedure 8).
4. **Budget versus actual.** Report by budget and by grant. Budget holders explain variances above [[10%]] within [[3 working days]].
5. **Cash flow.** Update the forecast for the next [[6 months]] and flag any month where cash may run short.
6. **Summary.** Send the director and treasurer a short note on cash, spending and problems.
7. **Treasurer review.** The treasurer checks each reconciliation against the statement received directly from the bank, queries anything unusual and signs it.
8. **Lock** the month in the software, or save a final numbered spreadsheet.

Every [[quarter]] these figures become management accounts for the board, as our Financial Controls Policy sets out.

## 10. Grant and restricted fund tracking and donor financial reporting

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Spend restricted funds only as agreed, and report to funders accurately and on time. |
| Who | Finance lead tracks and drafts; director checks and signs; treasurer reviews final reports. |
| When | Set up within [[5 working days]] of signing; reviewed monthly; reports by each deadline. |
| Records | Grants register, agreements and amendments, grant ledger, allocation workings, reports, funder approvals. |

1. **Set up.** Create a grant code, load the grant budget and record in the grants register: amount, currency, period, instalments, eligible costs, budget flexibility, procurement, receipt and exchange rate rules, audit and report dates.
2. **Code and allocate.** Every cost carries its grant code and budget line. Shared costs are allocated by the method in our Financial Controls Policy.
3. **Monitor** grant spending monthly and flag underspends and overspends early.
4. **Get approval first.** The director approves any move between grant budget lines, as our Delegation of Authority Matrix sets out. Moving money beyond the funder's flexibility limit also needs the funder's written approval before spending.
5. **Report.** Prepare each financial report from the accounts, reconciled to the grant ledger. The director checks it against the narrative report and signs it. Submit on time and file it with the workings.
6. **Partners.** Release sub-grant funds in instalments, review partners' financial reports against the Sub-grant Agreement and check a sample of their documents.
7. **Close out.** Unspent funds are returned, carried forward or reallocated only as the agreement says.

::: adapt Funders' financial rules
Some funders require a separate bank account, receipts stamped with the grant name, a set exchange rate, a project audit or their own report format. Some disallow costs paid outside the grant period or without the required quotes. Record each agreement's finance clauses in the grants register when you sign, and follow the stricter rule.
:::

## 11. Foreign currency and exchange rates

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Record foreign currency correctly and treat exchange differences consistently. |
| Who | Finance lead; director for exceptions. |
| When | At each conversion, each month end and each grant report. |
| Records | Bank conversion advices, exchange rate log, report workings. |

1. We keep our accounts in [[reporting currency]].
2. For each conversion, record the rate actually received, with the bank advice, in an exchange rate log.
3. For funder reports, use the method the agreement requires. If none, use [[the actual rate of each conversion / a weighted average of the conversions for that grant]], as our Financial Controls Policy sets, and say so in the report.
4. Record exchange gains and losses separately, by grant where they arise on grant funds.
5. Use informal money changers only if there is no alternative and the director approves in writing. Record the rate, reason and a signed receipt.

## 12. Fixed assets register and disposal

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Know what we own, where it is and who uses it, and dispose of it properly. |
| Who | Finance lead keeps the register; users look after assets; director and board approve disposals. |
| When | Added within [[5 working days]] of purchase; checked at least [[once a year]]. |
| Records | Assets register, handover forms, check sheets, disposal approvals. |

1. **Add** every item over [[USD 300]], and all laptops, phones and vehicles, to the register: description, serial number, date, cost, funder, location, user and reference.
2. **Label** it with our name and reference. The user signs a handover form.
3. **Check** every asset against the register at least [[once a year]], by someone other than the user.
4. **Report loss or theft** within [[24 hours]] under our Incident Reporting Procedure, with a police report for theft.
5. **Dispose.** Check the funder's rules first. Write-offs up to [[USD 500]] need the director and treasurer; above that, the board. Sell openly at a fair price, never privately to staff or board members. Wipe devices under our IT and Information Security Policy.

## 13. Year-end and audit or independent examination

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Close the year correctly and give the auditor or examiner what they need. |
| Who | Finance lead prepares; accountant supports; treasurer liaises with the auditor; board approves. |
| When | Draft accounts within [[3 months]] of year end; approved within [[6 months]]; filed by [[deadline]]. |
| Records | Year-end file, trial balance, accounts, audit queries, management letter, board minute. |

1. **Before year end**, confirm the auditor or examiner the board appointed and agree a timetable.
2. **At year end**, count cash, get statements for the last day, check the assets register, list money owed to and by us, and list fund balances, checked against grant reports.
3. **Complete payroll year end**: annual returns and staff tax certificates.
4. **Prepare the audit file**: trial balance, reconciliations, registers, board minutes, grant agreements, major contracts and payroll summaries. Answer queries within [[5 working days]].
5. **Approve and follow up.** The board approves the accounts and reviews the management letter. Each point gets an action, owner and date, and the treasurer reports progress until all are closed.
6. **File** the accounts with [[regulator / tax authority / registry]] and send them to funders who ask.

::: adapt Year-end deadlines and audit
Check your local law on when accounts, tax returns and annual reports must be filed, and with whom. Whether you need a full audit or a lighter independent examination depends on your country, income and governing document; some funders also require a project audit. Book your auditor early.
:::

## 14. Document filing and retention

{widths: 18 82}
| Purpose | Find any document quickly, prove every transaction and keep records as long as required. |
| Who | Finance lead; everyone who hands in receipts. |
| When | Filed within [[5 working days]] of payment; kept at least [[7 years]]. |
| Records | Paper and digital files, filing index, backup log. |

1. **Number** each payment voucher, for example [[PV-2026-001]], and use that number in the system and the bank reference.
2. **Keep one pack per payment**, holding every document for it, filed by month and account.
3. **Scan** each pack to [[our shared drive / accounting system]], named [[YYYY-MM_voucher number_payee]].
4. **Store** originals in a locked cabinet. Back up digital records at least [[weekly]].
5. **Keep** records for at least [[7 years]] after the financial year ends, or longer where a funder or the law requires (see our Records Retention Schedule). Limit access to payroll and participant records under our Data Protection Policy.
6. **Destroy** securely, only after checking that no grant, audit or dispute requires longer.

## 15. When something goes wrong

Report mistakes quickly. Never delete or overwrite a record: correct it with a new, explained entry.

{widths: 24 46 15 15}
| Problem | What to do | Who | Time limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lost receipt | Ask the supplier for a copy. If impossible, complete a missing receipt declaration under our Expenses and Per Diem Policy. | Person who paid | [[5 working days]] |
| Payment error | Ask the bank or provider to recall it, contact the recipient, record it in the error log. | Finance lead | Same day |
| Error in the accounts | Correct it with an explained journal entry, approved by a second person. | Finance lead | Next close |
| Cash difference | Recount with a second person, check vouchers, report to the director. | Petty cash holder | Same day |
| Email asking to change bank details or pay urgently | Do not pay. Call the sender on a number we hold. Report it. | Anyone | At once |
| Lost or stolen cash or asset | Report under our Incident Reporting Procedure; report theft to the police. | Person responsible | [[24 hours]] |
| Suspected fraud, bribery or misuse of funds | Do not investigate or confront anyone. Report to the fraud lead, or the second contact, under our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure. | Anyone | At once |

The finance lead reviews the error log with the treasurer every [[quarter]]; repeated errors mean we fix the procedure too. Where a funder's money is affected, the director tells the funder within the time the agreement sets, often [[24–72 hours]] for suspected fraud.
