- **[[Name, role]] approves expenses and travel** within the limits in our Delegation of Authority Matrix.
- **[[Name, role, for example the board treasurer]] approves the expenses of the person above**, including the founder or executive head. Nobody approves their own claim.
- **[[Name, role]] checks and pays claims**, issues and reconciles advances and keeps the records.
- **We review this policy and our rates** at least once a year, when prices change significantly, and when a new funder sets different rules.

Name real people. The most important rule is that nobody approves their own expenses. In a very small team, the founder's claims are usually approved by a board member, who can review them monthly rather than one by one. ::: glance
- You should never be out of pocket for doing your work, and never gain from it either.
- Get approval before you spend or travel. Spend as if it were your own money.
- Keep a receipt for every expense. If you cannot get one, complete a missing receipt declaration.
- For each trip we pay either a per diem or actual costs, not both.
- Use economy travel and the safest reasonable option. Safety comes before cost.
- Settle advances within [[5 working days]] of returning, with receipts and any unspent cash.
- Allowances for participants are fair, fixed in advance, paid equally and recorded. They are never an inducement.
- A false or inflated claim is fraud.
:::

# Who this policy applies to

This policy applies to staff, volunteers, interns and board members of [[Organisation name]] who spend money or travel for our work. It applies to consultants only where their contract says we will reimburse expenses; otherwise their fee covers them. Section 11 covers allowances we pay to participants and community members who take part in our activities.

It covers all costs paid from our funds, whatever their source, including those charged to a grant.

**Law and stricter rules.** [[Organisation name]] is registered in [[country]] and follows its tax and employment laws. Where a funder's grant agreement sets stricter rules on expenses, travel or per diems, those rules apply to every cost charged to that grant. Where rules differ, we follow the stricter one.

::: adapt Tax and employment law
In many countries, per diems and allowances above a set amount, or paid without receipts, count as taxable income or a taxable benefit, and the employer may have to report them or deduct tax. Check your local law with your tax authority or accountant, and set your rates so that they stay within any tax-free limits where you can. In the UK, for example, HM Revenue and Customs publishes approved mileage and benchmark subsistence rates. Check also whether your employment law or employment contracts set any travel or subsistence entitlements.
:::

# Policy

## 1. Our principles

We reimburse expenses that are:

1. **necessary** for our work and could not reasonably be avoided;
2. **reasonable and economical**, at a cost a careful person would accept for themselves;
3. **approved** in advance, within the budget;
4. **supported by evidence**, normally an itemised receipt;
5. **claimed promptly**, within [[30 days]] of being incurred.

You should be neither better nor worse off because you spent money for us.

## 2. Allowable expenses

{widths: 30 70}
| Type of expense | What we pay |
|---|---|
| Transport | Economy air, rail or bus fares; taxis or ride-hailing where public transport is unsafe, unavailable or impractical; mileage for your own vehicle (section 9); parking, fuel and tolls for our vehicles |
| Accommodation | Safe, clean, standard accommodation up to the rate in section 7, or actual cost where approved |
| Meals and incidentals | Per diem at the rate in section 7, or actual reasonable costs up to the same limit |
| Visas and travel documents | Visa fees, required vaccinations and medicines, and travel insurance not already provided by us |
| Communications | Airtime, data or calls needed for work, where we do not provide a phone or allowance |
| Activity costs | Venue, materials or refreshments for an approved activity, following our Procurement Policy for larger amounts |
| Care costs | Reasonable extra childcare or care costs caused by overnight travel for work, where approved in advance |
| Other | Any other cost approved in writing before it is incurred |

::: adapt Care costs and inclusion
Decide whether to cover extra care costs caused by work travel. Many small organisations do, because otherwise people with caring responsibilities, often women, are excluded from travel and the opportunities it brings. Check whether your funders allow this cost. If they do not, consider covering it from unrestricted funds.
:::

## 3. Expenses we do not pay

- alcohol, tobacco and personal entertainment, such as films or minibar items;
- fines, including parking and traffic fines, and penalties for late cancellation that were avoidable;
- personal items, such as clothing, toiletries or souvenirs;
- upgrades to business or first class, or to more expensive accommodation, for personal preference;
- costs for family members or friends travelling with you;
- tips above [[10%]] or local custom;
- costs already covered by someone else, such as meals provided at a conference or by a host;
- gifts, except as allowed in our Gift Acceptance Policy and Code of Conduct;
- any payment that could be a bribe or facilitation payment. Our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure applies.

## 4. Approval before you spend or travel

- **Travel:** complete a travel request at least [[5 working days]] before you go, and [[14 days]] for international travel. It states the purpose, dates, itinerary, estimated costs and budget line. Travel also needs the safety approval set out in our Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy.
- **Other expenses:** get approval from [[Name, role]] before spending more than [[amount]].
- **Limits:** approval limits follow our Delegation of Authority Matrix.
- **Unplanned costs:** if an unexpected cost arises during a trip, such as a cancelled flight, ask for approval by phone or message if you can, and explain it in your claim. Safety always comes first: do not put yourself at risk to save money.

## 5. Receipts and exceptions

Keep an itemised receipt for every expense you claim, except costs covered by a per diem. A receipt shows the supplier, date, items and amount. Photographs or scans of receipts are accepted if they are clear. Mobile money and bank statements count as evidence of payment, not of what was bought, so add a short note.

We know that in many places small traders, motorcycle taxis or market sellers cannot give receipts. In that case:

- ask for a handwritten receipt with the trader's name, phone number, date and amount, if possible;
- otherwise, complete the missing receipt declaration in the claim form: what you bought, where, when, why there was no receipt and how much you paid;
- missing receipt declarations are limited to [[amount]] per claim and [[amount]] per month, unless [[Name, role]] agrees otherwise in writing.

::: adapt Receipts in your context
Set the missing receipt limit to reflect how your team actually travels. Where most local transport is informal, consider a fixed local transport allowance per day instead of individual claims. Check each funder's rules: some accept self-declarations up to a small amount, others require receipts for every cost, and some will not reimburse costs without them at all.
:::

## 6. Per diems or actual costs

A **per diem** is a fixed daily amount to cover meals and small incidental costs, such as drinking water or laundry, while you are away from your usual place of work. You do not need receipts for costs it covers. **Actual costs** means we reimburse what you really spent, with receipts, up to a limit.

- For each trip, the travel request states whether a per diem or actual costs will apply. Do not claim both for the same day or the same cost.
- A per diem applies only when you are away for more than [[a set number of hours, e.g. 6 hours]], or overnight, and travelling more than [[distance, e.g. 30 km]] from your usual place of work.
- Accommodation is normally paid directly by us or reimbursed at actual cost, not included in the per diem.
- If meals are provided, by us, a host or a conference, the per diem is reduced as shown in section 7.
- Partial days are paid at [[50%]] of the daily rate on the days you leave and return, unless you leave before [[time]] or return after [[time]].

## 8. Travel class and booking

- Book through [[our booking process / Name, role]], or yourself with approval, as early as possible to get the best fare.
- Use economy class for all air travel. Business class may be approved only for medical reasons or other reasons approved in writing by [[Name, role]].
- Use public transport where it is safe, reliable and practical. Choose safer options over cheaper ones when the risk justifies it. Our Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy sets out which vehicles and routes are allowed, for example after dark.
- Accept flexible or refundable fares where plans are likely to change and the extra cost is small.
- Loyalty points earned on work travel may be kept, but must never influence which airline or hotel you choose.

## 9. Mileage

If you use your own car or motorcycle for approved work travel, we pay [[amount]] per [[km / mile]] for a car and [[amount]] for a motorcycle. This rate covers fuel, wear and tear and insurance. Record each journey in a mileage log: date, from, to, purpose and distance. We do not pay mileage for your normal journey between home and your usual place of work.

You must hold a valid licence and insurance that covers business use, and follow the road safety rules in our Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy. We do not pay for fines or for damage to your vehicle unless agreed in writing.

::: adapt Mileage rates
Use your tax authority's published rate if there is one, so that payments stay tax free. In the UK, for example, HM Revenue and Customs sets approved mileage allowance payment rates, which rose from 45 to 55 pence per mile for a car's first 10,000 business miles from the 2026/27 tax year: check the current figure. Where there is no official rate, base yours on the local fuel price and typical fuel consumption, plus a margin for maintenance, and review it when fuel prices change.
:::

## 10. Advances and reconciliation

An advance is money paid to you before a trip or activity so that you do not have to use your own funds.

1. Request an advance on the travel request or activity budget, at least [[5 working days]] before you need it.
2. Advances are paid by bank transfer or mobile money wherever possible. Cash advances above [[amount]] need approval from [[Name, role]].
3. You are responsible for the advance until it is reconciled. Keep it safe, separate from your own money where you can.
4. Within [[5 working days]] of returning, submit a reconciliation with all receipts and return any unspent money. If you spent more than the advance, we pay the difference once the claim is approved.
5. We do not issue a new advance while a previous one is outstanding, unless [[Name, role]] agrees in writing.
6. If an advance is not reconciled within [[30 days]], we may, where the law and your contract allow, deduct the unreconciled amount from money we owe you, after telling you in writing.

::: adapt Deductions from pay
Check your local employment law before deducting unreconciled advances from salary. In many countries, deductions from wages need the employee's written agreement, or are limited by law. Include a clause in your Employment Contract or advance request form where needed.
:::

## 11. Allowances for participants and community members

We sometimes pay allowances to people who take part in our activities: community members attending a training, workshop, meeting or consultation; members of community committees; or people who give their time to research or evaluations. These payments cover real costs, such as transport, a meal or lost income from a day's work. They are not a reward for agreeing with us.

We follow these rules:

- **Fair and consistent.** We set rates in advance for each type of activity and pay everyone in the same situation the same amount, whatever their status or relationship with our staff.
- **Not an inducement.** Allowances are set at a level that covers costs, not one that encourages people to attend for the money, to give particular answers or to take part in research they would otherwise refuse. People who do not attend are not pressured or penalised. Where people take part in research, our Research Participation Consent Form explains what they will receive.
- **Aligned with others.** Where government or other organisations in the area have agreed common rates, we follow them, so that we do not distort local practice or undermine others' work.
- **In kind where better.** Where it is safer or fairer, we provide transport, meals or childcare directly instead of cash.
- **Safe to pay.** We pay by mobile money where possible. Where we pay cash, two people from our team are present, and we do not carry more than [[amount]].
- **Recorded.** Each person signs, or gives a thumbprint, on an attendance and payment sheet showing their name, the activity, the amount and the date. A staff member and a witness countersign it.
- **Children.** We do not pay cash to children. Any allowance for a child's participation is given to their parent or guardian, or in kind.

::: adapt Community allowances
Decide whether you pay participants at all, and for what. Practice varies widely: in some places a transport refund is expected and fair; in others, so-called sitting allowances have grown so large that people attend for the money rather than the activity. Ask local partners and other organisations what is normal and fair. Some funders do not allow cash allowances, or require their approval of the rates. Check also whether payments to participants have any tax or social benefit implications under your local law.
:::

## 12. Funders' rules

Many funders set their own rules on travel class, per diem rates, receipts and which costs they will pay. Before charging any expense to a grant, check the grant agreement. Where a funder will not pay a cost that this policy allows, it may be paid only from unrestricted funds with the approval of [[Name, role]].

## 13. Honest claims

Expense claims rely on trust. Claiming for costs you did not incur, inflating amounts, claiming the same cost twice, altering receipts, claiming a per diem when meals were provided, or recording participants who did not attend are all fraud. We treat them under our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure and our Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure, and we may report them to the authorities and recover the money. If you make a genuine mistake, tell [[Name, role]] as soon as you notice, and we will correct it.

# Roles and responsibilities

- **Claimants** spend economically, keep receipts, claim honestly and promptly, and reconcile advances on time.
- **Approvers** check that each cost was necessary, reasonable and within budget before approving. They never approve their own claims or claims from close family members.
- **Finance, [[Name, role]],** checks and pays claims, tracks advances and keeps the records.
- **Activity leads** prepare attendance and payment sheets for participant allowances and make sure they are signed and countersigned.
- **The board** approves this policy and the rates, approves the executive head's expenses through [[Name, role]], and receives a short summary of expenses and any exceptions at least [[once a year]].

# Records, confidentiality and data

We keep claims, receipts, travel requests, advance reconciliations and participant payment sheets for [[7 years]] after the end of the grant or financial year, or longer if a funder or the law requires it, as set out in our Records Retention Schedule. Participant payment sheets contain personal data: we store them securely, share them only with funders or auditors entitled to see them, and handle them under our Data Protection Policy.

# If this policy is breached

Costs that do not follow this policy may not be reimbursed, or may have to be repaid. Repeated or deliberate breaches are dealt with under our Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure. Suspected fraud is handled under our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure. Anyone can raise a concern through our Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure.

# Monitoring and review

[[Name, role]] reviews a sample of at least [[ten]] claims and all participant payment sheets every [[quarter]], and checks that advances are reconciled on time. We review this policy and our rates at least once a year, when prices change significantly, and when a funder's rules require it.
