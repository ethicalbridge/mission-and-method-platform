# Purpose

This register helps us to:

- spot risks early, before they become crises;
- decide which risks matter most and where to spend our limited time and money;
- give each risk an owner and a clear set of actions;
- show the board and our funders that risks are managed.

A **risk** is something uncertain that, if it happens, would affect our people, our mission, our money or our reputation. An **issue** is a risk that has already happened: we deal with it through our incident procedures, and then ask what the register should learn from it.

# How to use this register

## 1. Describe the risk

Write each risk in three parts:

- **Cause:** the situation or weakness that creates the risk.
- **Event:** what could happen.
- **Consequence:** the effect on people, mission, money or reputation.

For example: "Because 70% of our income comes from one funder (cause), if that funder does not renew its grant (event), we would have to cut programmes and staff, and could close (consequence)." Writing "funding" alone tells you nothing about what to control. Writing the cause tells you where to act.

## 2. Score it before controls

Use the scales below to score likelihood and impact as if no controls were in place. This is the **inherent** score.

## 3. List the controls

Write down what we already do that reduces the likelihood or the impact: policies, checks, insurance, reserves, training. Only list controls that actually work today.

## 4. Score it again after controls

Score the risk again with those controls in place. This is the **residual** score. If the residual score is above our appetite (see below), agree further actions, an owner and a due date.

## 5. Review and report

Review the register each quarter. Close actions that are done, rescore risks, add new ones and remove those that no longer apply. Report to the board as set out in the escalation rule.

# Scoring scales

## 6. Likelihood

{widths: 12 22 66}
| Score | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Could happen only in exceptional circumstances; not expected in the next [[5]] years |
| 2 | Unlikely | Could happen, but not expected in the next [[2–3]] years |
| 3 | Possible | Might happen in the next [[2]] years; has happened to similar organisations |
| 4 | Likely | Will probably happen in the next [[12 months]]; has happened to us before |
| 5 | Almost certain | Expected to happen in the next [[12 months]], or is starting to happen |

## 7. Impact

{widths: 12 18 70}
| Score | Level | Examples of the effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Minimal | Minor inconvenience; loss under [[1%]] of annual income; no harm to anyone; no external interest |
| 2 | Minor | Short delay to activities; loss of up to [[2 weeks']] income; minor complaint; quickly fixed |
| 3 | Moderate | An activity stops for weeks; loss of up to [[1 month's]] income; minor injury; local media or a funder raises concerns |
| 4 | Major | A programme stops or a key funder is lost; loss of up to [[3 months']] income; serious injury; regulator or funder investigation; national media |
| 5 | Severe | Serious harm to a person, closure of the organisation, or loss of more than [[three months']] income; loss of registration; criminal investigation |

## 8. Score and rating

Score = likelihood × impact.

{widths: 20 20 60}
| Score | Rating | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| 15–25 | Very high | Act now. Report to the chair within [[5 working days]] and to the board. Board agrees the response. |
| 10–14 | High | Owner agrees actions with the [[Executive Director / founder]]. Reported to every board meeting as a top risk. |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Manage through normal controls. Review each quarter. |
| 1–4 | Low | Accept and monitor. Review each year. |

::: adapt Scales and amounts
Change the amounts and time periods so they suit your budget. Expressing financial impact as weeks or months of income works for most small organisations, because it shows how long you could survive. Keep the scales the same for at least a year, so that scores can be compared over time.
:::

# Risk appetite statement

**Risk appetite** is the amount and type of risk we are willing to accept to achieve our mission. The board sets it and reviews it each year.

{widths: 30 18 52}
| Area | Appetite | What this means |
|---|---|---|
| Safeguarding and the safety of people | Low | We will not accept avoidable risk of harm to the people we serve, staff or volunteers. We stop an activity if we cannot make it safe. |
| Fraud, bribery and corruption | Low | Zero tolerance. We put strong controls in place even when they cost time. |
| Legal and regulatory compliance | Low | We meet the law and our registration conditions. Where the law is unclear, we take advice and choose the cautious option. |
| Data protection and security | Low | We collect only what we need and protect it. |
| Financial sustainability | Moderate | We accept some uncertainty in income, but keep reserves as set out in our Reserves Policy. |
| Reputation | Moderate | We speak up on our issues, accepting some criticism, but never at the expense of the people we serve. |
| Programmes and innovation | Higher | We are willing to test new approaches, partners and places where the potential benefit is high and people are kept safe. |

Where our appetite is low, we aim to bring the residual score to [[8]] or less. If a risk in a low-appetite area stays above [[8]] after controls, the board reviews it at every meeting until it falls.

::: adapt Setting your appetite
The board should discuss and agree this statement, not just approve it. Keep safeguarding, fraud and legal integrity at low appetite: funders and regulators expect it. You may set a higher appetite for growth, advocacy or new programmes, depending on your strategy. Some risks with severe impact, such as harm to a child, keep an impact of 5 however good the controls are: controls mainly reduce the likelihood, so the residual score rarely falls very low. That is why the board keeps them under close review.
:::

# Escalation rule

- Any risk with a residual score of [[15]] or more: the owner tells the [[Executive Director / founder]] at once, and the [[Executive Director / founder]] tells the chair within [[5 working days]]. It goes on the agenda of the next board meeting, or an extra meeting if it cannot wait.
- Any risk in a low-appetite area with a residual score above [[8]]: reported to every board meeting.
- Any actual incident involving safeguarding, fraud, a data breach or a legal breach is reported under the relevant policy straight away, whatever its score in this register.
- Any new risk that could score [[15]] or more is added to the register and reported in the same way, without waiting for the quarterly review.

# Review cycle

{widths: 25 40 35}
| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Each quarter | Update scores, controls and actions; add and remove risks | Register keeper with risk owners |
| Each board meeting | Review top risks (residual 10+) and any low-appetite risks above [[8]] | Board |
| Each year | Review the whole register and the risk appetite statement; test the "if it happens" plans | Board, with the [[Executive Director / founder]] |
| After any serious incident or major change | Add or rescore the risks concerned; record lessons learned | Register keeper and the owner |

Keep each year's approved version so you can show how risks changed over time.

# If it happens: plans for our top three risks

For each of our top three risks we keep a one-page plan, so that the right people act quickly and nothing is forgotten in a crisis. The examples below cover three risks most small organisations share. Replace them with your own top three.

::: adapt Choosing your top three
Choose the three risks with the highest residual scores, or the three that would do the most harm if they happened. Keep each plan to one page, store it where the team and the board can find it offline, and test it once a year with a 30-minute "what if" discussion.
:::

## 9. Plan 1: Serious safeguarding incident

**Lead:** [[Safeguarding lead]]. **If the concern involves the lead or the founder:** [[Name, safeguarding lead board member / independent contact]].

{widths: 22 78}
| When | What we do |
|---|---|
| Immediately | Make the person safe. Call emergency services on [[number]] if anyone is in danger. Get medical help where needed. |
| Within [[24 hours]] | Record the facts under the Safeguarding Policy and Procedure. Tell the chair. Decide on protective steps, such as suspending the person concerned from contact. Preserve evidence. |
| Within [[72 hours]] | Report to the authorities where required, and to funders within the time their agreements set. Agree who speaks to the media: only [[Name]]. |
| Within [[1 month]] | Arrange an independent investigation where needed. Offer support to the survivor. Review what failed and update the register. |

## 10. Plan 2: Sudden loss of a major funder or cash crisis

**Lead:** [[Executive Director / founder]] with the treasurer.

{widths: 22 78}
| When | What we do |
|---|---|
| Within [[1 week]] | Update the cash forecast for the next [[13 weeks]]. Tell the chair. Pause non-essential spending and new commitments. |
| Within [[2 weeks]] | Board meeting to agree options: use of reserves, reduced programme, talks with other funders, bridging finance. Talk to the funder: ask about a no-cost extension or a phased exit. |
| Within [[1 month]] | Tell staff honestly and early, following employment law on any changes to contracts. Tell partners and communities what will change and when. |
| Ongoing | Protect the people we serve: finish or hand over activities responsibly, never leave them without notice. |

## 11. Plan 3: Founder or key person suddenly unable to work

**Lead:** [[Chair]], with [[Name, deputy]].

{widths: 22 78}
| When | What we do |
|---|---|
| Within [[48 hours]] | Chair names an acting lead. Access to bank accounts, email and key systems through the emergency access arrangements. |
| Within [[1 week]] | Tell funders and key partners who to contact. Check deadlines for reports, payroll, filings and grant renewals for the next [[3 months]]. |
| Within [[1 month]] | Board decides on interim arrangements and support for the founder. Agree a plan to share relationships and knowledge more widely. |
