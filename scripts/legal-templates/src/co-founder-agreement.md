::: adapt Before you use this agreement
**Use it** when two to four people start an organisation together, ideally before you register it, raise money or hire anyone. Fill it in together: the conversation matters as much as the document.

**Choose your structure and delete the other option.** This template covers both:

- **Option A: a company with shares**, such as a social enterprise company, community interest company, benefit corporation or co-operative with share capital. Founders own shares, which can vest, be sold or be bought back.
- **Option B: a non-profit with no shares**, such as an association, foundation, trust, non-profit company limited by guarantee or public benefit organisation. Nobody owns it. Founders hold governance seats and staff or volunteer roles, and the assets belong to the organisation and its purpose.

**Key legal risks.** Your constitution (articles of association, memorandum of incorporation, statutes, bylaws or trust deed) is the legally binding rulebook and usually prevails over this agreement. In a company, a formal shareholders' agreement, and changes to the articles, may be legally needed to make share transfers, vesting and buy-backs enforceable. Receiving shares can trigger tax. Non-compete clauses are unenforceable in some places. Rules for non-profits often limit founder control and founder pay.

**Get local review** from a company or non-profit lawyer before you sign, and make sure the constitution matches what you agree here.
:::

# Background

1. The Founders have come together to create [[Organisation name]] to [[one-line mission, for example "help smallholder farmers in East Africa earn a fair income from climate-resilient crops"]].
2. The Founders want to record how they will work together, make decisions, share [[ownership / governance roles]] and handle changes, so that the Organisation and its mission are protected whatever happens.
3. The Founders intend this agreement to be legally binding between them. Where it conflicts with the constitution or with mandatory law, the constitution or the law prevails, and the Founders will vote to bring the constitution into line with this agreement where the law allows.

# Agreed terms

## 1. Definitions

1. In this agreement:
  1. **"Board"** means the governing body of the Organisation, whatever it is called.
  2. **"Constitution"** means the Organisation's [[articles of association / statutes / bylaws / trust deed]], as amended.
  3. **"Founder"** means each person named as a Founder above, and anyone who joins under clause 13.
  4. **"Leaving Date"** means the date a Founder stops working in the Organisation in their Founder role, for any reason.
  5. **"Organisation Assets"** means everything used for the Organisation's work, including its name, brand, logo, domain names, websites, social media accounts, email accounts, bank accounts, data, documents, software, methods and relationships with funders and partners.
  6. **"Reserved Matters"** means the decisions listed in clause 4.3.
  7. **"Shares"** (Option A only) means shares in the Organisation, and **"Fair Value"** means their value as agreed by the Founders or, if they cannot agree within [[30 days]], as set by an independent accountant appointed under clause 16.
2. Words in the singular include the plural. "Including" means "including without limitation".

## 2. Purpose, values and mission lock

1. The purpose of the Organisation is [[purpose, matching the objects in the Constitution]].
2. The Founders share these values: [[for example: local leadership, honesty, accountability to the communities we serve, environmental care]].
3. The Founders will each act in good faith, in the best interests of the Organisation and its purpose, and in line with the Code of Conduct.
4. The Founders will not change the purpose, or remove any mission or asset protection from the Constitution, without the unanimous written agreement of all Founders [[and the approval of the Board]].
5. **Option A:** the Founders agree that at least [[percentage, for example 51%]] of distributable profits will be reinvested in the purpose or used for [[community benefit]], and that the Constitution will say so.
6. **Option B:** the Organisation's income and assets will be used only for its purpose. No Founder will receive any profit, dividend or share of assets, except reasonable pay for work done or reimbursement of expenses as allowed by clause 12 and the law.

::: adapt Choosing a mission lock
A promise between founders is weak protection if one of them sells their shares or the board changes. Put the lock in the Constitution as well. Examples of legal forms with built-in protection: the UK community interest company has an asset lock overseen by the CIC Regulator; US benefit corporations must pursue a stated public benefit under state law; India's Section 8 company (Companies Act 2013) bars dividends; South Africa's non-profit company under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 cannot distribute profits. Ordinary companies, such as Argentina's simplified joint-stock company (SAS, Law 27.349 of 2017), have no built-in lock, so write one into the statutes. Co-operatives follow their own co-operative laws. Check your local law on social enterprise forms, which change often.
:::

## 3. Roles and time commitment

1. Each Founder's role, responsibilities, location and time commitment are set out in Schedule 1.
2. Each Founder will give the time stated in Schedule 1. A Founder who wants to reduce their time for more than [[two months]] will discuss it with the other Founders first, and the Founders will agree any changes to roles, pay or [[vesting / governance seats]] in writing.
3. Founders will be honest with each other about their capacity, health, financial pressures and other commitments that affect the work.
4. The Founders will meet at least [[monthly]] to review progress, and at least [[once a year]] to review this agreement under clause 19.
5. Where a Founder is also employed or contracted by the Organisation, that relationship is governed by a separate Employment Contract or Independent Contractor Agreement, approved under clause 4.

## 4. Decision-making

1. Each Founder makes day-to-day decisions within their role in Schedule 1 and within the limits of the Delegation of Authority Matrix.
2. Decisions outside one Founder's role, and not listed below, are made by a **simple majority** of Founders [[or of the Board]]. Each Founder has one vote. [[Option A: or by a majority of votes attached to Shares, where the Constitution requires.]]
3. The following **Reserved Matters** need the **unanimous** written agreement of all Founders:
  1. changing the purpose, values, mission lock or Constitution;
  2. issuing new Shares or options, or admitting a new Founder (Option A), or appointing or removing a Board member (Option B, where the Founders control this);
  3. borrowing more than [[amount]], or giving any guarantee or security;
  4. buying, selling or licensing any major Organisation Asset, including the name, brand or core intellectual property;
  5. entering a single agreement worth more than [[amount]] or lasting more than [[two years]];
  6. setting or changing the pay of any Founder, or any transaction between the Organisation and a Founder or their relatives;
  7. merging, converting, closing or dissolving the Organisation, or starting insolvency proceedings; and
  8. [[any other matter the Founders choose]].
4. A Founder with a conflict of interest declares it and does not vote on that matter, following the Conflict of Interest Policy. Where the conflicted Founder's vote would be needed for unanimity, the remaining Founders decide unanimously.
5. Decisions are recorded in writing, by minutes or by email confirmed by all voting Founders, and kept in [[location]].

## 5. Deadlock

1. A deadlock exists when the Founders cannot reach a decision needed for the Organisation to continue its work after discussing it at [[two]] meetings held at least [[seven days]] apart.
2. The Founders will then:
  1. each write down their position and the reasons for it in no more than one page, within [[five days]];
  2. ask [[a trusted adviser, mentor or board chair agreed in advance: name]] to facilitate a discussion within [[14 days]]; and
  3. if there is still no agreement, refer the matter to mediation under clause 18.
3. While a deadlock continues, the Organisation continues its current activities and budget, and no Founder takes any Reserved Matter decision alone.
4. [[Optional, for two Founders only: if a deadlock on a fundamental matter is not resolved within 90 days of mediation, either Founder may offer to buy the other's Shares at a stated price; the other must either sell at that price or buy at that price within 30 days.]]

::: adapt Decisions and deadlock
List the matters that really need everyone's agreement: too many and nothing gets decided; too few and one founder can be outvoted on things that matter. With two founders, every disagreement can become a deadlock, so the facilitator and mediation steps matter. The optional buy-sell offer (sometimes called a "shotgun" clause) can resolve deadlock in a company, but it favours the founder with more money: use it with care, and it does not work in a non-profit. In a non-profit (Option B), the Board, not the Founders, usually holds final authority under law, so the Founders' vote may only be a recommendation to the Board.
:::

## 6. Option A: Shares and equity split

1. The Shares are held as set out in Schedule 2. The split reflects the Founders' agreed view of each person's past and expected future work, ideas, money, skills, networks and risk.
2. All Shares held by Founders are subject to vesting under clause 7 and to the leaver terms in clause 15, unless Schedule 2 says otherwise.
3. No Founder may sell, transfer, pledge or give away any Shares without the written consent of the other Founders, except as this agreement or the Constitution allows. Founders who wish to sell must first offer their Shares to the other Founders in proportion to their holdings, at Fair Value.
4. If the Organisation raises investment, Founders' holdings may be diluted. New investors will be asked to respect the mission lock in clause 2.
5. [[If you set up an employee or community share or option pool: the Founders agree to reserve percentage of Shares for a pool for staff, partners or community members.]]

## 7. Option A: Vesting

1. Each Founder's Shares vest over [[four years]] from [[the Vesting Start Date in Schedule 2]], as follows:
  1. no Shares vest during the first [[12 months]] (the "cliff");
  2. on the first anniversary, [[25%]] of the Founder's Shares vest; and
  3. the remaining Shares vest in equal [[monthly / quarterly]] instalments over the following [[36 months]].
2. "Vesting" means the Founder's right to keep the Shares if they leave. Until Shares vest, they can be bought back under clause 16.
3. While a Founder remains a Founder, they hold all their Shares, vested or not, and can vote and receive any dividends on them, subject to the Constitution.
4. [[Optional acceleration: if the Organisation is sold or merged and the Founder's role ends within 12 months as a result, all their Shares vest immediately.]]

::: adapt Equity, vesting and tax
A four-year vesting period with a one-year cliff is common practice in start-ups, not a legal rule. Social enterprises often choose three years, or vesting linked to milestones. An equal split is simple but not always fair: discuss time, risk and money honestly. Vesting usually works by "reverse vesting": founders own the shares from day one, and the company or other founders can buy back unvested shares. This may require provisions in the articles or a shareholders' agreement to be enforceable. **Tax can be significant.** Receiving shares for work may be taxed as income, and vesting can create a taxable event. Examples: in the US, founders often file a section 83(b) election within 30 days of receiving restricted stock; in the UK, a joint section 431 election under the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 is usually made within 14 days. Ask a local tax adviser before you issue shares.
:::

## 8. Option B: Governance seats and founder roles

1. The Organisation has no shares and no owners. The Founders hold the following roles:
  1. [[Founder 1]]: [[member of the Board and executive director]];
  2. [[Founder 2]]: [[member of the Board and programme lead]];
  3. [[Founder 3]]: [[member of the Board, unpaid]].
2. Each Founder is a [[member of the association / director of the non-profit company / trustee]] under the Constitution, with the rights and duties the law gives that role.
3. The Founders will appoint at least [[two]] independent Board members, who are not Founders, employees or relatives of Founders, within [[12 / 24]] months, so that the Board can oversee the Founders fairly.
4. A Founder's Board seat lasts for the term in the Constitution and may be renewed. It is not property and cannot be sold or transferred.
5. Founders who are paid staff report to the Board for their staff role, and step out of Board decisions about their own pay, performance or contract.

::: adapt Governance seats in a non-profit
In a non-profit, founders do not own the organisation, and regulators expect an independent board. Many countries limit paying board members: for example, in England and Wales charity trustees are generally unpaid unless the governing document, the Charity Commission or the law allows it. US tax-exempt organisations must avoid private benefit and excessive pay to insiders. Kenya's Public Benefit Organizations Act 2013 was brought into force in 2024 and changes the registration and governance rules for many NGOs: check the current regulations. In India, trusts, societies and Section 8 companies have different governance rules; in Brazil, associations and foundations follow the Civil Code; in Nigeria, incorporated trustees are registered under the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020. Decide early how founders who are staff will be held accountable.
:::

## 9. Intellectual property

1. Each Founder assigns to the Organisation all intellectual property they have created, or create, for the Organisation or its work, including its name, brand, logo, website, content, methods, software, data and materials. For anything created before the Organisation was registered, this assignment takes effect on registration.
2. Each Founder lists in Schedule 3 any pre-existing materials they bring and will keep. Each Founder grants the Organisation a [[free, non-exclusive, perpetual, worldwide]] licence to use those materials for its work.
3. Where the law allows, each Founder waives their moral rights in work assigned to the Organisation.
4. Each Founder will sign any document needed to register or confirm the Organisation's rights.
5. No Founder will register any trademark, domain name or social media handle similar to the Organisation's name in their own name.

::: adapt Intellectual property and early work
Founders often create the name, logo, website and methods before the organisation exists. Until there is an entity, those rights usually belong to whoever created them, so a written assignment on registration matters. Some countries require an assignment of copyright to be in writing and signed. Moral rights cannot be waived in some countries, such as France, Brazil and Argentina. Register the name as a trademark in the Organisation's name in your main countries if the brand is valuable.
:::

## 10. Confidentiality

1. Each Founder will keep confidential all non-public information about the Organisation, its finances, funders, partners, staff and the people it serves, during and after their time as a Founder.
2. This does not stop a Founder from disclosing information when the law requires it, raising a concern under the Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure or with a regulator, or taking legal or financial advice.
3. Personal data is handled under the Data Protection Policy.

## 11. Non-solicitation and outside activities

1. While a Founder, and for [[12 months]] after their Leaving Date, a Founder will not:
  1. persuade any employee, volunteer or contractor of the Organisation to leave it; or
  2. persuade any funder, partner or client of the Organisation to stop or reduce its support, or to move it to another organisation in which the Founder is involved.
2. Founders may have other paid or unpaid work and interests, provided they declare them in Schedule 1, give the time agreed, and avoid conflicts under the Conflict of Interest Policy.
3. A Founder will not start or join an organisation doing substantially the same work in [[the same countries]] while they are a Founder, without the written agreement of the other Founders.
4. [[Optional and subject to local law: for 12 months after their Leaving Date, a Founder who held Shares will not set up or work for a direct competitor in named countries.]]

::: adapt Non-compete and non-solicit clauses
Enforceability varies widely. Many courts enforce restrictions only if they protect a legitimate interest, are limited in time, place and scope, and are no wider than necessary. California voids most non-competes, with limited exceptions for people who sell their ownership in a business. In the UK, reforms to limit non-competes have been proposed; check the current position. Several civil-law countries require the former partner or employee to be paid during the restricted period. In the social sector, a broad non-compete may also harm the communities you serve. A narrow non-solicitation clause, like the one above, is more likely to be enforced and is usually enough.
:::

## 12. Money: investment, loans, pay and expenses

1. Each Founder will put the following money or assets into the Organisation: [[amounts, items and dates, or "None"]]. These are recorded in Schedule 3.
2. **Option A:** money put in is [[paid for Shares / a loan / a gift]], as recorded in Schedule 3. **Option B:** money put in is [[a gift / a loan]]; it does not give the Founder any ownership or extra vote.
3. A loan from a Founder is made on written terms approved under clause 4, stating the amount, interest (if any) and repayment. Loans are repaid only when the Organisation can afford it and after its obligations to staff, tax authorities and funders are met, unless agreed otherwise.
4. Founders will be paid as follows: [[no Founder is paid until the Organisation has secured funding of amount / named Founders are paid salary or fees set out in Schedule 1]]. Any pay is reviewed [[once a year]] and approved under clause 4.3 [[and by the independent Board members]].
5. Founders are reimbursed for reasonable expenses, at cost and with receipts, under the Expenses and Per Diem Policy.
6. No Founder may commit the Organisation to spending above [[amount]] without approval under the Delegation of Authority Matrix. All Organisation money is held in accounts in the Organisation's name, with [[two]] authorised signatories.

::: adapt Money between founders and the organisation
Write down every amount a founder puts in, and whether it is a gift, a loan or payment for shares. Unrecorded money is a common cause of disputes. Founder loans to non-profits can create conflicts of interest, so the independent board members should approve them. In many countries, paying founders of a non-profit is allowed for real work at a reasonable rate, but must be approved by those without a conflict and disclosed in the accounts. Payments to founders may also need tax and social security treatment as employment income.
:::

## 13. Adding new co-founders

1. A new Founder may join only with the unanimous written agreement of the existing Founders.
2. The new Founder must sign a deed of adherence agreeing to be bound by this agreement, and the Founders will update Schedules 1 to 3.
3. **Option A:** any Shares issued to the new Founder are subject to vesting under clause 7, from the date they join. **Option B:** the new Founder's governance seat, if any, follows the Constitution.

## 14. Leaving: notice and handover

1. A Founder who wishes to leave will give the other Founders at least [[three months']] written notice, unless they agree a shorter period.
2. During notice, the leaving Founder will hand over their work, knowledge, contacts, passwords and documents in an orderly way, following a handover plan agreed with the other Founders within [[14 days]] of notice.
3. A Founder who leaves resigns from any Board seat, office or bank mandate they hold because of their Founder role, unless the remaining Founders and the Board ask them to stay.
4. If a Founder dies or becomes permanently unable to work, the other Founders will treat their family or representatives with care and apply the Good Leaver terms.
5. The Founders may agree, by unanimous decision of the others, to remove a Founder who is a Bad Leaver, after giving them written reasons and a fair chance to respond.

## 15. Good leaver and bad leaver

1. A **Good Leaver** is a Founder who leaves because of death, serious ill health or disability, caring responsibilities, removal without cause, or any other reason the other Founders agree in writing.
2. A **Bad Leaver** is a Founder who:
  1. leaves within [[12 months]] of [[the Vesting Start Date (Option A) / the date of this agreement (Option B)]] for a reason not listed in sub-clause 1;
  2. is removed after committing fraud, theft, sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment, or another serious breach of the Code of Conduct;
  3. commits a serious or repeated breach of this agreement and does not put it right within [[30 days]] of written notice; or
  4. materially breaches clause 10 or clause 11.
3. Any Founder who is neither a Good Leaver nor a Bad Leaver is treated as a Good Leaver, unless the other Founders agree otherwise.

## 16. After leaving: buy-back of Shares (Option A) or end of roles (Option B)

1. **Option A:** when a Founder leaves, the Organisation or the remaining Founders may buy their Shares, by written notice within [[90 days]] of the Leaving Date, as follows:
  1. a Good Leaver keeps their vested Shares, and sells their unvested Shares at [[the lower of Fair Value and the price paid / nominal value]];
  2. a Bad Leaver sells all their Shares, vested and unvested, at [[the lower of Fair Value and the price paid]].
2. Payment is made within [[60 days]] of the price being agreed or set, or in instalments over up to [[24 months]] if the Organisation cannot afford it at once.
3. If the Founders cannot agree Fair Value within [[30 days]], an independent accountant agreed by them, or appointed by [[the professional accounting body in country]], will set it. Their decision is final, except for obvious error, and the Organisation pays their fee.
4. **Option B:** a leaving Founder's governance seat, staff role and any authority to act for the Organisation end on the Leaving Date, unless the Board decides otherwise. They have no claim to any assets, funds or reserves of the Organisation. Any loan they made is repaid on its terms.

::: adapt Leaver terms and valuation
These terms decide what a departing founder walks away with, so agree them while you are all committed. Courts in some countries refuse to enforce terms they see as penalties, for example a forced sale of vested shares at a very low price, so a Bad Leaver price at the lower of Fair Value and cost is usually safer than nominal value. A buy-back of shares by the company itself is regulated by company law in most countries, and may need specific approvals and enough distributable reserves. Check your local law on share buy-backs. In a non-profit, there is nothing to buy back: the issue is a fair handover and recognition of the leaving founder's work.
:::

## 17. Name, brand, domains and accounts

1. All Organisation Assets belong to the Organisation, even if registered or paid for by a Founder. Where any is in a Founder's name, that Founder holds it for the Organisation and will transfer it on request.
2. The Organisation keeps a register of its Organisation Assets in Schedule 3, including who holds the login, recovery email and two-factor device for each account. At least [[two]] Founders or staff have admin access to every critical account.
3. On their Leaving Date, a leaving Founder will transfer all logins, admin rights, domain registrations and recovery details to the person the remaining Founders name, and will not change passwords, delete content or redirect domains.
4. A leaving Founder may describe their role accurately in their CV and public profile, but may not use the Organisation's name or brand to imply they still represent it.
5. If the Organisation is dissolved, its name and brand pass with its other assets under the Constitution and the law, and no Founder may use them without the agreement of the others.

## 18. Disputes

1. If a dispute arises under this agreement, the Founders will first talk to each other openly and try to resolve it within [[14 days]].
2. If that fails, they will try mediation by [[a named mediator or mediation service, for example the national mediation centre]], sharing the cost equally, within [[30 days]].
3. If mediation does not resolve the dispute within [[60 days]], any Founder may refer it to [[the courts of country / arbitration under the rules of named institution, with one arbitrator, seated in city]].
4. Nothing in this clause stops a Founder from seeking urgent court orders to protect confidential information, intellectual property or Organisation Assets.

## 19. Review of this agreement

1. The Founders will review this agreement at least [[once a year]], and whenever a Founder joins or leaves, the Organisation changes structure, or it raises significant investment.
2. Changes are valid only if made in writing and signed by all Founders.

## 20. General

1. This agreement, with its schedules and the Constitution, is the entire agreement between the Founders about its subject.
2. If any part of this agreement is invalid, the rest continues, and the Founders will replace the invalid part with a valid one as close as possible to their intention.
3. This agreement is governed by the law of [[country where the Organisation is registered]].
4. This agreement may be signed in counterparts and electronically.

::: adapt Governing law and making it binding
Choose the law of the country where the Organisation is registered, so that this agreement and the Constitution follow the same law. Founders living in different countries should take advice in each. Some countries require shareholders' agreements to be notarised, registered or consistent with the constitution: for example, in South Africa a shareholders' agreement must be consistent with the company's memorandum of incorporation. Check your local law on the formalities.
:::
