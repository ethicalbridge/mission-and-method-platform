::: adapt Before you use this form
**Electronic marketing rules differ widely.** Fundraising appeals and campaign updates count as direct marketing in many laws. Examples:

- **EU:** the ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC), applied through each country's national law, generally requires prior consent for marketing emails and texts to individuals.
- **UK:** the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR) require consent for marketing emails and texts, with a "soft opt-in" exception for existing customers. Since 5 February 2026, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 also gives charities a "charitable purpose soft opt-in" for people who gave their details while supporting or showing interest in the charity's purposes, if offered a simple opt-out then and in every message: check the ICO's guidance on its conditions. Marketing calls must be screened against the Telephone Preference Service unless the person has consented.
- **US:** the CAN-SPAM Act 2003 allows marketing email without prior consent but requires a working unsubscribe; texts and automated calls fall under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act 1991, which often requires prior written consent.
- **Canada:** Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) requires consent for most commercial electronic messages, with an exemption for registered charities whose message's main purpose is raising funds.

Many other countries apply their general data protection law, for example the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019, Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, POPIA in South Africa (which has specific rules on unsolicited electronic direct marketing) or Brazil's LGPD. Check your local law on electronic marketing. Using separate, unticked opt-in boxes for every channel, as this form does, meets the strictest common standard.

**Language and literacy.** Offer the form in your supporters' languages. If you take consent by phone, read the wording and record the date, time and who took it.
:::

::: adapt Channels and frequency
Only list channels you will really use, and frequencies you will keep to. Supporters trust a promise of "four appeals a year" when you stick to it. For WhatsApp, check the platform's business messaging rules, which require opt-in before you message people; never add supporters to groups where others can see their numbers. Use a broadcast list or channel instead. If you also contact people by post or phone on the basis of legitimate interests rather than consent, where your law allows, explain this in your Privacy Notice and give an easy way to opt out.
:::

::: adapt Tax relief for donors
Tax relief rules depend entirely on your country and registration. Examples: **UK:** Gift Aid lets registered charities claim back basic-rate tax on donations from UK taxpayers who have made a valid declaration; use HMRC's model wording. **US:** donors need a written acknowledgment from the charity for any single gift of USD 250 or more to claim a deduction. **South Africa:** approved public benefit organisations issue section 18A receipts. **India:** eligible organisations issue receipts that let donors claim a deduction (section 80G of the Income-tax Act 1961; the Income-tax Act 2025, in force from 1 April 2026, renumbers these rules, so check the current section). Many countries give no tax relief for donations, or only to certain registered bodies: check your local tax law, and never promise relief you cannot give.
:::

::: adapt Recurring payments
Follow your payment provider's and bank's rules, for example the Direct Debit Guarantee in the UK, or card scheme rules on recurring payments. Never collect or store full card numbers yourself: use a provider that meets the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS). For mobile money, check your provider's rules on standing payments and refunds. Say how you handle refunds of payments taken in error.
:::

# Annex: Guidance for staff and volunteers collecting consent

- **Never pre-tick boxes**, and never assume a donation means consent to marketing. Each channel needs its own clear "yes".
- **Record the proof**: date, how consent was given, the form version and who took it. Enter it in [[database]] within [[5 working days]], then store or securely destroy the paper form.
- **On the phone**, read the channel options aloud word for word and record the answer. Do not call people who have asked not to be called.
- **At events**, keep forms out of sight of other people, and never leave completed forms unattended.
- **Respect "stop" immediately.** Flag the record the same day, across all systems and lists.
- **Take extra care with people in vulnerable circumstances.** If someone seems confused, under pressure or unable to understand, do not take a donation or consent. Follow up later, or not at all. Never ask people for a donation while they are receiving help from us.
- **Refresh consent** every [[2 years]], or when you add a new type of message.
- **Check suppression lists** before every campaign, such as unsubscribes, deceased supporters and [[national do-not-call register, if any]].
