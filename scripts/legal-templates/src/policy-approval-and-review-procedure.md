[[Organisation name]] is a [[small organisation of X people / start-up run by its founders]]. We have [[number]] policies and procedures, and the same few people write, approve and use them. This procedure keeps that simple. Until we grow:

- **[[Name, role]] is our policy coordinator.** They keep the policy register and the policy folder, track review dates, and collect acknowledgements.
- **Each document has an owner**, named in the register, who drafts it, keeps it up to date and answers questions on it.
- **The board** approves policies. **The director** approves procedures, forms and guidance, as our Delegation of Authority Matrix sets out.
- **We review this procedure** every [[two years]], and when our governing document or the Delegation of Authority Matrix changes.

Name real people. In a team of two to five, the director is usually the policy coordinator and owns most documents. That is fine, but give at least the finance and safeguarding documents to someone else where you can, so that they are not all checked by one person. A board member, often the secretary, can keep an eye on the register and raise overdue reviews at board meetings.

# Purpose and scope

This procedure explains how [[Organisation name]] creates, approves, shares, stores, reviews and retires its governing documents. It applies to:

{widths: 25 75}
| Document type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Policy | Sets out what we commit to and the rules we follow, for example our Financial Controls Policy. A **core policy** is one the board, a funder or the law treats as essential, such as safeguarding, finance, fraud and data protection. |
| Procedure | Sets out how we put a policy into practice, step by step, for example our Finance Procedures. |
| Code | Sets standards of behaviour, for example our Code of Conduct. |
| Governance document | Sets how we are governed, for example our Governing Body Terms of Reference and Delegation of Authority Matrix. |
| Form, template or guidance | Supports a policy or procedure, for example a consent form or a contract template. |

It does not cover our governing document (constitution, articles or statute), which can only be changed as that document and the law allow.

# The policy lifecycle

Every document passes through five stages:

1. **Draft and consult.** The owner drafts the document from a template or the current version, consults the people it affects, and gets advice where needed.
2. **Approve.** The right person or body approves it, and the decision is recorded.
3. **Communicate and acknowledge.** Everyone it applies to receives it, understands it and confirms they have read it. Training is given where needed.
4. **Monitor.** The owner checks that it is followed and works in practice.
5. **Review.** At the review date, or earlier if something changes, the owner reviews it, and the cycle starts again.

# 1. Deciding which policies we need

The director and policy coordinator decide which documents we need, and in what order, by looking at:

- **the law** in each country where we are registered or work;
- **funders and partners**, whose due diligence and grant agreements often list required policies;
- **our risks**, as recorded in our Risk Register;
- **our activities**, for example work with children, cash payments, research or handling personal data.

Each new document is added to the policy register with an owner and a target date before drafting starts. We prefer a few documents that people use over many that nobody reads.

::: adapt Which documents to start with
Most small organisations start with: Code of Conduct; Safeguarding Policy and Procedure; Financial Controls Policy; Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure; Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure; Conflict of Interest Policy; Data Protection Policy; Complaints and Feedback Procedure; and Delegation of Authority Matrix. Add others as your work, team and funding grow. Check your grant agreements: some funders require a specific policy before the first payment. Use the Legal Review Checklist to spot documents the law requires where you work.
:::

# 2. Drafting

1. The owner starts from a reliable template, such as [[template source]], or from the current version.
2. They adapt every placeholder and adapt box to our country, size, activities and funders. A template copied without changes is a warning sign for funders and auditors.
3. They check that the draft matches our other documents: the same roles, amounts and time limits as the Delegation of Authority Matrix, Financial Controls Policy and Code of Conduct.
4. They write in plain language, in [[language(s)]], and plan translations for the people who need them.
5. They mark the draft "DRAFT", with a version number such as 0.1, and complete Part 1 of the policy approval form (Annex A).

# 3. Consulting

The owner consults the people a document affects before it is approved, and gives them at least [[10 working days]] to respond.

- **Staff and volunteers** are consulted on every policy and procedure that affects their work.
- **The people we serve** are consulted, where relevant, on documents that directly affect them, such as safeguarding, complaints and feedback, consent forms and participant allowances.
- **Partners** are consulted where a document changes what we expect of them.
- **Board members** with relevant skills may comment on drafts before the formal decision.

The owner records who was consulted, the main comments and how they were dealt with, in Part 2 of Annex A.

::: adapt Consulting the people we serve
Keep it proportionate and safe. Options: a short discussion at an existing community meeting; testing a complaints poster or consent form with a few participants; asking a partner organisation or community advisory group for comments; or a child-friendly session for documents about children. Explain how comments were used. Never ask people to share their own experiences of harm in a consultation, and follow your Safeguarding Policy and Procedure when working with children.
:::

# 4. Getting advice

Before approval, the owner decides whether the document needs outside advice. It usually does when it:

- creates legal obligations or rights, for example contracts, employment rules or data protection;
- sets duties to report to authorities, for example safeguarding or fraud;
- is new to us, or responds to a new law, regulator or funder requirement.

The owner uses our Legal Review Checklist to prepare questions for [[our lawyer / a pro bono adviser]], and asks [[our accountant]] about finance and tax documents and [[a safeguarding adviser]] about safeguarding documents. They record the advice and changes made in Annex A.

# 5. Approval

{widths: 30 25 45}
| Document | Approved by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Core policies, codes and all other policies | Board | At a quorate meeting or by written resolution, under our Governing Body Terms of Reference |
| Governance documents (Governing Body Terms of Reference, Delegation of Authority Matrix, Risk Register) | Board | Reserved to the board |
| Procedures, including this one | Director | Must follow board policies; reported to the next board meeting |
| Forms, templates and guidance | Director | Or a manager the director names in writing |
| Minor updates to a policy (names, contacts, job titles, links, typing errors) | Director | Reported to the next board meeting; no change to meaning |

For board approval:

1. The owner sends the final draft, the completed approval form and a one-page summary of changes to the secretary at least [[10 days]] before the meeting, so that papers reach members [[7 days]] before.
2. The board discusses the document and approves it, approves it with changes, or sends it back. Members with a conflict of interest declare it.
3. The minutes record the decision, using the wording in Annex B, with the version number and next review date.
4. The chair, or the director for procedures, signs the approval form. The document takes effect on [[the date of approval / a date the board sets]].

::: adapt Approval levels
Check your governing document first: it may require members or a general assembly to approve some documents, or name who signs. Make this table match your Delegation of Authority Matrix exactly. Some funders require board approval of safeguarding or anti-fraud policies, and some ask to see the board minute. If your board meets rarely, use a written resolution for urgent approvals, if your governing document allows it.
:::

# 6. Version control and document records

Every approved document has:

- an "About this document" page showing the owner, who approved it, the version, the approval date and the next review date;
- a document history table listing every version;
- a version number: **0.1, 0.2** for drafts; **1.0** for the first approved version; **1.1, 1.2** for minor updates; **2.0** for a major revision approved again in full;
- a file name in this format: **[[Org short name]]_[[Document title]]_v[[number]]_[[YYYY-MM]]**, for example [[ORG_Code-of-Conduct_v2.0_2026-03]].

The owner records every change in the change log (Annex C). The policy coordinator updates the register on the day of approval.

# 7. Communicating, acknowledging and training

Within [[10 working days]] of approval:

1. The policy coordinator places the approved version in the policy folder and removes the old one.
2. The owner tells everyone it applies to, with a short summary of what has changed and why.
3. Each person confirms they have read it, using the acknowledgement statement in the document, a signed form or an online form. The policy coordinator records the date in [[the register / an acknowledgement log]].
4. Where a document changes how people work, the owner gives a short briefing or training session within [[one month]].

New staff, volunteers and board members read and acknowledge the core documents in their [[first week]]. Everyone re-confirms the Code of Conduct, safeguarding and anti-fraud documents every [[year]]. Contractors and partners confirm through their contract.

We make documents accessible: plain-language summaries, translations and spoken briefings for people with limited literacy. We publish documents the public needs, such as our Safeguarding Policy and Procedure, Complaints and Feedback Procedure, Privacy Notice and Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure, on [[our website]].

# 8. Storage: one source of truth

- All current approved documents are kept in one place: [[the "Policies – current" folder on our shared drive]]. This is the only official version.
- Approved versions are saved as PDF, read-only for most users. Editable masters are kept separately, and only owners and the policy coordinator can change them.
- Printed copies are marked "Uncontrolled when printed: check the policy folder for the current version".
- Field offices and partners receive links, not copies, wherever connectivity allows.

# 9. Monitoring and review

**Monitoring.** Owners check that their documents work in practice, using incidents, complaints, audit findings, questions from staff and feedback from the people we serve. They raise problems with the director at any time.

**Scheduled review.** Every document is reviewed by its next review date, at least every [[one to two years]]. Core policies are reviewed [[every year]]. The policy coordinator reminds owners [[two months]] before the date.

**Triggered review.** The owner reviews a document straight away, without waiting for the date, after:

- a serious incident, complaint or near miss that the document should have prevented or handled better;
- an audit, evaluation or funder finding;
- a change in the law or regulation where we are registered or work;
- a new funder or grant agreement that requires different rules;
- a significant change in our activities, structure, countries or team.

**Outcome of a review.** If nothing needs to change, the approver confirms the document and sets a new review date, recorded in the register. Minor updates follow the director's route in section 5. Any change to meaning goes back through the full lifecycle.

::: adapt How often to review
Annual review of every document is too much for most small teams. A common pattern is: core policies every year; other policies and procedures every two years; forms and templates when the related policy changes. Funders sometimes require an annual review of safeguarding and anti-fraud policies, so check your agreements. A review that changes nothing still counts, as long as it is recorded.
:::

# 10. Retiring old versions

When a new version is approved, or a document is withdrawn:

1. The policy coordinator moves the old version to [[the "Policies – archive" folder]], marked "SUPERSEDED on [[date]]".
2. They remove it from the website and shared links, and ask anyone holding printed copies to destroy them.
3. They update the register to show the status "Superseded" or "Withdrawn", and the reason.
4. We keep archived versions as our Records Retention Schedule sets out, because we may need to show which rules applied at a given time, for example in a dispute or an audit.

# 11. Partners

Partners who deliver work with our funds must have policies that meet our standards on, at least, safeguarding, fraud and financial management, and data protection where they handle personal data. We check this through due diligence under our Partner Due Diligence and Sanctions Screening Policy. Where a partner has no suitable policy, they may adopt ours for the project, or develop their own with our support within an agreed time. The Sub-grant Agreement or Partnership Memorandum of Understanding sets out which policies apply. We tell partners when we change a policy that affects them.

::: adapt Working with partners
Be proportionate and respectful. Small local partners may have strong practice but few written documents. Offer templates, time and support rather than only demanding paperwork, and accept a partner's own policy where it meets the same standard. Where a funder requires its rules to flow down to partners, say so in the agreement.
:::

# Roles and responsibilities

{widths: 25 75}
| Who | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Board | Approves policies and governance documents; receives the annual report on policies; makes sure reviews happen. |
| Director | Approves procedures, forms and guidance and minor policy updates; decides which documents we need. |
| Policy coordinator | Keeps the register, the policy folder and acknowledgement records; reminds owners of reviews; archives old versions. |
| Document owners | Draft, consult, get advice, brief people, monitor and review their documents. |
| Everyone | Reads and follows the documents that apply to them, uses only the current version, and suggests improvements. |

# Records

We keep the register, approval forms, consultation notes, advice, board minutes, acknowledgements and archived versions as our Records Retention Schedule sets out. Acknowledgement and training records contain personal data and are handled under our Data Protection Policy.

# Monitoring and review of this procedure

Each year the director reports to the board on the number of documents current, overdue and in draft, acknowledgement and training rates, and any triggered reviews. The director approves this procedure, and the board notes it, because it describes how the board approves policies. It is reviewed every [[two years]].
