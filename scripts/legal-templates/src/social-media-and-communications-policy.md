# Who this policy applies to

This policy applies to everyone who works with or for [[Organisation name]], including staff, volunteers, interns, board members, contractors and consultants. It covers:

- our **official accounts and channels**: social media, website, blog, newsletter, WhatsApp channels and groups, podcasts and video channels;
- **public communications** for us: media interviews, public speaking, press releases and printed materials;
- your **personal accounts**, when you mention [[Organisation name]], our work, the people we serve or our partners, or when your posts could reasonably be linked to us.

It applies on every platform, including new ones, and wherever you are. If a funder's or partner's communications rules are stricter, for example on branding or approval, follow theirs on their projects.

**Law.** [[Organisation name]] is registered in [[country]] and follows its laws, and the law of other countries where we work, including on data protection, defamation, copyright, advertising and elections. Where rules differ, we follow the one that gives people more protection.

# Our policy

## 1. Official accounts belong to the organisation

- Every official account is registered to an organisational email address, such as [[social@yourorganisation.org]], never to a personal email or phone number.
- Each account has **at least two administrators**, one of whom is [[the director / a board member]].
- Passwords are long, unique and kept in our password manager, [[name of tool]]. Two-step verification is switched on for every account.
- The communications lead keeps a list of all accounts, administrators and recovery details, and reviews it [[every quarter]].
- When someone leaves, their access is removed on their last day, and shared passwords are changed.
- We do not open new official accounts, pages or groups without the communications lead's approval. Project or country accounts follow the same rules.

::: adapt Platforms and security
List the platforms you use, for example Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, TikTok, YouTube or a WhatsApp channel. Delete those you do not use: an abandoned account can be hijacked and used to impersonate you. Check that your password manager is approved in your IT and Information Security Policy. Some platforms let you add people as page roles without sharing passwords; use that where possible.
:::

## 2. Who can post, and who approves

| Type of content | Who can post | Approval before publishing |
|---|---|---|
| Routine updates: events, news, sharing partners' posts, thank-yous | Communications lead and [[authorised people]] | Not needed, but check against Annex A |
| Stories or images of the people we serve | Communications lead | Second person checks consent and safeguarding |
| Statements on public issues, campaigns or policy | Communications lead | Director |
| Responses to a complaint, a crisis or criticism of us | Spokesperson | Director, and board chair if serious |
| Paid advertising and fundraising appeals | Communications lead | [[Director / fundraising lead]] |

Anyone can suggest a post. Send it to the communications lead with the photos, consent forms and any facts that need checking.

::: adapt Approval levels
Match approval to your risk. A two-person team may only need a second check for stories about people, statements on public issues and crisis responses. If you work on sensitive topics, such as gender-based violence, migration, LGBTQI+ rights or conflict, consider a second check on every post. Agree a target, for example approval within [[1 working day]], so the process does not stall your communications.
:::

## 3. Tone, voice and brand

- We are **honest, warm, clear and respectful**. We write in plain language and avoid jargon.
- We describe results accurately. We do not exaggerate impact, claim credit for others' work, or present estimates as facts.
- We credit partners, communities and photographers, and we use people's own words.
- We use our logo, colours and fonts as set out in [[our brand guidelines]], and funders' logos as their agreements require.
- We only use images, music, text and videos we have the right to use, and we credit them. Something being online does not mean it is free to use.
- We do not post anything abusive, discriminatory, sexually explicit, or that mocks any group, in line with our Code of Conduct and Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy.

## 4. Ethical storytelling and consent

People own their stories. We tell them in a way the person would recognise and be comfortable with.

- We use a person's image, voice or story only with their informed consent, recorded on the Photo and Story Consent Form, and only for the uses they agreed to.
- For children, we need a parent's or guardian's consent and the child's own agreement.
- We show people as capable and active, not helpless. We avoid images of suffering used to provoke pity, and never use images of people in distress without careful judgement and consent.
- We never offer help, money or services in exchange for a photo or a story.
- We check that consent has not expired or been withdrawn before we reuse an image.

## 5. Safeguarding online

- We **never post identifiable images of children or adults at risk** where this could put them in danger, for example survivors of violence, children in care, refugees fleeing persecution or people whose health status is private.
- We never post a child's full name with their image, or details that show where they live, study or meet.
- We **switch off location tagging** and remove location data from images before posting. We post about events and visits after they happen, unless a public announcement is needed.
- We check backgrounds for house numbers, school names, signs, vehicle plates, documents or screens that could identify people or places.
- We do not use personal accounts to contact the people we serve, and we never send private messages to children through social media, as set out in our Code of Conduct.
- If anyone sees content that may put someone at risk, including posts by others that identify the people we serve, they tell the safeguarding lead the same day.

## 6. Accessibility

- Add **alt text** (a short written description) to every image.
- Add **captions** to videos, and a transcript for audio where possible.
- Use clear fonts, good colour contrast, and capital letters at the start of each word in hashtags, such as #CleanWaterNow, so screen readers can read them.
- Do not put important information only in an image. Write it in the post as well.
- Post in [[languages]] used by the communities we serve, where possible.

::: adapt Accessibility and language
Many organisations follow the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) for their website, and some countries require it for public or publicly funded bodies: check your local law on digital accessibility. Choose languages that reach the communities you serve, not only funders. Voice notes and short videos often reach more people than text in low-literacy settings.
:::

## 7. AI-generated and edited content

- We **never create or use AI-generated or altered images, video or audio that show real people**, or that pretend to show the people we serve, our activities or events.
- We do not use AI to create "typical" beneficiaries, fake testimonials or quotes.
- If we use AI for illustrations, icons or abstract graphics, we label them as AI-generated.
- We may use AI tools to help draft text, but a person checks every fact, and we never put personal or confidential information into AI tools that are not approved under our IT and Information Security Policy.
- We only edit photos for basic corrections such as cropping, brightness or blurring faces to protect identity. We never edit them to change what happened.

## 8. Accuracy and misinformation

- We check facts, figures and sources before posting. We link to the original source where we can.
- We do not share unverified claims, rumours or content from unknown sources, especially during emergencies, elections or health outbreaks.
- If we post something wrong, we correct it openly and quickly. We do not silently delete it unless it puts someone at risk.
- If false information about us circulates, the communications lead tells the director the same day, and we decide together whether and how to respond, following Crisis communications below.

## 9. Political activity and elections

[[Organisation name]] may speak up on issues linked to our mission, such as [[examples, e.g. children's rights, access to clean water]]. We do this based on evidence and the views of the communities we serve.

- We are **non-partisan**. We never support or oppose a political party or candidate, and we never ask people to vote for or against one.
- We do not share or "like" party political content on official accounts.
- During election periods, the director approves every post on public policy issues.
- If you stand for election or hold a political role, tell the director. Keep your political activity separate from your work and do not use our name, logo or contacts.

::: adapt Political activity and campaigning law
Rules on political activity by non-profits vary a lot. Examples: in England and Wales, the Charity Commission's guidance on campaigning and political activity (CC9) allows campaigning that furthers a charity's purposes, but never party political support. The UK's Transparency of Lobbying, Non-Party Campaigning and Trade Union Administration Act 2014 (often called the Lobbying Act) regulates spending on campaigning before elections. In the US, 501(c)(3) organisations are prohibited from intervening in political campaigns for or against candidates. In several countries, NGO laws restrict political or advocacy activity, especially by organisations with foreign funding. Check your local law on NGO political activity and on election-period rules before campaigning, and check your funders' conditions on advocacy.
:::

## 10. Personal accounts of staff and volunteers

We respect your right to a private life and to free expression. When you use personal accounts:

- **Say your views are your own** if you mention your work or comment on our field, for example "Views are my own" in your profile.
- **Never share confidential information**: about the people we serve, colleagues, partners, funders, negotiations or internal matters.
- **Never post images or stories of the people we serve** on personal accounts, unless we have shared them first on our official accounts and you share our post.
- **Do not speak for [[Organisation name]]**, or create accounts, pages or groups that look official, without approval.
- **Do not connect with or follow the people we serve** on personal accounts, especially children, unless you knew them before. If they contact you, tell your manager.
- **Behave as you would at work.** Harassment, bullying or discrimination online can breach our Code of Conduct, even on a personal account.
- **Think before you post** about political issues, conflicts or our partners and funders. Others may link your views to us.

We do not monitor your personal accounts. We will only look into them if someone raises a concern.

::: adapt Personal accounts and employment law
Check your local employment and privacy law before you set rules for staff's personal accounts or act on what they post. In many countries, employers cannot monitor private accounts or discipline staff for lawful views expressed privately, unless there is a clear link to their work. Keep the rules focused on confidentiality, safeguarding and harm to colleagues.
:::

# How we respond

## 11. Comments, criticism and trolling

We welcome questions and fair criticism. We respond in a calm and respectful way, and we never argue in public.

{widths: 30 70}
| Type of comment | What we do |
|---|---|
| Question or fair criticism | Reply politely within [[2 working days]]. Offer to continue privately if personal details are involved. |
| Complaint about our work | Thank them, and invite them to use our Complaints and Feedback Procedure. Log it. |
| False information | Reply once, briefly, with the facts and a source. Do not keep arguing. |
| Abuse, hate speech, threats, spam or harassment | Hide or delete, block and report to the platform. Take a screenshot first. Report threats to the director the same day and, if serious, to the police. |
| Comments that identify or risk harm to someone we serve | Hide or delete at once and tell the safeguarding lead. |
| Coordinated attacks or a sudden rise in negative comments | Tell the director. Pause scheduled posts. Follow Crisis communications below. |

If you are targeted online because of your work with us, tell the communications lead. We will support you, for example by reporting accounts, tightening privacy settings and providing [[support]]. You do not have to respond yourself.

::: adapt Comment moderation
Publish a short community guideline on your pages, for example "We remove abuse, hate speech, spam and personal information." This makes moderation decisions fairer and easier to explain. Set filters for offensive words in your local languages. Some platforms let you limit who can comment during a crisis.
:::

## 12. Media enquiries

- Only the spokesperson, [[Name, role]], or someone they authorise, speaks to journalists for [[Organisation name]].
- If a journalist contacts you, be polite, take their name, outlet, deadline and question, and pass it to the spokesperson **the same day**. Do not comment, even "off the record".
- We never give journalists access to the people we serve without their informed consent, and never to children without a parent's or guardian's consent and a safeguarding check.

## 13. Crisis communications

A communications crisis is any situation that could seriously harm our reputation, or the safety of people, and draws public attention. For example, an allegation against us on social media, a serious incident reported by the media, a hacked account or a viral negative post.

1. **Tell the spokesperson and the director immediately**, and report under the Incident Reporting Procedure.
2. **Pause scheduled posts and advertising** within [[1 hour]].
3. **Check the facts.** Do not speculate, blame or deny before you know what happened.
4. **Put people's safety first.** Never publish information that could identify a survivor, a person who reported a concern or anyone at risk.
5. **Publish a short holding statement** within [[24 hours]] if needed, approved by the director: what we know, what we are doing, and when we will update.
6. **Tell those affected first where possible**: staff, partners, funders and the communities involved, before or as the statement goes out.
7. **Update** as agreed, and close with a short review of what we learned.

If an account is hacked, the second administrator changes passwords, contacts the platform and posts a short notice once access is restored.

# Records, confidentiality and data

- The communications lead keeps consent forms linked to every image and story we publish, in [[secure location]], with their expiry dates.
- We keep a record of approvals for sensitive posts, screenshots of serious abuse or threats, and our crisis statements, for [[3 years]].
- Personal data in comments, messages and supporter lists is handled under our Data Protection Policy. We do not copy people's personal details from social media into our records without a clear reason.
- We delete images and stories when consent ends or is withdrawn, and remove them from our accounts within [[10 working days]].

# If this policy is breached

Report breaches to the communications lead, or, if they involve safeguarding, to the safeguarding lead, within [[24 hours]]. We may remove content, remove access to accounts, and take action under our Code of Conduct, Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure or the person's agreement with us. Posting content that puts someone at risk, or sharing confidential information, may be treated as serious misconduct.

# Monitoring and review

The communications lead reviews account access and security [[every quarter]], and reports to the director [[every year]] on reach, engagement, complaints, moderation and any incidents. We review this policy every year, after a crisis, or when we start using a new platform.
