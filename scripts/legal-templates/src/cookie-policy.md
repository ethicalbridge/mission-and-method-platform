::: adapt Before you use this policy
This policy must describe the cookies your website actually sets, and your cookie banner must do what this policy says. Before you adapt it:

1. Scan your website with a free cookie scanner, or open your browser's developer tools (Application or Storage tab) and list the cookies set on each main page, including donation and contact pages.
2. Ask your web developer or check your website platform's settings for any analytics, advertising pixel, chat widget, map, video or social media plugin.
3. Decide which categories you need. Many small organisations need only strictly necessary cookies and one analytics tool. Fewer cookies mean less to manage and less risk.
4. Set up a banner that blocks non-essential cookies until the visitor agrees, offers "Accept" and "Reject" with equal prominence, and lets visitors change their mind later.

Repeat the scan at least once a year and whenever you add a plugin or tool.
:::

# About this policy

# What cookies are

A **cookie** is a small text file that a website saves on your computer, phone or tablet when you visit. It lets the website recognise your device, for example to remember your choices or to count how many people visit a page.

Websites also use **similar technologies** that work in the same way, such as pixels (tiny invisible images), local storage in your browser, and scripts from other companies. In this policy, "cookies" includes all of these.

Cookies can be:

- **first-party**, set by our website, or **third-party**, set by another organisation whose service we use on our website;
- **session cookies**, deleted when you close your browser, or **persistent cookies**, which stay on your device until they expire or you delete them.

Cookies can collect personal information, such as an online identifier, your IP address or your browsing activity. When they do, we treat this information as set out in our Privacy Notice.

# The cookies we use

We group cookies into four categories. You can choose which non-essential categories to allow.

## 1. Strictly necessary cookies

These make our website work and keep it secure. For example, they remember your cookie choices, keep a donation or contact form working while you fill it in, and protect the site from spam and attacks. They do not need your consent, because the website cannot work properly without them. You can block them in your browser settings, but parts of the website may then stop working.

## 2. Preference cookies

These remember choices you make, such as your language or region, so you do not have to set them again. We use them only if you agree.

## 3. Analytics cookies

These help us understand how people use our website: which pages are visited, how people arrive and where they leave. We use this to improve the website and our communications. We use [[name of analytics tool]] and have set it to [[shorten IP addresses / not share data for advertising / keep data for X months]]. We use analytics cookies only if you agree.

## 4. Marketing cookies

These are set by advertising and social media platforms, such as [[Meta, Google Ads, LinkedIn]]. They measure whether our campaigns work and may be used to show you our messages on other websites. The platforms may combine this information with what they already know about you. We use marketing cookies only if you agree.

::: adapt Which categories and consent rules apply
Delete any category you do not use. If you use no analytics or marketing cookies at all, say so plainly: it builds trust.

Country notes. In the EU, the ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC), as applied by each member state's law, requires consent before setting any cookie that is not strictly necessary, and consent must meet the GDPR standard: a clear action, no pre-ticked boxes, and refusing as easy as accepting. In the UK, the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR) set similar rules; since 5 February 2026 the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 allows some low-risk cookies, such as certain analytics and functionality cookies, without consent if visitors are clearly told and given a simple, free way to object. Advertising and tracking cookies still need consent. Check ICO guidance on the conditions before relying on this. Brazil's ANPD has published guidance on cookies under the LGPD. Many other countries, including several in Africa and Asia, have no cookie-specific rule, but their general data protection law still applies when cookies collect personal information, so be transparent and offer a choice. If you have many visitors from the EU or UK, apply EU and UK rules to everyone: it is simpler and fair.
:::

# Your choices

**Our cookie banner.** When you first visit our website, a banner asks whether you accept non-essential cookies. You can accept all, reject all, or choose by category. We do not set non-essential cookies until you choose. Rejecting cookies does not stop you using our website or donating.

**Changing your mind.** You can change or withdraw your choice at any time through the "Cookie settings" link at the bottom of every page. If you withdraw consent, we stop setting those cookies. Cookies already set stay until they expire or you delete them in your browser.

**Your browser.** Most browsers let you see, block and delete cookies, and some block third-party cookies by default. Look in the "Settings", "Privacy" or "Help" menu of your browser. Blocking all cookies may stop some websites, including ours, from working properly.

**Opt-out tools.** Some providers offer their own opt-out tools, for example [[the Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on]]. Advertising platforms let you control personalised adverts in your account settings.

::: adapt Banner and consent records
Use a banner tool that records each visitor's choice (date, time, version of the banner and categories chosen): this is your evidence of consent. Many website platforms and consent management tools do this, including free versions for small sites. Ask for consent again at least every [[6 to 12 months]], or when you add a new cookie category. Some advertising and analytics platforms also require you, under their own terms, to collect consent from visitors in the EU and UK and to pass their choice to the platform; check the terms of the tools you use. Delete "Opt-out tools" if you use no third-party tools.
:::
