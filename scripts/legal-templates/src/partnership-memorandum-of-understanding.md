::: adapt Before you use this memorandum
**Use it** when organisations agree to work together towards a shared goal, each contributing its own skills, staff or resources: a joint programme, a network or coalition, a referral partnership, or a consortium preparing joint bids.

**Do not use it on its own** when money passes from one partner to another to deliver activities. Then sign a Sub-grant Agreement (for grant funds) or a Services Agreement (for paid services) as well, and refer to it in clause 5. If partners will share personal data about the people they serve, sign a Data Sharing Agreement.

**Binding or not?** An MOU is mainly a statement of intentions. This template makes a small number of clauses binding, listed in the table below, and the rest non-binding. Calling a document an MOU does not by itself make it non-binding: in some legal systems, including many civil-law countries, a document can bind the parties if it shows agreement on essential terms, and parties owe duties of good faith while they negotiate. Keep the status table, and check your local law.

**Fairness:** read it from the point of view of the smallest partner. If it gives that partner duties without the time, money or support to meet them, change it.
:::

::: note How this memorandum works
This memorandum records how the partners intend to work together. **Only the clauses marked "Binding" in the table below create legal obligations.** All other clauses are a sincere statement of intent: the partners will try in good faith to follow them, but they are not legally enforceable, and no partner can claim compensation for a failure to follow them.

::: adapt Choosing what binds
The binding clauses are those that protect people and reputations whatever happens to the partnership: confidentiality, data, logos, safeguarding, how to settle disputes, and how to leave. Partners can move other clauses into the binding list, for example a firm commitment to contribute staff time, but every binding duty should have the resources to meet it. If you need the whole relationship to be enforceable, use a full partnership or consortium agreement instead.
:::

# Background

1. [[Partner 1]] [[e.g. works with smallholder farmers in X district on climate-resilient farming]].
2. [[Partner 2]] [[e.g. provides financial literacy training and links co-operatives to savings groups]].
3. The Partners believe that by working together they can [[e.g. help 1,500 farming households increase and stabilise their income by 2028]], more effectively than either could alone.

# Our partnership principles

The Partners will work according to these principles, drawn from the Principles of Partnership endorsed by the Global Humanitarian Platform in 2007 (equality, transparency, a results-oriented approach, responsibility and complementarity):

- **Equality and respect.** Each Partner has an equal voice, whatever its size or budget. We respect each other's mandate, independence and ways of working.
- **Local leadership.** Partners closest to the communities lead on decisions about how activities are delivered there. Communities are involved in decisions that affect them.
- **Transparency.** We share information on plans, budgets, risks and problems openly and early, including the costs each Partner bears.
- **Fair resourcing.** Budgets prepared together include a fair share of each Partner's running costs (overheads), not only activity costs. No Partner is expected to fund the partnership from its own reserves without agreeing to it.
- **Shared risk.** We do not pass risks to another Partner without also passing the resources, support and authority to manage them.
- **Complementarity and learning.** We build on each other's strengths and learn from each other. Capacity strengthening goes both ways.
- **Accountability.** We are accountable to the communities we serve, to each other and to our funders, in that order of priority when they conflict.

::: adapt Your principles
Keep the principles short and real. Partners in humanitarian work may also refer to the Core Humanitarian Standard, the Grand Bargain localisation commitments or the Charter for Change, whose signatories commit to support local and national organisations more fairly, including their core costs. Before signing, discuss one practical test: "If a funder cuts the budget by 30%, how will we share the cut?" Write the answer into clause 5.
:::

# Agreed terms

## 1. Definitions and status of this memorandum (binding)

1. In this memorandum, **Partnership Activities** means the activities described in Schedule 1. **Confidential Information** means information a Partner shares for the partnership that is marked confidential or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential. **Personal Data** means information about an identified or identifiable living person.
2. Clauses 1, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13 and 14 are legally binding. All other clauses, the principles and the schedules record intentions only and are not legally binding, except that the minimum standards in Schedule 3 apply as part of clause 8.
3. This memorandum does not oblige any Partner to enter into any further agreement, to commit funds or to apply for funding.

## 2. Shared purpose and objectives (non-binding)

1. The purpose of the partnership is [[e.g. to improve the income and resilience of farming households in X district]].
2. The Partners aim to achieve these objectives by [[date]]:
  1. [[Objective 1, e.g. 1,500 households adopt at least three climate-resilient practices]];
  2. [[Objective 2, e.g. 60 farmer groups have active savings schemes]]; and
  3. [[Objective 3, e.g. a joint model is documented and shared with the county government]].

## 3. Activities and responsibilities (non-binding)

1. Each Partner intends to carry out the activities assigned to it in Schedule 1, with the staff and resources shown in Schedule 2.
2. Each Partner remains responsible for how it delivers its own activities, in line with its own policies and the law.
3. A Partner that cannot deliver an activity as planned will tell the others as soon as possible, so that the steering group can agree a solution.

## 4. Governance and decision-making (non-binding)

1. The Partners will form a **steering group** with [[one / two]] representatives from each Partner, with authority to take decisions for their organisation.
2. The steering group will:
  1. agree the annual workplan and joint budget;
  2. review progress, risks and learning;
  3. decide on joint funding applications and joint communications; and
  4. review this memorandum each year.
3. The steering group meets [[every three months]], in person or online, and when any Partner asks. The convening Partner sends the agenda at least [[7]] days before, and the minutes, with agreed actions, within [[10]] days after.
4. Decisions are taken by consensus. If consensus cannot be reached after two meetings, the matter is handled under clause 11. A decision that adds work or cost for a Partner needs that Partner's agreement.
5. Meetings are held in [[language(s)]]. Where needed, the Partners will arrange interpretation and share papers in good time, so that everyone can take part fully. The cost of attending is [[met from the joint budget / borne by each Partner]].

::: adapt Governance that works for small partners
Keep governance light: two to four meetings a year is usually enough. Rotate the chair, and hold some meetings where the local partner works. Budget for travel, data bundles or interpretation so that smaller partners can attend. If a funder requires a lead organisation, state that the lead's extra powers are limited to what the funder requires, and that other decisions stay with the steering group.
:::

## 5. Resources and funding (non-binding)

1. Each Partner intends to contribute the staff time, expertise, facilities and funds shown in Schedule 2.
2. The Partners will agree together which Partner leads each joint funding application, based on fit and capacity rather than size. Every Partner named in a proposal will see and approve its budget and its description of that Partner's role before submission.
3. Joint budgets will include a fair share of each Partner's running costs, at [[the same indirect cost rate for all Partners / at least X% of direct costs]], where the funder allows.
4. Where one Partner passes funds to another, they will sign a separate Sub-grant Agreement or Services Agreement before any funds move. If that agreement conflicts with this memorandum, that agreement prevails.
5. If funding is reduced or ends, the steering group will agree how to adjust activities, sharing the reduction fairly and protecting commitments to communities first.

::: adapt Money and fair overheads
Be specific in Schedule 2: hours a month, named staff, cash amounts, in-kind items. Unpaid time is a real cost, especially for small organisations. Funders' rules on indirect costs vary widely, and some cap or exclude them for sub-grantees: check each funder's rules, and negotiate the same rate for all Partners where possible. If the lead partner keeps a management fee, say so here.
:::

## 6. Names, logos and visibility (binding)

1. No Partner will use another Partner's name, logo or trade marks without its prior written approval, which may be given by email for each use or for an agreed type of use.
2. Joint publications and communications about the partnership will credit all Partners fairly and visibly, with logos of equal size unless the Partners agree otherwise, and in line with any funder's visibility rules.
3. Each Partner may describe the partnership factually in its own reports and materials.
4. A Partner whose participation ends, or whose approval is withdrawn, will stop new uses of another Partner's name and logo within [[30]] days.

## 7. Confidentiality, data and intellectual property (binding)

1. Each Partner will keep the other Partners' Confidential Information confidential, use it only for the partnership and share it only with people who need it and are bound to keep it confidential. These duties last for [[3]] years after this memorandum ends, and without time limit for Personal Data and safeguarding information.
2. The Partners will share Personal Data about the people they serve only under a signed Data Sharing Agreement, and only what is needed. Each Partner will comply with the data protection laws that apply to it, including [[e.g. the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019]].
3. Each Partner keeps ownership of the intellectual property (such as copyright in materials, methods and tools) it brings to the partnership or creates alone.
4. Materials the Partners create together ("Joint Materials") are owned [[jointly in equal shares / by the Partner that leads their creation]]. Each Partner may use, adapt and share Joint Materials free of charge for non-commercial purposes, crediting all Partners. Commercial use needs the consent of all Partners.
5. Knowledge, stories and images from communities are used only with their informed consent, and community members are credited as they wish.

::: adapt Data and intellectual property
Decide early who owns joint toolkits, curricula or research. Joint ownership can make later decisions slow, because each owner may need to agree. A common alternative is that the lead author owns the material and grants all Partners a free, permanent licence, or that Joint Materials are published under an open licence such as Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). Check your funders' rules, which may require open publication. Indigenous and traditional knowledge needs particular care: agree with the community how it may be recorded and used.
:::

## 8. Safeguarding, fraud and counter-terrorism financing (binding)

1. Each Partner will have, or adopt within [[3 months]] of signing, policies at least as protective as the minimum standards in Schedule 3, on safeguarding (including protection from sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment, known as PSEAH), fraud and corruption, and whistleblowing. A Partner may adopt another Partner's policy for the partnership.
2. Each Partner will apply the six core principles of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) on sexual exploitation and abuse to all its staff and volunteers working on the partnership.
3. Each Partner will tell the other Partners' focal points, within [[24 hours]] for safeguarding concerns and [[48 hours]] for suspected fraud, of any incident or allegation connected with the partnership, sharing only the information needed and protecting the identity of survivors and whistleblowers. Each Partner will cooperate with any investigation.
4. Each Partner confirms that neither it, nor its board members or senior staff, is on the UN Security Council Consolidated List or [[the other sanctions lists the Partners' funders use]], and will tell the others at once if this changes. No Partner will knowingly provide funds or support to any person or group on those lists.
5. Where a funder's conditions apply to Partnership Activities, the Partner receiving the funds will share those conditions with the other Partners affected, and any further duties will be set out in a Sub-grant Agreement.
6. A Partner that needs support to meet these standards will tell the steering group, which will agree a plan and, where possible, budget for it. No Partner will be excluded for a gap it is actively working to close, unless the gap puts people at serious risk.

::: adapt Safeguarding and compliance flow-down
These are the duties most funders expect every partner to meet, so they are binding. Keep them proportionate: a small partner may share a safeguarding lead with another organisation for a time, or adopt the convening Partner's policies while it develops its own. Sanctions screening follows a risk-based approach, as encouraged by FATF Recommendation 8 on non-profit organisations: do not exclude partners or communities on assumptions about religion or region. Check whether your country's counter-terrorism financing law sets extra duties for non-profits, and use the Partner Due Diligence and Sanctions Screening Policy.
:::

## 9. Reporting and learning (non-binding)

1. Each Partner will share a short progress update on its activities with the steering group before each meeting, using a format the Partners agree.
2. The Partners will agree together how to monitor results, and will share data needed for joint reports in time for funder deadlines, giving at least [[15]] days' notice of what is needed.
3. At least once a year, the Partners will review what is working, what is not and what they have learned, including feedback from the communities they serve.

## 10. Communication between partners (non-binding)

1. Each Partner names a focal point in Schedule 3 as its main contact, and a deputy.
2. Partners will reply to each other within [[5]] working days, and the same day for safeguarding or security matters.
3. The Partners will agree together any public statement about the partnership, and will tell each other in advance of media contact about it. In an emergency, a Partner may speak about its own work and will inform the others as soon as possible.
4. Each Partner will tell the others promptly about changes that may affect the partnership, such as a change of leadership, funding, legal status or a serious complaint.

## 11. Resolving disagreements (binding)

1. The Partners will try first to resolve any disagreement through their focal points, within [[10]] working days of it being raised in writing.
2. If that fails, the heads of the Partners concerned will meet, in person or online, within a further [[15]] working days.
3. If they cannot agree, the Partners will invite a mediator acceptable to all of them, such as [[a respected local figure, network coordinator or mediation centre]], and share the cost [[equally / in proportion to their budgets]].
4. No Partner will start court proceedings about this memorandum before completing these steps, except to seek urgent protection of its rights under clauses 6, 7 or 8.

## 12. Costs, independence and liability (binding)

1. Each Partner pays its own costs, except as agreed in writing or in a separate agreement.
2. This memorandum does not create a legal partnership, joint venture, agency or employment relationship. No Partner may make commitments, sign documents or incur costs for another without its written authority.
3. Each Partner remains responsible for its own staff, volunteers, activities and compliance with the law.
4. No Partner is liable to another for failing to follow a non-binding clause. Each Partner's liability for breach of a binding clause is limited to direct loss, except for fraud, deliberate breach, or any liability that cannot be limited by law.

## 13. Term, review and exit (binding)

1. This memorandum starts when all Partners have signed it and lasts until [[date]], unless the Partners agree in writing to extend it.
2. The steering group will review this memorandum at least once a year. Changes must be agreed in writing by all Partners.
3. Any Partner may leave the partnership by giving [[60]] days' written notice to the others.
4. If a Partner commits a serious breach of clause 8 or acts in a way that puts people at serious risk, any other Partner may end its own participation at once by written notice, and the other Partners may together suspend that Partner by written notice.
5. When a Partner leaves, or the partnership ends, the Partners will agree an exit plan within [[30]] days. It will:
  1. protect the communities served, by completing, handing over or responsibly closing activities and telling communities what will happen;
  2. return or delete Confidential Information and Personal Data as the Data Sharing Agreement requires;
  3. settle any agreed costs; and
  4. agree how the partnership is described publicly afterwards.
6. Clauses 6, 7, 8.3, 11, 12 and 13.5 continue after a Partner leaves or the memorandum ends.

::: adapt Leaving well
The exit clause is often the most useful one. Agree in advance what happens to staff, equipment, data and community commitments if a partner leaves or funding ends. Notice periods of 60–90 days are common. Do not let a local partner be left to explain an abrupt closure to communities alone: share that responsibility in the exit plan.
:::

## 14. Governing law and signing (binding)

1. This memorandum is governed by the law of [[country]].
2. It may be signed in counterparts, including by electronic signature, and in [[English and language]]. If versions differ, the [[English]] version prevails.

Minimum standards that each Partner meets or is working towards under clause 8:

- A safeguarding policy, a named safeguarding lead, and a safe way for communities to raise concerns, including about the Partner's own staff.
- Safe recruitment: references for all staff and volunteers working with children or adults at risk, and other checks where the law allows.
- A code of conduct signed by staff and volunteers, based on the IASC six core principles.
- A fraud and corruption policy, basic financial controls (two people involved in every payment), and a way to report concerns safely.
- A whistleblowing route that does not depend on the person concerned.
- Sanctions screening of the Partner's board, senior staff and main suppliers against the lists in clause 8.4, with results recorded.
- Secure storage of personal data and a named person responsible for data protection.
