[[Organisation name]] is a [[small organisation of X people / start-up run by its founders]]. This is our [[first]] version of this policy. Until we grow:

- **[[Name, role]]** keeps the gifts and hospitality register and advises anyone unsure whether to accept a gift.
- **[[Name, role]], usually the director,** decides whether to accept donations up to [[amount and currency]], after the checks in this policy.
- **The board** [[or the chair and treasurer together]] decides on donations above that amount, on any gift that raises a reputational concern, and on any gift offered to the director personally.
- **We review this policy** at least once a year, and when we start a new kind of fundraising.

::: adapt Who holds these roles
Name real people. In a very small organisation the director may both raise funds and decide whether to accept them. That is normal, but any gift linked to the director, a board member or their close associates must go to the board. Set the director's limit to match your Delegation of Authority Matrix.
:::

::: glance
- Never accept a gift or hospitality that could influence, or look as if it influences, a decision you make for us.
- Small tokens under [[amount]] are fine. Declare anything above that within [[5 working days]] in the register. Never accept cash.
- Never accept anything from a supplier during a tender, or from someone applying to us for a job, contract or support.
- We welcome donations that fit our mission, come from legitimate sources and leave us independent.
- We check donors in proportion to the size and risk of the gift, including sanctions lists for larger gifts.
- We may decline, or return, any gift that could harm the people we serve, our independence or our reputation.
- We honour the conditions of restricted gifts, and agree them in writing.
:::

# Who this policy applies to

Part A applies to everyone who works with or for [[Organisation name]]: staff, volunteers, interns, board members [[trustees / directors / committee members]], advisers, contractors and consultants. It covers gifts and hospitality offered because of your role, including to your close family.

Part B applies to every donation [[Organisation name]] is offered: from individuals, companies, foundations, other organisations, governments and community groups, whether in money, goods, services or other assets.

**Law.** [[Organisation name]] is registered in [[country]] and follows its laws on fundraising, donations, tax receipts, foreign funding, money laundering and sanctions. Where a funder's or partner's rules are stricter, we follow the stricter rule.

::: adapt Law and countries
List the laws that apply to your donations. Common areas to check: rules on receiving foreign funding (for example, in India foreign contributions are regulated by the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act 2010 and must be received in a designated account); rules on issuing tax receipts to donors (for example, in the US a donor needs a written acknowledgement for a gift of USD 250 or more, and in the UK Gift Aid has its own declaration rules); public fundraising permits; and anti-money laundering duties. If you are not sure what applies, check your local law on fundraising and foreign funding before you accept international donations.
:::

# Part A: Gifts and hospitality

## 1. Our principles

1. Your decisions for us must be free from any personal benefit, and must look that way to others.
2. Gifts and hospitality must be modest, occasional, open and never asked for.
3. If you are unsure, say no politely, or ask [[Name, role]] before you accept.
4. Record what you are offered, not only what you accept.

## 2. What you may accept

{widths: 28 72}
| Gift or hospitality | What to do |
|---|---|
| Token items worth less than [[amount and currency]] | You may accept. No need to record. Examples: a calendar, a pen, tea and snacks at a meeting, a small locally made item. |
| Worth [[amount]] to [[higher amount]] | Ask [[Name, role]] before accepting where possible. Record it in the register within [[5 working days]]. We may share it with the team, raffle it for our work or display it. |
| Worth more than [[higher amount]] | Politely decline. If refusing would cause serious offence, accept on behalf of [[Organisation name]], hand it to [[Name, role]], and record it. It becomes our property. |
| Never acceptable, whatever the value | Cash, mobile money, vouchers, loans or anything that can be turned into cash. Anything from a supplier during a tender or contract renewal. Anything from a job applicant or someone applying for support. Anything offered in return for a decision. Personal discounts not available to all staff. |

::: adapt Thresholds
Set amounts that make sense locally, for example the price of a modest meal for the lower threshold and a few times that for the upper one. Use one currency and convert others at the date of the gift. Many small organisations simply set one threshold and record everything above it. Some ban all gifts from suppliers. Make the amounts match your Code of Conduct.
:::

## 3. Gifts from the people we serve

In many cultures, communities offer food, drink or a small gift as a sign of respect and thanks. Refusing can cause offence. You may accept small gifts of food, drink or a symbolic item, and share them with the team where possible. Never accept anything of real value, such as livestock, produce for resale or money, from people we serve. They may feel obliged to give it, or expect better treatment in return. If you are offered something you cannot accept, thank the person warmly and explain that our rules do not allow it.

## 4. Hospitality

Reasonable hospitality, such as a working lunch or a reception linked to our work, is acceptable if it is not lavish and has a clear work purpose. Ask [[Name, role]] before accepting:

- hospitality above [[amount and currency]] per person;
- any invitation that includes travel, accommodation or a partner or family member;
- any invitation to a sporting, cultural or leisure event.

We normally pay our own travel and accommodation costs when attending events organised by others. If a funder or partner pays, we record it and make sure it is allowed by their rules and ours.

## 5. Giving gifts and hospitality

- Gifts we give must be modest, appropriate and approved by [[Name, role]]. Branded items, local crafts and certificates are good choices.
- Never give gifts or hospitality to public officials, other than token branded items, unless [[Name, role]] has approved it in advance and it is allowed under local law.
- Never give anything to influence a funding, permit, contract or other decision. Follow our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure.
- Hospitality for participants, volunteers and partners at our activities, such as meals and refreshments, is a normal programme cost. It must be in the budget and supported by receipts.

## 6. How to declare and record

Within [[5 working days]] of being offered a gift or hospitality above [[amount and currency]], tell [[Name, role]] by email. Give the date, who offered it, what it was, its approximate value, whether you accepted or declined, and why. [[Name, role]] enters it in the gifts and hospitality register (see the annex). Gifts offered to board members are reported to the chair. The board reviews the register [[once a year]].

# Part B: Donations to [[Organisation name]]

## 7. Our principles

We welcome donations that help us achieve our mission. We accept a donation only if:

1. it comes from a legitimate, lawful source;
2. it fits our mission and values;
3. it does not compromise our independence, our relationships with the communities we serve, or our ability to speak out;
4. we can meet any conditions attached to it;
5. accepting it would not harm the people we serve, our staff or our reputation.

The final test is: would we be comfortable if the people we serve, our other funders and the media knew about this gift and its terms?

## 8. Checks on donors

We check donors in proportion to the size of the gift and the risk.

{widths: 28 72}
| Gift size (illustrative) | Checks |
|---|---|
| Under [[amount and currency]] | Basic checks: the gift is from a real person or organisation and the payment comes from their own account. |
| [[amount]] to [[higher amount]] | Basic checks, plus an online search for adverse news, and a check of the donor against the UN Security Council Consolidated List and the sanctions lists our funders use. |
| Above [[higher amount]], or any gift that raises concern | All the above, plus: who owns or controls the donor; where the money comes from; any links with our board or staff; and any conditions. The director prepares a short note for the board, which decides. |

We repeat checks for regular donors at least [[every two years]], and whenever we hear something that raises concern. We record the date and result of each check. Our Partner Due Diligence and Sanctions Screening Policy explains how to run sanctions checks.

::: adapt Donor checks
Set amounts that match your income. A gift that is large compared with your annual income deserves closer checks even if it is small in absolute terms; for example, set the higher threshold at [[10%]] of your annual income. Include companies and foundations as well as individuals. If you receive funds from abroad, check whether your country requires you to report or register them. Some funders require you to screen all donors above a certain amount; check your grant agreements.
:::

## 9. Gifts we will not accept

We will decline a donation if:

- we know or suspect it comes from crime, corruption, tax evasion or money laundering;
- the donor, or anyone controlling it, is on a sanctions list that applies to us or our funders;
- the donor's activities directly conflict with our mission, for example [[list sectors, such as tobacco, weapons, or companies under investigation for harm to the communities we serve]];
- it comes with conditions that would give the donor control over our programmes, staff, beneficiary selection or public statements, or a commercial benefit we cannot provide;
- accepting it would put the people we serve, our staff or partners at risk;
- it is offered in return for a decision, a contract or a benefit to the donor or someone close to them;
- the donor appears unable to afford it, or not to understand what they are giving, for example a person who is unwell or vulnerable;
- it is offered by someone applying to us for a job, a contract or support while that decision is pending.

::: adapt Ethical red lines
Decide which sectors or donors you will not accept money from, based on your mission. A health organisation may refuse tobacco and alcohol money; an environmental group may refuse fossil fuel companies; a peace-building group may refuse political parties. Some organisations exclude government funding to stay independent. Be specific: vague rules are hard to apply. Ask the board to agree the list.
:::

## 10. Conditions and restricted gifts

- A **restricted gift** is one the donor says must be used for a specific purpose, project or place. An **unrestricted gift** can be used for any of our charitable purposes.
- We agree any restriction in writing before we accept the gift, using a grant agreement, letter or the donor's own form.
- We record restricted gifts separately in our accounts and spend them only as agreed, following our Financial Controls Policy.
- We include a clause, wherever possible, that lets us use the gift for a similar purpose if the original one becomes impossible, with the donor's agreement.
- We may ask to use [[a percentage, e.g. 10%]] of a restricted gift for the core costs that make the project possible.
- If we cannot meet a condition, we tell the donor promptly and agree a new purpose or return the unspent amount.

## 11. Gifts in kind

Gifts in kind are goods or services instead of money: equipment, supplies, food, medicines, office space, professional services or volunteer time from a company.

- We accept them only if we need them and can use them within a reasonable time.
- We check that goods are safe, legal and of acceptable quality. Medicines, food and baby products must meet national standards and have a suitable expiry date. Used equipment must work.
- We consider the hidden costs: transport, storage, customs duties, installation, maintenance and disposal.
- We do not accept goods that would undermine local markets or producers, unless there is a clear need.
- We record significant gifts in kind in our assets register or accounts at a fair value. We do not give donors an inflated value for their tax purposes.

## 12. Anonymous donations

Donors may ask that their name is not published, and we respect that. For gifts above [[amount and currency]], we must still know who the donor is internally, so that we can complete our checks. We do not accept large gifts where we cannot identify the true source.

## 13. Cash donations

We accept small cash donations, for example at events and collections. Two people count cash and sign the record. We bank it within [[3 working days]]. We do not accept single cash donations above [[amount and currency]]; we ask the donor to pay by bank transfer instead.

## 14. Other kinds of gift

We may accept these, with the board's approval and professional advice where needed:

- **Legacies:** gifts left in a will. We respond promptly to executors, check any conditions, and record them as restricted or unrestricted as the will states.
- **Shares and securities:** we normally sell them promptly, unless the board decides otherwise.
- **Land and property:** only after a survey, legal checks and an assessment of ongoing costs.
- **Cryptocurrency:** only through a regulated payment provider that converts it to [[currency]] immediately, and only where local law allows. We apply the same donor checks as for other gifts.
- **Sponsorship and cause-related marketing:** only under a written agreement that sets out how the company may use our name and logo.

::: adapt Legacies, crypto and other gifts
Delete what you will not accept. Some countries restrict or ban dealings in cryptocurrency, and many banks are cautious about it; check your local law and talk to your bank first. Legacies and property gifts can involve local inheritance, land and tax law; take legal advice before accepting. If you work with company sponsors, decide which uses of your logo you will allow.
:::

## 15. Refusing or returning a gift

1. If anyone has concerns about a proposed gift, they tell the director before it is accepted.
2. The director, or the board for larger gifts, decides within [[10 working days]], using the principles and checks in this policy.
3. We decline politely and in writing, without needing to give detailed reasons.
4. If we find a problem after accepting a gift, we may return it. We return money only to the same person or account it came from, never to a third party, and only after checking it has cleared.
5. We record every refusal and return, and the reasons, in the donations register.

## 16. Thanking donors and their data

- We thank donors promptly and give receipts where the law or the donor requires them.
- We only publish a donor's name, logo or story with their consent, using our Fundraising and Communications Consent Form where relevant.
- We handle donors' personal information under our Data Protection Policy. We do not sell or share donor lists.
- Donors get no say over who receives support, and no access to personal information about the people we serve.

# Records, confidentiality and data

- We keep a gifts and hospitality register (see the annex) and a donations register with checks, decisions and reasons.
- We keep them for [[7 years]], like our other financial records, following our Records Retention Schedule, and make them available to our auditors and, where required, to funders and regulators.
- We handle donors' and staff members' personal information under our Data Protection Policy.

# If this policy is breached

Accepting an improper gift, failing to declare one, or accepting a donation in breach of this policy may lead to disciplinary action, the end of a role or contract, and the return of the gift. If a gift may be a bribe, or a donation may involve money laundering, we follow our Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure.

# Monitoring and review

[[Name, role]] reports to the board [[once a year]] on the registers, including gifts declined and donations refused or returned. The board reviews this policy [[every year]], and when we start a new kind of fundraising.
