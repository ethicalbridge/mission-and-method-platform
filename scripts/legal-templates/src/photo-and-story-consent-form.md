::: adapt Before you use this form
**Lawful basis.** An identifiable photo, video, voice recording or story is personal data. We use consent here because it is the fairest basis for public storytelling, and many data protection laws, including the GDPR, the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019, Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023, South Africa's POPIA and Brazil's LGPD, require consent to be freely given, specific and informed, and let people withdraw it at any time (the GDPR adds: as easily as they gave it). Images that reveal health, religion, ethnicity, sexual orientation or similar sensitive information need extra care and often explicit consent. Some countries also give people a separate right over their own image, for example Brazil's Civil Code (Article 20) and Argentina's Civil and Commercial Code (Article 53): check your local law on image rights.

**Children.** Get consent from a parent or guardian and the child's own agreement (assent). Check your local law on the age at which a child can consent alone; many organisations ask parents up to 18 whatever the law says.

**Language and literacy.** Explain the form out loud in the person's language. Use the verbal or thumbprint record if they cannot or prefer not to sign. Never ask for consent in exchange for help, or while someone is waiting for a service.
:::

# What we are asking

[[Organisation name]] would like to take or use **[[photos / video / audio recordings / your story or quotes]]** of you [[and your child]], taken on [[date]] at [[place or activity]] by [[name of photographer or staff member]].

We tell stories so that people understand and support our work. We want to tell yours truthfully, with dignity, and in a way you are comfortable with.

**Saying no is completely fine.** It will not affect any help, service or support you receive from us, now or in the future. You can say yes to some uses and no to others.

# How we will use them

We will only use your photos and story for the purposes you tick below. We will:

- show or describe the photos and story to you before use, where possible;
- never change your words in a way that changes their meaning;
- never describe you in a way that is untrue or takes away your dignity;
- keep your images and details secure, and only share them with the people and organisations you agree to;
- never sell your images or story.

If we want to use them in a way not listed here, we will ask you again.

::: adapt Uses and identification options
Delete uses you will never need, and add any you will, for example a book, an exhibition or advertising. Keep each use as a separate tick box: a single "yes to everything" box is not specific consent. If funders may use images in their own materials, say so clearly; once shared, you have less control. For people at risk, such as survivors of violence, refugees, people living with HIV, LGBTQI+ people or human rights defenders, make "no face, no real name, no location" the default and explain why.
:::

# How long, and how to withdraw

We will use your photos and story for **[[3 years]]** from the date you sign, unless you tell us to stop sooner. After that we will ask you again, or stop using them and delete them, except for copies kept in our archive [[for our own records only]].

**You can change your mind at any time**, by contacting [[Name, role]] at [[phone, WhatsApp or email]], or by telling any of our staff. We will stop using your photos and story in anything new and remove them from our website and social media within [[10 working days]].

Please understand that some things cannot be undone:

- printed materials already given out, such as reports or posters, cannot be collected back;
- we cannot remove copies that other people or organisations have already downloaded or shared;
- news stories published by journalists are outside our control.

We will ask funders and partners we shared them with to stop using them too.

::: adapt Duration and withdrawal
Choose a period you can manage, commonly 2 to 5 years, and diarise reviews. For children, consider ending use when the child turns 18, or asking the young person again then. If you keep an internal archive, say so and do not publish from it. Under the GDPR and similar laws, withdrawing consent does not make past use unlawful, but you must stop new use.
:::

Some laws let older children consent for themselves: check your local law on children's consent. For children in care, the legal guardian may be the state or an institution. # Annex A: Guidance for staff taking consent

Read this before you take photos, record video or collect stories for [[Organisation name]].

#### Before you start

- Ask first, before you take any photo. Explain the form in the person's own language and give them time.
- Make sure the person understands that saying no will not affect their help in any way. **Never** take photos or stories in exchange for help, food, money or services, and never while someone is queuing for them.
- If someone seems unsure, take that as a no.
- Keep this form, and any photos, on [[approved devices / our secure folder]], not on your personal phone or social media.

#### When not to take photos

Do not take photos or record people:

- who are in distress, injured, grieving, ill or undressed, unless there is a clear purpose, the person has given informed consent once calm, and your manager has agreed. Ask yourself: would I want to be shown like this?
- in places that reveal sensitive information, such as a health clinic, shelter, safe house, detention centre or place of worship, unless agreed in advance;
- who are at risk if identified, for example survivors of violence, people fleeing danger or human rights defenders, unless you follow the safest identification options;
- in a group where you cannot get everyone's consent. Take the photo so that people who did not agree cannot be recognised.

#### Telling stories ethically

- Show people as active and capable, not as helpless victims.
- Use people's own words. Do not add drama, and do not combine different people's stories into one without saying so.
- Never use AI to create or alter images of real people, or to make up a "typical" person we serve.
- Never post photos with location tags or details that show where a child or person at risk lives.

#### After you finish

- Give the person a copy of the form, or a card with our contact details.
- Record the consent and its expiry date in our [[photo consent log]], linked to the image files.
- Report any concern about a child or adult at risk under the Safeguarding Policy and Procedure.

We want to show people what we do, so they can help more children like you.

**You can say yes or no.** Both are fine. If you say no, nothing bad will happen, and you can still come to [[activity]].

**If you say yes:**

- we will not say your full name or where you live;
- you can tell us to stop at any time. Just tell [[name]] or your [[parent / carer]].
