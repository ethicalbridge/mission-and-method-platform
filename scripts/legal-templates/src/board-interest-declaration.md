::: adapt Before you use this form
Use this form on appointment, every year (usually before the meeting that approves the accounts), and whenever a member's interests change. Make sure the categories and the examples match your Conflict of Interest Policy and the rules in your governing document. Some laws require directors or trustees to declare interests in a set way, or require related-party transactions to be shown in the annual accounts: check your local company, charity or non-profit law. The form collects personal information about board members and their family. Tell them why, who will see it and how long you keep it, and follow your Data Protection Policy.
:::

# Purpose of this form

[[Organisation name]] needs to know about any interest that could influence, or could be seen to influence, a decision you take as a member of our [[board of trustees / board of directors / management committee]]. Declaring an interest is not an accusation and does not mean you have done anything wrong. It lets the board decide, openly, how to manage it.

A **conflict of interest** is any situation where your personal interests, or those of people close to you, or your duty to another organisation, could conflict with your duty to act only in the best interests of [[Organisation name]]. It may be actual, possible in future, or perceived by a reasonable outsider. All three must be declared.

# How to complete it

1. Complete every part. Where a category does not apply to you, write "none" in that row.
2. Include interests of your **related persons** (see Part 3) where you know of them.
3. Include interests that ended in the last [[12 months]].
4. If you have nothing to declare, tick the nil return box in Part 2.
5. If in doubt, declare. The board can decide that an interest needs no action.
6. Return the signed form to [[Name, secretary]] at [[email]] within [[14 days]].
7. Tell the secretary within [[28 days]] if anything changes during the year.

::: adapt Categories and sensitive information
Keep the categories that match your Conflict of Interest Policy. Membership of a political party, trade union or religious organisation is sensitive personal information in many data protection laws, and in some countries it can put people at risk. Ask for it only where it is relevant to a real conflict (for example, a board member who is an elected official or party office holder in an area where you work), and tell members they may declare it to the chair privately instead of on this form.

# Part 3: Related persons

A **related person** is someone whose interests could reasonably be seen as your own. Include:

- your spouse, civil partner or partner;
- your children, step-children, parents and siblings, and their partners;
- anyone living in your household;
- a business partner, or a company or organisation you control or manage.

::: adapt Family and community ties
In close-knit communities many board members are related to staff, partners or the people you serve. That is normal and often a strength. The aim is not to avoid every link but to know about them and manage them. You may extend "related person" to include clan, kinship or community ties where these carry real obligations, as is common in many places.
:::

# Part 4: How conflicts will be managed

For each interest declared, the board decides how it will be managed and records the decision below. The member concerned does not take part in that decision.

{widths: 30 45 25}
| Interest (from Part 2) | How it will be managed | Agreed by and date |
|---|---|---|
| [[e.g. Sister is a field officer]] | [[e.g. Member leaves the meeting for any item on field staff pay, recruitment or discipline]] | [[Board, DD Month YYYY]] |
| [[ ]] | [[ ]] | [[ ]] |

The usual options, from least to most restrictive, are:

- **Record only:** the interest is declared and minuted, and no further action is needed.
- **Declare and stay out of the item:** the member declares at the meeting, takes no part in the discussion or vote, and is not counted in the quorum.
- **Leave the room:** the member leaves for the whole item and does not receive the related papers.
- **Approve the transaction with safeguards:** for a contract with a member or related person, the board approves it only if it is clearly in our best interests, on terms at least as good as the market, after at least [[two / three]] comparable quotes, and with the member absent.
- **Step down from a role or the board:** where the conflict is so large or frequent that the member cannot do their job.

# Guidance notes

## Examples of interests to declare

- **Employment:** you work for a bank, a government department or an international NGO that funds or partners with us.
- **Directorships:** you are on the board of another organisation that applies for the same grants as we do, or which we sub-grant to.
- **Shareholdings:** you or your partner own part of a company that sells us goods or services, such as transport, printing, IT or rent.
- **Family:** your nephew applies for a job with us; your partner is a consultant we may hire.
- **Gifts and hospitality:** a supplier or funder paid for your travel, a meal above [[amount and currency]], or a gift connected to your board role. Our Gift Acceptance Policy explains what may be accepted.
- **Memberships:** you hold office in a local authority, political party or community body that decides on permits or funding we need.
- **Funders and suppliers:** you advise a foundation that is considering a grant to us, or you sit on a selection panel for a fund we apply to.
- **Using our services:** you or a family member receive support from our programmes.

## What does not usually need declaring

Small, everyday matters with no link to our work, such as an account at a high-street bank or membership of a sports club, do not need declaring unless they connect to a decision we are taking.

## Gifts thresholds

Declare any gift or hospitality connected to your board role worth more than [[amount and currency]], or several smaller gifts from the same source in a year that together exceed [[amount and currency]].

::: adapt Gift threshold and transparency
Set a threshold that makes sense locally, for example the cost of a modest meal. Use the same amount as your Gift Acceptance Policy and Code of Conduct. Decide whether you will publish a summary of board interests in your annual report or on your website. Many organisations do this to show transparency; some funders and regulators require it. If you publish, show only names, roles and organisations, never related persons' details.
:::

## Keeping the forms

Keep signed forms and the register securely, with access limited to the secretary, the chair [[and the auditor]]. Keep them for [[the period in our Records Retention Schedule, e.g. 6 years after the member leaves]]. Destroy them securely after that.

::: adapt Data protection
Board members' declarations contain personal information about them and their families. Give members a short privacy note (or point to your Privacy Notice), limit who sees the forms, and set a retention period. Check your local data protection law, for example the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019, Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, South Africa's POPIA or Brazil's LGPD.
:::
