::: adapt Before you use this form
**When to use it.** Use it for research and evaluations: interviews, focus groups, surveys and observation where results will be analysed and shared. For routine monitoring of your services, a short privacy statement is often enough. Ask whether you need **ethics approval**: universities, many funders and some governments require it, especially for research with children, health topics or people at risk. Some countries also require a research permit, for example from NACOSTI (National Commission for Science, Technology and Innovation) in Kenya: check your local law on research permits.

**Lawful basis.** Consent to take part in research is an ethical requirement. For data protection, some laws let you rely on another basis, such as public interest or legitimate interests, for processing research data. Under the GDPR and laws like the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019, Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, POPIA and LGPD, health, ethnicity, religion or sexual life data need extra safeguards. Do a Data Protection Impact Assessment for sensitive or large studies.

**Children, language and literacy.** For anyone under [[18]], get a parent's or guardian's consent and the child's own assent. Translate the sheet, read it aloud where needed, and use the verbal consent record for people who cannot or prefer not to sign.
:::

# Part 1: Information sheet

Please keep this sheet. A member of our team will go through it with you.

## What is this study about?

[[Organisation name]] is carrying out a study called **[[study title]]**. We want to understand [[purpose in one or two plain sentences, e.g. how young farmers use the training we offer, and what we could do better]]. The study is [[funded by funder name / carried out with partner name]].

We are asking you because [[e.g. you took part in our training in 2025 / you live in the area where we work]].

## What would taking part involve?

If you agree, you will be asked to [[take part in an interview / join a group discussion with about 8 people / answer a survey]] about [[topics]]. It will take about **[[45 minutes]]**, at [[place / by phone / online]], at a time that suits you.

With your permission, we will [[audio record / take notes]] so that we capture your words accurately. You can skip any question, take a break or stop at any time, without giving a reason.

## Do I have to take part?

**No. Taking part is your choice.** If you say no, or stop part-way, it will not affect any help, service or support you receive from [[Organisation name]] or our partners, now or in the future.

## Are there any risks or benefits?

Some questions may touch on [[sensitive topics, e.g. money, family or health]]. You do not have to answer them. If anything upsets you, we can stop, and we can tell you about [[support services]].

In a group discussion, we ask everyone to keep what others say private, but we cannot guarantee that they will.

There may be no direct benefit to you. What we learn will help us [[improve our programmes / share lessons with others]].

## Will I be paid?

[[We will give you amount or item, e.g. a meal and travel costs / airtime worth amount]] to thank you for your time. You will receive it even if you stop early or skip questions.

::: adapt Incentives
Keep incentives modest and in line with local norms, so that they recognise people's time and costs without pressuring anyone to take part. Common choices are travel costs, a meal, phone airtime or a small voucher. Pay everyone who starts, whether they finish or not. Check whether your ethics committee or funder sets rules on incentives, and how they must be recorded.
:::

## How will my information be kept private?

- We will replace your name with a code in notes and transcripts, and keep the list linking codes to names separately. This is called **pseudonymisation**. Before we share results, we remove anything that could identify you (**anonymisation**).
- Only the research team, [[names or roles]], will see the information that identifies you.
- We store information securely in [[e.g. password-protected files on our encrypted cloud storage]]. Paper forms are kept in a locked place.
- We will delete recordings within [[3 months]] once they are typed up, and keep anonymised notes for [[5 years]].
- We will only share identifying information without your permission if we believe you or someone else is at serious risk of harm, and we will try to tell you first.

## What will happen to the results?

We will write a [[report / summary / article]] about what we learn, and share it with [[the communities involved, funders, partners, the public]]. We will not use your name or anything that could identify you, unless you agree below. We will share a short summary of the findings with participants by [[community meeting / WhatsApp / letter]].

## Ethics approval

This study has been reviewed and approved by [[name of ethics committee or review board]], reference [[number]], on [[date]]. [[Or: This study did not require formal ethics review because reason. It has been reviewed by name, role.]]

::: adapt Ethics review
If no formal review is needed, have someone independent of the study check the questions, consent process and data plan. Name them here. If a partner university or research institute is involved, use their ethics process and add their contact details. Never claim approval you do not have.
:::

## Who can I contact?

- Questions about the study: [[Name, role]], [[phone / WhatsApp / email]].
- A concern or complaint about how the study is run: [[Name, role, not part of the research team]], [[contact]], or through our Complaints and Feedback Procedure.
- Ethics committee: [[contact details, if applicable]].

## Withdrawing

You can withdraw from the study at any time until **[[date]]**, by contacting [[Name]] at [[contact]]. We will then delete your information. After that date, your anonymised answers will already be combined with others for the report, and we cannot remove them, but we will not use any quotes from you that are not yet published.

::: adapt Withdrawal date
Set the date when analysis will start, usually a few weeks after data collection. Before then, participants should be able to withdraw fully. Explain the date clearly. Under the GDPR and similar laws, people can withdraw consent at any time, but once data is fully anonymised it is no longer personal data. Check your local law on research exemptions to data subject rights, which vary.
:::

**Child's assent.** The person taking consent explains the study to the child using the simple version below, without the parent answering for them.

- We want to learn about [[topic]], so we can [[make our activities better]].
- We will ask you some questions. It will take about [[30 minutes]].
- You do not have to answer anything you do not want to. You can stop whenever you like, and nobody will be cross.
- We will not tell anyone what you said with your name on it, unless we are worried you are not safe.

[ ] The child agreed to take part. Their words: [[record]]
[ ] The child did not agree. They will not take part.

::: adapt Research with children
Research with children usually needs ethics approval, a safeguarding plan and staff trained to talk with children. Interview children where they can be seen but not overheard, and never alone behind closed doors. A child's "no" is final, even if the parent agrees. Check your local law on the age at which young people can consent to research themselves; in some countries, older adolescents can, especially on health topics.
:::

# Annex: Guidance for staff taking consent

- Take consent before any data is collected, and in private. Never in front of community leaders, employers or family who might pressure someone.
- Allow time. Read the sheet aloud if needed. Ask the person to explain back, in their own words, what they are agreeing to.
- Do not link taking part to any service, distribution or registration. Do not collect data while people wait for help.
- Give the participant the information sheet, or a card with the contact details, to keep.
- Store signed forms separately from interview notes and recordings, so that names cannot be linked to answers.
- If someone discloses harm or abuse, follow the Safeguarding Policy and Procedure the same day.
- Record each participant's choices in the [[consent log]], so that you respect "no recording" or "no quotes" decisions during analysis.
