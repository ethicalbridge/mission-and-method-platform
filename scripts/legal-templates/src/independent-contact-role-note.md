# Why this role exists

In a small organisation, one person often leads the work, holds the money, manages the team and receives every safeguarding or whistleblowing concern. That creates a gap: if the concern is about that person, or they are involved, the reporter has nowhere safe to go.

The independent contact fills that gap. Our policies say that concerns about [[the founder / the director / the safeguarding lead]] ("the lead"), or cases the lead is involved in or conflicted about, go to the independent contact instead. The independent contact also covers urgent safeguarding cases when the lead cannot be reached for more than [[48 hours]].

# How to use this note

1. Adapt this note, then share it with the person you would like to invite.
2. Meet them, explain the organisation and the policies, and answer their questions.
3. If they agree, both sign the appointment letter at the end, and the board records the appointment.
4. Publish the contact details in every policy that refers to the role, and tell your team, partners and funders.
5. Review the arrangement every year.

# Who can be the independent contact

The independent contact should be:

- **independent of the lead:** not managed or paid by them, with no business, family or close personal link to them;
- **trusted:** someone people would feel safe telling about a sensitive concern;
- **experienced:** with sound judgement and, ideally, experience of safeguarding, human resources, law, audit or non-profit governance;
- **reachable:** able to reply within [[2 working days]], and the same day for anything urgent;
- **willing to keep things confidential** and to handle personal information carefully.

They should not be: a relative, partner or close friend of the lead; an employee of the organisation, or a contractor or consultant doing other work for it; a major donor, or a funder's officer managing our grant; or someone with a commercial relationship with the organisation.

::: adapt Choosing the person
Good options for a small organisation:

- a board member who is not an employee and is not close to the founder (common, and easy for funders to understand);
- a trusted professional outside the organisation, such as a retired lawyer, HR manager, auditor or safeguarding adviser;
- the leader or safeguarding lead of a peer organisation, under a reciprocal arrangement (you act for each other);
- a paid safeguarding or whistleblowing service, if your budget allows.

Think about language, time zone and gender: a reporter of sexual harassment may prefer to speak to a woman, for example. If you can, name a second person as back-up. If the founder is the only board member, or the board is made up of family members, this role is even more important, and a funder may ask about it.
:::

# What the independent contact does

1. **Receives** concerns sent to [[independent.contact@yourorganisation.org]], an address only the independent contact can read, or by [[phone]].
2. **Acknowledges** each concern within [[2 working days]], and the same day if someone may be at risk. They give a reference number and explain what happens next.
3. **Assesses** the concern: is anyone at risk now; which policy applies; does the law require a report to the authorities?
4. **Asks for protective steps** where needed: for example suspending the lead's access to a system, pausing an activity, or asking the lead to step aside from a decision. The organisation must act on these requests, and the lead cannot see the case while they are its subject.
5. **Makes sure the concern is handled fairly.** For anything serious, they appoint an experienced external adviser or investigator. They agree its terms of reference and receive its report.
6. **Refers** the matter to the police or other authorities where someone is at risk or the law requires it, taking care not to put a survivor at greater risk.
7. **Gives feedback** to the reporter, as far as confidentiality allows, and tells the board the outcome without identifying the reporter.
8. **Keeps a short record:** date, reference, what was raised, what they did, decisions and outcome. They keep it separately from the organisation's systems.

The independent contact also hears requests to review decisions made under our policies, where the original decision was the lead's.

# What the independent contact does not do

- They do not investigate alone or act as a judge. For anything serious, they bring in an experienced investigator.
- They do not run the organisation, manage staff, approve spending beyond the agreed limit, or speak for the organisation publicly.
- They do not take on the organisation's legal duties. Responsibility for safeguarding, employment and compliance stays with [[Organisation name]] and its board.
- They do not provide counselling or legal advice to anyone involved.
- They do not handle routine complaints or disputes that do not involve the lead.

# Covering when the lead is unavailable

If the safeguarding lead cannot be reached for more than [[48 hours]], for example because of illness, travel or leave, the independent contact covers urgent safeguarding cases. The lead tells the independent contact before any planned absence of more than [[5 working days]]. During cover, the independent contact follows the steps above and hands the case back when the lead returns, unless the lead is involved.

# Confidentiality and personal information

The independent contact shares what they learn only with those who need to know to keep someone safe or to handle the concern fairly. They:

- use a password-protected email account and devices not shared with others;
- do not use the information for any other purpose;
- keep records only as long as our Records Retention Schedule allows, then delete them securely, or hand them to the next independent contact;
- act on behalf of [[Organisation name]] for this purpose only, under our Data Protection Policy.

They may disclose information where the law requires it or where someone is at serious risk.

# Getting outside advice

The independent contact can get outside advice whenever they need it: from a lawyer, an HR or safeguarding specialist, or an external investigator. [[Organisation name]] pays for it. The independent contact can commit up to [[amount and currency]] per case without asking the lead. Above that, they agree costs with [[the board chair / another board member]], never with the lead if the lead is the subject of the case.

::: adapt Budget and advisers
Set a spending limit that is realistic for your context: enough for an initial legal consultation or a few days of an external investigator. Name in advance one or two advisers the independent contact can call, for example a local employment lawyer or a safeguarding consultancy, and check their rates. Some funders will cover investigation costs on projects they fund, or require you to use an investigator they approve: check your grant agreements.
:::

# Conflicts of interest

If the independent contact has a conflict in a particular case (for example, they know the reporter or the person concerned well), they tell the reporter, step aside and pass the concern to [[back-up independent contact / the board chair / an external adviser]]. They declare any interest that could affect the role when they are appointed, and each year, using the Board Interest Declaration if they are a board member.

# Time commitment and support

We expect [[a few hours a year]]: a briefing when appointed, an annual check-in and any cases that arise. A serious case may take several hours over a few weeks, mostly coordinating outside advisers. [[Organisation name]] will give the independent contact:

- a briefing on the organisation and its policies, and our safeguarding training [[or the offer of recognised external training]];
- payment of any outside advice or investigator they need;
- reimbursement of reasonable expenses under our Expenses and Per Diem Policy;
- an annual anonymised summary of concerns handled by the lead, so that they can spot patterns.

::: adapt Publishing contact details
Use a dedicated email address that only the independent contact can read. Do not use an address on the organisation's domain unless the lead has no administrator access to it; a separate account on a reputable email service may be safer. If the independent contact is outside your country, add a phone or messaging option that works locally. Update every policy and your website when the person changes.
:::

# Appointment letter

Dear [[Name]],

Thank you for agreeing to act as the independent contact for [[Organisation name]], from [[start date]]. This letter confirms the arrangement. The role is described in the attached Independent Contact Role Note, which forms part of this letter.

You will receive concerns about [[the founder / director / safeguarding lead]], and cases in which they are involved or conflicted, under our safeguarding, anti-harassment, fraud and whistleblowing policies. You will also cover urgent safeguarding cases when our safeguarding lead cannot be reached for more than [[48 hours]].

This is a [[voluntary / paid]] role. [[Organisation name]] will pay for any outside advice you need up to [[amount and currency]] per case without further approval, and will reimburse reasonable expenses. You will keep confidential everything you learn in the role, during and after your appointment, except where the law requires disclosure or someone is at serious risk. You act on behalf of [[Organisation name]] for this purpose only, and responsibility for our legal duties stays with us.

Either of us can end this arrangement with [[one month's]] written notice. We will name a new independent contact before the notice ends, and you will hand over any open cases and records securely.

::: adapt Legal status of the role
Decide whether the role is voluntary or paid, and whether a short contract is needed. If you pay the independent contact, check local tax and employment rules: in most countries an occasional advisory role is a service, not employment, but check. Confirm that your board or directors' liability insurance, if you have it, covers the independent contact when acting for you. Under the GDPR and similar laws, an independent contact acting for you is usually treated as part of your organisation, or as a processor acting on your instructions: check your local law and record the arrangement.
:::

# Review

We review this note and the arrangement every [[12 months]], when our team or structure changes, and after any case the independent contact has handled. The board records the appointment and each review in its minutes.
