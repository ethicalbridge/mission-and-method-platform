::: adapt Before you use this agreement
**Use it** for a significant gift: one above [[amount and currency]], a gift paid in instalments, a gift of goods (in kind), a gift for a specific purpose (restricted), or any gift that comes with naming or recognition. The donor may be an individual, a company or a foundation. Apply the checks in your Gift Acceptance Policy before you sign.

**Do not use it** for small one-off donations (a thank-you letter and receipt is enough), for a grant from an institutional funder that has its own contract, or for sponsorship where the company receives advertising or other commercial benefits in return. Sponsorship is a commercial deal and may be taxed differently.

**Keep it in proportion.** Delete the clauses you do not need. A cash gift with no conditions needs little more than clauses 1 to 3, 10, 12 and 17 to 19.

**Local law:** gift and tax rules vary a great deal. For very large gifts, gifts of land, property or shares, or gifts from abroad, ask a lawyer in [[country]] to review the final version, using the Legal Review Checklist.
:::

# Background

1. The Organisation is [[a short description, e.g. a non-profit organisation that runs community health programmes in X region]]. Its purposes are set out in its [[constitution / articles of association / statutes]].
2. The Donor wishes to support the Organisation's work by making the Gift described in Schedule 1, and the Organisation wishes to accept it on the terms of this agreement.
3. The Gift is a voluntary donation. The Donor receives nothing in return except the recognition and reports described in this agreement.

# Agreed terms

## 1. Definitions and interpretation

1. In this agreement:
  1. **Gift** means the money, goods or other assets described in Schedule 1, including each Instalment.
  2. **Gift in Kind** means any part of the Gift made in goods, equipment, services or other assets rather than money.
  3. **Instalment** means each payment or delivery set out in the payment plan in Schedule 1.
  4. **Purpose** means the use of the Gift stated in Schedule 1. If Schedule 1 says the Gift is unrestricted, the Purpose is the Organisation's general charitable or social purposes.
  5. **Restricted Gift** means a Gift, or part of a Gift, that the Donor asks to be used only for a specific project, place, activity or period.
  6. **Data Protection Law** means all laws on personal data that apply to either party, including [[e.g. the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 / the EU or UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) / Argentina Law 25.326]].
  7. **Working Day** means a day other than a weekend or public holiday in [[country or city]].
2. The schedule forms part of this agreement. If a clause conflicts with the schedule, the clause prevails.
3. "Including" means including without limitation. "In writing" includes email.

## 2. The Gift

1. The Donor gives the Organisation the Gift described in Schedule 1.
2. The Gift is **[[unrestricted / restricted to the Purpose in Schedule 1]]**.
3. The Gift becomes the property of the Organisation when it is received: for money, when it clears in the Organisation's bank account; for a Gift in Kind, when the Organisation accepts delivery under clause 13.
4. The Donor does not expect, and will not receive, any goods, services, advertising or other benefit in return for the Gift, apart from the recognition and reports in clauses 7 to 9.

## 3. Payment and delivery

1. The Donor will pay money by bank transfer to the Organisation's account in Schedule 1, in [[currency]], on the dates in the payment plan. The Donor pays its own bank charges.
2. The Organisation will confirm any change of bank account in writing, signed by [[two authorised signatories]], and the Donor will check it by telephone with the Organisation's contact before paying into a new account.
3. The Organisation does not accept single cash payments above [[amount and currency]].
4. The Donor will deliver any Gift in Kind to [[address]] on the date in Schedule 1, with the documents described there. [[The Donor / The Organisation]] pays the costs of transport, insurance in transit, customs duties and import taxes.
5. The Organisation will confirm receipt of each Instalment in writing within [[10]] Working Days.

## 4. Pledges paid in instalments

1. Where the Gift is paid in Instalments, the payment plan in Schedule 1 is the Donor's statement of intention to give, made so that the Organisation can plan its work.
2. If the Donor expects to pay late, or to stop or reduce payments, the Donor will tell the Organisation in writing as soon as possible, and at least [[60]] days before the next Instalment is due where it can.
3. If an Instalment is more than [[30]] days late, the Organisation may write to the Donor to ask whether it will be paid. If there is no reply within a further [[30]] days, the Organisation may treat the remaining Instalments as cancelled.
4. If the Donor stops or reduces payments:
  1. the Organisation keeps the Instalments already received and uses them for the Purpose;
  2. the Organisation may reduce, delay or end the restricted activity in proportion to the money received, and will tell the Donor what it has done;
  3. the Organisation will not start legal action to collect unpaid Instalments [[unless the Donor has signed this agreement as a deed and the board decides otherwise]]; and
  4. recognition under clause 7 may be adjusted in proportion to the amount actually given.

::: adapt Pledges and whether they can be enforced
Decide whether the pledge is a firm promise or a statement of intention. Most small organisations choose intention (as drafted), because suing a donor is rarely worth the cost to money and reputation. But a firm promise can matter if you are taking on staff or a building on the strength of it.

**Country notes:** in England and Wales a promise to give something for nothing in return is generally binding only if it is made as a deed, which has its own signing and witnessing rules. In many US states a written charitable pledge can be enforced, especially if the organisation relied on it. Some civil-law countries require a donation, or a promise to donate, of certain assets to be made in a notarised document or public deed. Check your local law before relying on any pledge.

Your accounts may also need to show pledges in a particular way. Ask your accountant how to record them.
:::

## 5. Use of the Gift

1. **Unrestricted Gift.** The Organisation may use an unrestricted Gift for any of its charitable or social purposes, as its board decides.
2. **Restricted Gift.** The Organisation will use a Restricted Gift only for the Purpose, within the period in Schedule 1, and will record it separately in its accounts, following its Financial Controls Policy.
3. **Core costs.** The Organisation may use up to [[10]]% of a Restricted Gift for the support costs that make the Purpose possible, such as management, finance, rent and IT.
4. **Timing.** The Organisation may hold a Restricted Gift until it is needed for the Purpose. [[Any interest earned on it will be added to the Gift / may be used for the Organisation's general purposes]].
5. **Underspend.** If part of a Restricted Gift is not needed for the Purpose once the Purpose is complete, the Organisation may use up to [[amount or percentage]] for [[a similar purpose / its general purposes]]. It will ask the Donor before using any larger amount.
6. **Overspend.** The Donor has no duty to cover costs above the Gift.

::: adapt Restricted or unrestricted
Unrestricted gifts are the most useful to a small organisation: they pay for staff, systems and the unexpected. Explain this to donors and invite them to give unrestricted, or at least to allow a share for core costs. A share of 10–15% is common; some donors accept more.

If the gift is restricted, describe the Purpose in Schedule 1 broadly enough to cope with change. "Girls' education in X district" is easier to deliver than "school fees for 40 named girls at one school". A narrow Purpose is more likely to fail, and then you need clause 6.
:::

## 6. If the Purpose cannot be met

1. If the Organisation reasonably believes that a Restricted Gift can no longer be used for the Purpose, because the Purpose has been met, has become impossible, unlawful or unsafe, or no longer serves the people it was meant for, it will tell the Donor in writing and explain why.
2. The parties will then try, in good faith and within [[30]] days, to agree one of these options in writing:
  1. **Vary** the Purpose, the timetable or the activity, keeping as close as possible to the Donor's original wishes;
  2. **Redirect** the unspent Gift to another programme of the Organisation that the Donor approves; or
  3. **Return** the unspent part of the Gift to the Donor, under clause 11.4.
3. If the Donor cannot be reached, has died or has stopped existing, or does not reply within [[60]] days of the Organisation's written notice, the Organisation may use the unspent Gift for a purpose as close as possible to the original Purpose, as its board decides and records.
4. The Organisation will not change the Purpose of a Restricted Gift in any other way without the Donor's written consent.

::: adapt When plans change
Clause 6.3 lets you act when the donor is no longer there to ask. Without it, money may sit unused for years.

**Country notes:** some legal systems have their own rules for changing the purpose of a charitable gift. In England and Wales, for example, charity law allows a gift to be applied to a similar purpose when the original one fails (the "cy-près" doctrine), sometimes only with the regulator's approval. Some countries require a foundation's or association's board, or a court, to approve such changes. Check your local law on restricted funds and changes of purpose. Also check that your constitution allows the Purpose you agree.
:::

## 7. Recognition, naming and anonymity

1. The Organisation will thank the Donor and recognise the Gift as the Donor chooses in Schedule 1: by name, in a chosen form of words, or anonymously.
2. **Anonymity.** If the Donor asks to remain anonymous, the Organisation will not publish the Donor's name or details. The Organisation will still record the Donor's identity internally, and may disclose it to its auditors, bank, regulators or where the law requires.
3. **Naming.** Where Schedule 1 gives the Donor a naming opportunity, such as a room, building, fund, scholarship or programme:
  1. the name will be used for [[number]] years, or for the life of the named item if shorter;
  2. if the item is replaced, rebuilt, merged or closed, the Organisation will try to recognise the Donor in a comparable way, but has no duty to keep the original name;
  3. naming depends on the Gift being paid in full, unless the parties agree otherwise in writing.
4. **Removing recognition.** The Organisation may remove or change any recognition, including a name, if continuing it would, in the board's reasonable opinion, seriously harm the Organisation's reputation, the people it serves or its relationships with other funders. It will first discuss this with the Donor where possible. This does not entitle the Donor to a refund.
5. The Organisation will give no other benefit in recognition of the Gift, such as advertising, exclusive rights, or preferential access to services or people.

::: adapt Naming and recognition
Naming is a valuable thank-you, but it is hard to undo. Set a time limit (10 or 20 years is common) rather than "for ever", and keep clause 7.4 so that you can act if a donor later becomes involved in a scandal. Agree the exact wording, size and position of any plaque in Schedule 1.

Large recognition packages, such as logos on vehicles or advertising, may turn a gift into sponsorship. In some countries that means the payment is taxed as income for services, or VAT is due. Ask a tax adviser if recognition goes beyond a simple thank-you.
:::

## 8. Use of names and logos

1. The Organisation may use the Donor's name, and [[logo]], only in the ways listed in Schedule 1, and will send the Donor any new public use for approval at least [[5]] Working Days before it is published.
2. The Donor may say publicly that it supports the Organisation, using the Organisation's name [[and logo]], in the form approved by the Organisation. The Donor will not use the Organisation's name or logo to endorse its products, services or political views, or in a way that suggests the Organisation endorses them.
3. Each party may withdraw its consent to future uses by written notice. Printed materials already produced may be used up.
4. Stories, photos and data about the people the Organisation serves are not shared with the Donor except as consented to by those people, using the Organisation's Fundraising and Communications Consent Form or a similar consent.

## 9. Reporting to the Donor

1. For an unrestricted Gift, the Organisation will send the Donor [[a thank-you letter and its annual report / a short update each year]].
2. For a Restricted Gift, the Organisation will send the Donor a short report [[every 6 months / once a year]] and a final report within [[3]] months of the end of the period in Schedule 1. Each report will describe what the Gift has made possible, the amount spent and any significant change or difficulty.
3. Reports will be proportionate to the size of the Gift. Unless agreed in Schedule 1, the Organisation does not have to provide an audit, receipts for individual items or information that would identify the people it serves.
4. The Organisation will tell the Donor promptly if it becomes aware of fraud, loss or serious misuse affecting the Gift, and what it is doing about it.

## 10. The Donor's confirmations

The Donor confirms that:

1. the Gift belongs to the Donor, and comes from a lawful source and not from crime, corruption, tax evasion or money laundering;
2. the Donor owns any Gift in Kind outright, free from any loan, charge or claim, and has the right to give it;
3. any Gift in Kind is, as far as the Donor knows, safe, legal and fit for its normal use, and the Donor has told the Organisation about any defect, risk or restriction on its use;
4. if the Donor is a company or foundation, the Gift has been approved by whoever must approve it under the Donor's own rules;
5. the Gift is not made to obtain any decision, contract, benefit or favourable treatment for the Donor or anyone connected with it;
6. the Donor has not attached, and will not later attach, conditions other than those written in this agreement;
7. the Donor has disclosed any connection with the Organisation's board, staff, partners or suppliers; and
8. the Donor has made the Gift freely, having had the chance to take independent advice, including on tax.

The Donor will tell the Organisation at once if any of these confirmations stops being true.

## 11. The Organisation's right to refuse or return a Gift

1. The Organisation accepts gifts under its Gift Acceptance Policy, which it will share with the Donor on request. It may decline all or part of a Gift before it is received, without giving detailed reasons.
2. The Organisation may decline future Instalments, or return a Gift already received, if:
  1. any confirmation in clause 10 is or becomes untrue;
  2. the Donor, or anyone who owns or controls it, is or becomes subject to sanctions under clause 15;
  3. accepting or keeping the Gift would breach the law, a condition of another funder, or the Gift Acceptance Policy; or
  4. the board reasonably decides that keeping the Gift would seriously harm the Organisation's independence, reputation or the people it serves.
3. The Organisation will tell the Donor in writing before returning a Gift, except where the law forbids this.
4. The Organisation will return money only to the account it came from, and only after it has cleared. It may deduct reasonable bank charges. It will not return money that has already been properly spent on the Purpose, unless it was received in breach of clause 10. It will not return money to anyone where the law requires it to report or freeze it instead.
5. The Organisation may sell, give away or dispose of a Gift in Kind it cannot use, after offering to return it to the Donor at the Donor's cost.

## 12. Tax receipts

1. The Organisation will issue the receipt or acknowledgement that the law of [[country]] allows or requires for the Gift, within [[30]] days of receiving it, if the Donor asks [[or automatically for gifts above amount]].
2. The Organisation makes no promise that the Donor can claim tax relief, a deduction or any other tax benefit. The Donor is responsible for its own tax position and should take its own advice.
3. If the Organisation loses its tax status, or the rules change, it will tell the Donor before receiving further Instalments.
4. The Organisation will not issue a receipt for more than the amount it received, or include the value of any benefit it gave in return.

::: adapt Tax receipts
Fill in only what is true for your organisation. Many countries let donors claim tax relief only on gifts to organisations that are registered or approved for that purpose, and only with a receipt in a set form.

**Country notes, as examples:** in the US, a donor needs a written acknowledgement from the organisation for a gift of USD 250 or more, and the organisation describes but does not value non-cash gifts. In the UK, Gift Aid lets a charity claim tax back on gifts from UK taxpayers who have signed a Gift Aid declaration. In Spain, organisations covered by Law 49/2002 issue a donation certificate. In Mexico, only authorised recipients (donatarias autorizadas) can issue deductible receipts. Rules on cross-border gifts are often stricter. Check your local law and the donor's country before promising anything.
:::

## 13. Gifts in kind

1. The Organisation will inspect a Gift in Kind within [[10]] Working Days of delivery and may refuse any item that is damaged, unsafe, unsuitable, past or close to its expiry date, or not as described.
2. Medicines, food, baby products, vehicles and electrical equipment must meet national standards and come with the documents listed in Schedule 1, such as certificates of origin, quality or roadworthiness.
3. **Valuation.** The Organisation does not value Gifts in Kind for the Donor. Any value given in Schedule 1 is the Donor's own estimate. The Organisation may record the Gift in its accounts at the fair value it decides under its accounting rules, which may differ. If the Donor needs a valuation for tax purposes, the Donor will obtain an independent one at its own cost.
4. Once accepted, the Organisation may use, store, transfer or, under clause 11.5, dispose of a Gift in Kind as it sees fit, consistent with the Purpose.
5. The Donor is responsible for any warranty, safety recall or licence linked to the goods, and will pass on the benefit of any manufacturer's warranty.

::: adapt Gifts in kind
Before you accept goods, ask what they will cost you: transport, customs, storage, installation, maintenance and disposal. Second-hand computers without licences, expired medicines and food that does not meet local standards cause real harm and cost. Agree in Schedule 1 who pays which costs.

Never write a value on a receipt that you have not checked. Inflated values for tax purposes can expose both you and the donor to penalties. In the US, for example, donors usually need a qualified appraisal for non-cash gifts above USD 5,000, and the organisation only signs to confirm receipt. Check your local law on recording and receipting non-cash gifts.
:::

## 14. Independence

1. The Organisation alone decides how it runs its programmes, whom it serves, whom it employs and what it says in public. The Donor has no right to direct or control the Organisation's operations, staff, partners, selection of the people it serves, or public statements, and will not seek to do so through the Gift.
2. The Donor will not receive personal information about the people the Organisation serves, or contact them directly, except through the Organisation and with their consent.
3. Any advice the Donor offers is welcome but not binding. Any visit to the Organisation's programmes will be arranged with the Organisation and follow its Safeguarding Policy and Procedure and Code of Conduct.
4. The Gift does not make the Donor a member, partner, employer or agent of the Organisation.

## 15. Anti-bribery, fraud and sanctions

1. Neither party will offer, give, request or accept a bribe, facilitation payment or any other improper advantage in connection with the Gift. The Organisation follows its Fraud, Bribery and Corruption Policy and Procedure. Laws with reach beyond borders, such as the UK Bribery Act 2010 and the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, may apply.
2. The Donor confirms that neither it, nor its owners, board members or senior managers, nor anyone acting for it, is on the UN Security Council Consolidated List or [[the US OFAC, EU, UK or national sanctions lists]], and that the Gift does not come from anyone who is. The Donor will tell the Organisation at once if this changes.
3. The Donor will give the Organisation the information it reasonably asks for to check the Donor's identity and the source of the Gift, in line with the Organisation's Partner Due Diligence and Sanctions Screening Policy and its duties under the law, including the risk-based approach to non-profits in FATF Recommendation 8.
4. The Organisation may report a suspicion to the authorities where the law requires it, without telling the Donor, and may delay accepting or returning a Gift while it does so.

## 16. Data protection and confidentiality

1. The Organisation is the controller of the personal data it holds about the Donor and the Donor's contacts. It will use that data to manage the Gift, thank and report to the Donor, issue receipts, carry out its checks and meet its legal duties, as described in its Privacy Notice, and in line with Data Protection Law and its Data Protection Policy.
2. The Organisation will contact the Donor about other fundraising appeals or events only as the Donor chooses in Schedule 1, and the Donor may change its mind at any time.
3. The Organisation will not sell or share the Donor's personal data with others for their marketing.
4. Each party will keep confidential the other's information that is marked confidential or is obviously confidential. The Organisation may share this agreement with its board, auditors, bank, regulators and, where relevant, other funders of the Purpose. [[The amount of the Gift will / will not be published in the Organisation's annual report.]]

::: adapt Donor data and public disclosure
Your Privacy Notice must explain how you use donor data, including any research you do on prospective major donors (for example, searching public sources about their wealth). Several data protection authorities have taken action against charities for wealth screening that donors were not told about. If you rely on consent for marketing, record it. Check your local law on direct marketing.

Some regulators or funders require you to publish large donations, or donors above a set amount. Political or campaigning organisations may have extra disclosure rules. Check what applies before promising confidentiality.
:::

## 17. Term and termination

1. This agreement starts on the date of the last signature below and continues until the Gift has been received and used, and the final report under clause 9 has been sent, unless it ends earlier under this clause.
2. Either party may end this agreement by written notice if the other commits a serious breach and does not put it right within [[30]] days of a written request. The Organisation may also end it at once under clause 11.
3. The Donor may end future Instalments under clause 4. This does not entitle the Donor to the return of any money already received.
4. When this agreement ends, the Organisation will deal with any unspent Restricted Gift under clauses 5.5 and 6, and stop using the Donor's name and logo, except in materials already published and in historical records.
5. Clauses 6, 7.2, 7.4, 10, 11.4, 12, 13.3, 14, 16, 18 and 19 continue after this agreement ends.

## 18. Disputes and governing law

1. The parties' contacts, and then [[the Organisation's chair and the Donor or its senior representative]], will try in good faith to settle any disagreement within [[30]] days. If it is still unresolved, they [[may / will]] try mediation through [[mediator or mediation centre]] before going to court.
2. This agreement is governed by the law of [[country or state]], and the courts of [[country or city]] have [[exclusive / non-exclusive]] jurisdiction.

::: adapt Law, disputes and signing
**Governing law:** usually the law of the country where the Organisation is registered. If the donor is abroad, check both countries' rules: some countries require the Organisation to register or report foreign donations, or to receive them in a designated account. For example, in India, foreign contributions are regulated by the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act 2010.

**Formalities:** check whether a gift of this kind must be signed before a notary, made as a deed, or stamped to be valid, especially for land, property, vehicles and shares, or where you want the pledge to be enforceable. Check whether electronic signatures are accepted for gifts.
:::

## 19. Notices and general terms

1. Formal notices must be sent by email to the other party's contact in this agreement, with a copy by post or courier to its address if they concern termination, a return of the Gift or a dispute.
2. This agreement, with Schedule 1, is the whole agreement about the Gift and replaces any earlier promises or discussions about it. Changes are valid only if made in writing and signed by both parties.
3. Neither party may transfer its rights or duties under this agreement without the other's written consent, except that the Organisation may transfer them to a successor organisation with similar purposes if it merges, restructures or closes.
4. No one else has rights under this agreement. Failing to enforce a right does not waive it. If any part is invalid, the rest continues.
5. This agreement may be signed in counterparts and electronically. If it is translated, the [[English]] version prevails.
