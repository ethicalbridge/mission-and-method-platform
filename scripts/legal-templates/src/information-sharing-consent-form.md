::: adapt Before you use this form
**When to use it:** whenever you want to share a person's information with another organisation for their benefit, such as a referral, and consent is the right basis. **When not to use it:** do not ask for consent if you would share anyway, for example to protect someone from serious harm or because the law requires it. Asking would mislead the person. Explain those cases instead, as section 6 does. **Free choice:** consent must be freely given. Make clear that saying no will not affect the support we give. **Children:** decide the age at which a child can consent for themselves; check your local law. Below that age, a parent or guardian consents, and the child's views are still heard, unless involving the parent would put the child at risk. **Language and literacy:** read the form aloud in the person's language and use the verbal consent record where needed. Name your data protection law, for example Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019, Brazil's LGPD or South Africa's POPIA, in your Privacy Notice.
:::

Name each organisation before you meet the person. Do not use general wording like "partner organisations": consent must be specific. If you refer regularly to the same organisations, sign a Data Sharing Agreement with each one, setting out how they protect the information. Never share through personal messaging accounts. Use the secure channel the agreement sets.

# 4. How long this consent lasts

Your consent lasts for [[12 months / until the referral is complete]], unless you withdraw it sooner. We keep this form for [[2 years]] after your consent ends, so that we can show what you agreed. The organisations we share with keep your information under their own rules, which they will explain to you.

# 5. How to withdraw your consent

You can withdraw your consent at any time, for any organisation, by telling any member of our staff, or by contacting [[Name, role]] at [[phone / email / office address]]. We will stop sharing from then on and tell the organisations concerned. We cannot take back information that we have already shared, but we will ask the organisation to stop using it where we can.

# 6. When we may share without your consent

In a few situations we may share information without asking you first, or even if you have said no:

- if we believe you, a child or another person is at serious risk of harm;
- if the law requires us to, for example a court order or a legal duty to report;
- to follow our Safeguarding Policy and Procedure.

If this happens, we share only what is needed, and only with the people who need it. Where it is safe to do so, we will tell you before or soon after.

::: adapt Sharing without consent
Check your local law on mandatory reporting. Many countries require certain people or organisations to report suspected child abuse, and some require reporting of other crimes or of certain infectious diseases. Describe the duties that apply to you in plain words, and align this section with your Safeguarding Policy and Procedure.
:::

# Annex: Guidance for staff taking consent

### Before the conversation

- Check which organisations you want to refer to, what each one needs, and that a secure way to share exists.
- Fill in the recipients table before you meet the person.
- Arrange an interpreter if needed. Avoid using family members, especially children, to interpret.
- Find a private place where the person cannot be overheard.

### During the conversation

- Explain the form in simple words. Go through each organisation and what you would share with it.
- Check understanding by asking the person to tell you, in their own words, what will happen.
- Make clear that saying no is fine and will not affect our support. Watch for signs that they feel pressured.
- Explain section 6 honestly, so that they are not surprised later.
- If the person seems unable to decide, for example because of distress, illness or the effects of alcohol, do not take consent. Come back later, or talk to your manager or the safeguarding lead.

### After the conversation

- Share only what was agreed, with only the organisations marked "Yes", using the secure channel.
- Record what you shared and when in the office use section.
- Store the form securely: in a locked cabinet or our [[case management system]], not on a personal phone.
- If the person withdraws consent, record it the same day and tell the organisations concerned within [[2 working days]].

::: adapt Children and people at risk
For children, explain the form in a way that suits their age, and record their view even when a parent signs. For survivors of violence, check whether calling or texting is safe before you record a phone number, and never send referral details to a shared phone. Add any rules from your referral network or protection cluster, for example standard inter-agency referral forms.
:::
