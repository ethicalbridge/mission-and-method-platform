# Who this policy applies to

This policy applies to everyone who travels for [[Organisation name]]: staff, volunteers, interns, board members, and consultants travelling on our behalf, unless their contract makes them responsible for their own safety arrangements. It applies equally to people from the country where the travel takes place and to people from elsewhere.

It covers all work travel: international trips, overnight travel within a country, and day visits to communities and field sites. It also covers visitors we invite, such as funders or partners, while we organise their programme, as far as we can reasonably expect.

**Law and stricter rules.** [[Organisation name]] is registered in [[country]] and follows its laws on health and safety at work. When people travel to other countries, we also follow the law that applies there. Where a partner, host or funder has stricter security rules, the stricter rules apply while you work with them.

::: adapt Law and duty of care
Name the country where you are registered. Most countries have occupational health and safety laws that place duties on employers, for example the UK Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974, South Africa's Occupational Health and Safety Act 1993 and Kenya's Occupational Safety and Health Act 2007. Check your local law on whether these duties also cover volunteers and work abroad. Duty of care can also be enforced through the courts: in 2015 a Norwegian court found a humanitarian organisation grossly negligent after a staff member was kidnapped, because risks had not been properly assessed and managed. ISO 31030:2021 gives international guidance on travel risk management, if you want a recognised benchmark.
:::

# Policy

## 1. Our duty of care

Duty of care means we take reasonable, practical steps to protect the health, safety and wellbeing of the people who travel for us. In practice, we will:

1. think about risks before every trip, and decide with you how to reduce them;
2. give you the information, briefing, equipment and insurance you need;
3. stay in contact while you travel, and act quickly if you do not check in;
4. respond to incidents and emergencies, and support you and your family afterwards;
5. never ask you to travel where we believe the risk is not acceptable;
6. learn from every incident and near miss.

You also have responsibilities: to follow this policy, the briefing and any local rules, to tell us about changes and concerns, and to look after your own safety and that of others.

## 2. The same care for everyone

Local staff, volunteers and community-based team members often face the greatest risks, yet in many organisations they receive less protection. We do not accept that. Everyone who travels for us, whatever their nationality, contract or role:

- receives the same risk assessment, briefing and check-in arrangements for the same trip;
- is covered by insurance that meets the same minimum standards;
- can raise safety concerns and refuse unsafe travel on the same terms;
- receives the same support after an incident.

Where a person's local knowledge or identity changes the risk, up or down, the assessment says so. We never assume that a local person is safe simply because they are local.

| High | Areas affected by conflict, kidnap risk, serious unrest or disease outbreak; areas where government advice is against all but essential travel | Full risk assessment reviewed by [[Name, role, or an external adviser]]; written security plan; check-ins at least [[twice a day]]; approval by [[Name, role]] |

We do not travel where government advice is against all travel, unless [[the board]] approves in writing, with specialist security advice and insurance that covers the trip.

## 4. Risk assessment before travel

The traveller and the focal point complete the risk assessment in Annex 1 together, at least [[5 working days]] before low and medium-risk travel and [[14 days]] before high-risk travel. It covers:

- the purpose of the trip, and whether it is necessary or could be done remotely;
- the security situation: crime, unrest, conflict, kidnap, terrorism, checkpoints;
- health: disease, access to medical care, vaccinations;
- transport and road conditions;
- accommodation and communications;
- risks linked to the traveller's identity, where the traveller chooses to discuss them (section 9);
- the controls we will put in place, and who is responsible;
- what we will do if something goes wrong: nearest hospital, safe places, evacuation routes and contacts.

If the risk changes during the trip, for example because of an election, protest or flood, the traveller and the focal point reassess it straight away. Either of them can decide to delay, change or end the trip.

## 5. Security briefings

Before medium and high-risk travel, the focal point, or a partner who knows the area well, briefs the traveller. The briefing covers the current situation, areas and times to avoid, local laws and customs, transport arrangements, accommodation, check-in times, emergency contacts and what to do in an incident. For high-risk travel we also brief on hibernation (staying in a safe place until it is safe to move), relocation and evacuation plans.

On arrival in an unfamiliar area, the traveller also receives a local briefing from our partner or host where possible.

## 6. Check-in procedures

Before you travel, share your itinerary using Annex 2: dates, routes, transport, accommodation, contacts and agreed check-in times.

| Risk level | Check-in | If a check-in is missed |
|---|---|---|
| Low | On arrival and at the end of the trip | Contact person tries to reach you within [[2 hours]] |
| Medium | Daily at [[time]], and on arrival and departure at each place | After [[1 hour]]: call and message you, then your travel companions and host. After [[3 hours]]: alert the focal point, who starts the emergency response |
| High | At least [[twice a day]] and before and after each journey | After [[30 minutes]]: call you and your companions. After [[1 hour]]: alert the focal point and [[Name, role]], who start the emergency response |

Check in by [[phone call / message / a shared location app agreed in advance]]. Agree a simple code word that means you are under pressure and need help, and keep it confidential. If you change your plans, tell your check-in contact before you move.

::: adapt Check-in arrangements
Choose check-in methods that work where you travel. Where mobile coverage is poor, agree fixed times and places with signal, or consider a satellite communicator for remote or high-risk travel. Shared location apps can help, but think about who can see the data, how long it is kept and whether it could put the traveller at risk if a device is lost or searched. Our IT and Information Security Policy applies.
:::

## 7. Insurance

We provide insurance for everyone who travels for us, including volunteers and interns. It covers at least:

- emergency medical treatment and hospital costs;
- medical evacuation and repatriation;
- personal accident;
- travel disruption and loss of belongings, up to [[amount]];
- [[24-hour assistance line: insurer name and number]].

Before each trip, the focal point checks that the insurance covers the destination and the activities planned. Many policies exclude areas where government advice is against travel, or events such as war or civil unrest. If the policy does not cover the trip, the trip does not go ahead until suitable cover is arranged.

Each traveller carries the insurance details and assistance number, on paper and on their phone.

::: adapt Insurance
Ask a broker or insurer who understands non-profit travel. Check that national staff and volunteers are covered, not only international staff, and whether local health insurance or national schemes already cover some risks. For high-risk areas, specialist cover may be needed, including for kidnap and crisis response: if you hold such cover, keep its existence confidential, as many insurers require. Check your local law on whether certain insurance, such as workers' compensation, is compulsory for employees.
:::

## 8. Health

- Check health advice for your destination at least [[6 weeks]] before international travel. We pay for required vaccinations and preventive medicines, such as malaria prevention.
- Carry a basic first aid kit and enough of any regular medicines for the whole trip, plus extra days, with prescriptions.
- Tell the focal point, in confidence, about any health condition, disability or pregnancy that could affect your safety or need adjustments. You choose what to share. We use it only to plan support.
- Do not travel if you are unwell. Tell your manager, and we will adjust the plan.
- Travel can be tiring and stressful. Take the rest you are entitled to, and tell us if the work is affecting your wellbeing.

## 9. Personal safety

### General precautions

- Follow the briefing and local advice. Respect local laws, dress and customs.
- Keep a low profile: avoid displaying valuables, and avoid demonstrations, political events and large gatherings.
- Carry a charged phone, emergency contacts, a copy of your identity document and some local cash in a separate place.
- If you are robbed or threatened, hand over what is asked. Nothing you carry is worth your life.
- Use accommodation approved in the risk assessment, with secure doors and, where possible, a room above the ground floor.
- Protect information on your devices. Do not carry sensitive data you do not need for the trip.
- Follow our Code of Conduct at all times, including during free time.

### Risks linked to who you are

Some risks depend on a person's identity or how others see them: for example gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, ethnicity, religion, nationality, disability or age. A place that is low risk for one person can be higher risk for another. We handle this with care:

- **You never have to disclose personal information.** We give every traveller information about identity-related risks for the destination, so you can decide what is right for you without having to tell us why.
- **If you choose to discuss it,** the conversation is confidential. Only the focal point and, if you agree, the approver will know. We record only what is needed for the risk assessment.
- **Women travellers** may face specific risks, including sexual harassment and assault. We consider accommodation, transport, travelling alone, night-time movement and the availability of female colleagues, translators or drivers where helpful.
- **LGBTQ+ travellers** may face legal and social risks. In some countries, same-sex relationships or certain forms of gender expression are criminalised, and devices, messages or dating apps may be searched. We share this information with every traveller, and support any adjustment you ask for.
- **You can decline** a trip because of an identity-related risk, without giving details, and it will not affect your role or opportunities. We will look for an alternative, such as remote work or a different team member, without assumptions.

::: adapt Identity-related risks
Keep this section, even in a small team: these risks are real and often overlooked. The Global Interagency Security Forum (GISF) publishes free guidance on managing the security of aid workers with diverse profiles. ILGA World publishes regular reports on laws affecting LGBTQ+ people by country. Check current sources before each trip, as laws change. Never ask travellers to disclose their sexual orientation, gender identity or other sensitive characteristics, and never record them without explicit consent: in many data protection laws, this information is special category or sensitive personal data.
:::

## 10. Road safety

Road crashes are one of the most common serious risks to people travelling for work. We:

- use vehicles that are roadworthy, with working seat belts, and drivers who are licensed, rested and do not drive under the influence of alcohol or drugs;
- require everyone to wear seat belts, and helmets on motorcycles;
- avoid road travel after dark outside towns in medium and high-risk areas, unless approved in the risk assessment;
- limit driving to [[8 hours]] a day, with breaks at least every [[2 hours]];
- never ask a driver to speed to keep to a schedule; anyone in the vehicle can ask the driver to slow down or stop;
- do not use phones while driving, unless hands-free and safe;
- choose reputable transport companies, and avoid overloaded or unsafe vehicles.

If you use a motorcycle taxi, it must be [[allowed in the risk assessment / not used in medium and high-risk areas]], and you must wear a helmet.

## 11. Your right to refuse unsafe travel

You can refuse to travel, or stop a trip and return, if you believe the risk to yourself or others is not acceptable. Tell the focal point as soon as you can. You will not be penalised, disciplined or treated less favourably for doing so in good faith. We will discuss your concern, and either change the plan or find another way to do the work. Managers must never pressure anyone to travel or stay.

::: adapt Right to refuse
Some employment and health and safety laws give workers an express right to withdraw from serious and imminent danger without penalty. Check your local law, and make sure your Employment Contract and Volunteer Agreement do not contradict this section.
:::

# Procedure: incidents and emergencies

## 1. If something happens

1. **Get safe.** Move away from danger if you can. Get medical help if anyone is hurt.
2. **Call for help.** Contact local emergency services on [[number]] if needed, then our 24-hour number, [[number]]. If you need medical help abroad, also call the insurer's assistance line.
3. **Report.** Tell the focal point what happened, where you are, whether anyone is hurt and what help you need.
4. **Follow instructions** from the focal point, local authorities and the insurer.

## 2. How we respond

{widths: 8 47 22 23}
| Step | What happens | Who | Time limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receive the alert, confirm the person's location and needs, and log the time | Focal point or deputy | Immediately |
| 2 | Arrange immediate help: medical care, safe transport, safe accommodation, insurer assistance | Focal point | Within [[1 hour]] |
| 3 | Inform [[Name, role]] of any serious incident | Focal point | Within [[2 hours]] |
| 4 | Contact the traveller's emergency contact, with their agreement where possible | Focal point | As agreed, and within [[24 hours]] for serious incidents |
| 5 | Decide whether others need to change plans, hibernate, relocate or leave the area | Focal point and [[Name, role]] | Same day |
| 6 | Record the incident and report it as set out in our Incident Reporting Procedure | Focal point | Within [[24 hours]] |
| 7 | Tell funders, insurers or authorities where agreements or the law require it | [[Name, role]] | As required |
| 8 | Offer support: medical follow-up, counselling, time off, practical help | Line manager | Within [[1 week]] |

For incidents involving sexual violence, follow a survivor-centred approach: the person affected decides what happens next wherever possible, receives medical care quickly (including emergency contraception and HIV post-exposure prophylaxis, which work best started as soon as possible and within 72 hours), and is offered confidential support. Our Safeguarding Policy and Procedure applies where relevant.

If a traveller is missing, detained or kidnapped, [[Name, role]] leads the response with specialist advice. We do not speak to the media, post on social media or contact the people involved directly without that advice.

::: adapt Emergencies and evacuation
Prepare in advance for your most likely serious incidents: road crashes, medical emergencies, robbery, detention, sudden unrest or natural disasters. List the nearest good hospital, the insurer's assistance number and your embassy or consular contacts for each area where you work. If you work in high-risk areas, consider a short contract with a security adviser or membership of a security network, and agree in advance who would lead a crisis response.
:::

# After travel

Within [[5 working days]] of returning from medium or high-risk travel, or after any incident, the traveller and the focal point hold a short debrief. It covers what went well, any incidents or near misses, any change in the risks and lessons for future trips. The focal point updates the risk assessment for the area and our Risk Register where needed.

We also ask how the traveller is. Difficult trips can affect wellbeing even when nothing goes wrong. We offer access to [[support, e.g. counselling or an employee assistance service]] where needed.

# Roles and responsibilities

- **Travellers** complete risk assessments, attend briefings, follow this policy, check in on time and report incidents and concerns.
- **The travel safety focal point, [[Name, role]],** reviews risk assessments, gives briefings, keeps the travel log, holds check-ins and leads the response to incidents.
- **The deputy, [[Name, role]],** covers the focal point and approves their travel.
- **Line managers** make sure travel is necessary, approve low-risk trips and support travellers before and after.
- **[[Name, role]]** approves high-risk travel and leads the response to serious incidents.
- **The board** approves this policy, approves any travel against government advice, and receives a summary of incidents and near misses at least [[once a year]].

# Records, confidentiality and data

We keep risk assessments, itineraries and check-in logs for [[2 years]], and incident records for [[6 years]] after the incident, or longer if legal proceedings or insurance claims are ongoing, as set out in our Records Retention Schedule.

Travel records include personal data such as emergency contacts and locations, and sometimes sensitive information about health or identity. We collect only what is needed, store it securely, share it only with those who need it to keep the traveller safe, and delete it on time. Our Data Protection Policy applies.

# If this policy is breached

If someone travels without the required approval, ignores a briefing or misses check-ins without reason, the focal point discusses it with them and their manager. Serious or repeated breaches that put people at risk may be dealt with under our Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure. A manager who pressures someone to undertake unsafe travel is also in breach. Concerns can be raised through our Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure.

# Monitoring and review

The focal point reviews the travel log and all incident reports every [[quarter]]. We review this policy at least once a year, when we start working in a new area, and after any serious incident or near miss.
