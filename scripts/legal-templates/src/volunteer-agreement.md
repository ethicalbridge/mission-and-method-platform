::: adapt Before you use this agreement
**Use it** for anyone who gives their time to [[Organisation name]] without pay: regular and occasional volunteers, unpaid interns, students on placement, and board members who also help with day-to-day work. **Do not use it** for anyone you pay beyond their actual expenses, or who must work set hours in return for a benefit. They may legally be workers or employees: use the Employment Contract or Independent Contractor Agreement instead.

**This agreement is deliberately written as mutual expectations, not as a contract of employment.** It uses words such as "we hope", "we will" and "we ask you", and avoids "duties", "pay", "obligations" and "disciplinary action". Keep that tone when you adapt it. That is also why the main section is called "Our mutual expectations" rather than "Agreed terms". Only clauses 8, 9 and 10 (confidentiality, data protection and intellectual property) are intended to be binding.

**Volunteering laws differ.** Some countries have a specific volunteering law, for example Brazil (Law 9.608 of 1998, which requires a signed term of adhesion), the Philippines (Volunteer Act of 2007, Republic Act 9418), Colombia (Law 720 of 2001) and Peru (General Law on Volunteering, Law 28238): check whether yours has one, and whether it requires a written agreement or specific content. Elsewhere, courts apply general employment tests. Get local advice if your volunteers work long or regular hours, receive allowances or are recruited from abroad.
:::

::: adapt Warning: when a volunteer becomes a worker
In many countries, a volunteer can legally become a worker or employee, with rights to minimum wage, leave, social security and protection from dismissal. The main risk factors are:

- **Payments beyond actual expenses:** flat daily allowances, stipends, "pocket money", honoraria or payments that do not match receipts.
- **Benefits in kind given in return for work,** such as valuable training unrelated to the role, accommodation or meals beyond what the role requires, or free services.
- **Contractual obligations:** a duty to work set hours, minimum commitments enforced by penalties, or disciplinary procedures.
- **Promises of a job:** telling volunteers or interns that unpaid work will lead to paid employment.
- **Doing the same job as paid staff,** under the same control, for long periods.

Country notes: in the UK, someone who receives more than genuine expenses or is bound to work in return for a benefit may be a "worker" entitled to the minimum wage; the National Minimum Wage Act 1998 has a limited exemption for voluntary workers in charities and voluntary organisations. In the US, people generally cannot volunteer for a for-profit business under the Fair Labor Standards Act; non-profits can have volunteers, but stipends and "nominal fees" need care. Internships are regulated in several countries: in France, for example, internships of more than two months must carry a minimum payment, and in the UK unpaid interns doing real work may be entitled to the minimum wage. Where no specific rule exists, courts usually look at payment, control and obligation. Check your local law on volunteers and interns.
:::

# Background

1. [[Organisation name]] works to [[one-line mission]]. Volunteers are central to how we do this.
2. You have chosen to give your time to us as a volunteer, in the role described in Schedule 1.
3. This agreement sets out what you can expect from us, and what we hope for from you, so that your volunteering is safe, rewarding and well supported.

# Our mutual expectations

## 1. About this agreement

1. This agreement is a statement of mutual expectations between you and us. It is not a contract of employment, and you are not our employee or worker.
2. Except for clauses 8, 9 and 10, it is binding in honour only and is not intended to create legal obligations on either side.
3. You are free to stop volunteering at any time, and we are free to stop offering you volunteering opportunities. Neither of us will be penalised for doing so.
4. You will not receive any payment, reward or benefit for your volunteering, apart from reimbursement of actual expenses under clause 5 and the support in clause 4.
5. Volunteering with us does not guarantee future paid employment. If you apply for a paid job with us, your application will be considered fairly alongside all others.

::: adapt Which parts are binding
Rules on whether a volunteer agreement can bind either side vary. In common-law countries, a promise given without anything in return may not be enforceable as a contract unless it is signed as a deed, while civil-law countries treat unpaid agreements differently. Data protection and confidentiality duties may also apply through the law, whatever this document says. If confidentiality or intellectual property really matters (for example, volunteers designing your brand or handling sensitive case files), consider a separate signed confidentiality undertaking or deed, using our Mutual Confidentiality Agreement as a starting point. Check your local law on volunteer agreements.
:::

## 2. Your role

1. Your volunteer role is described in Schedule 1. It sets out what the role involves, where it takes place and who supports you.
2. We will agree any change to your role with you. You are welcome to suggest changes, or to try a different role.
3. You will not be asked to do work that replaces a paid member of staff, or to take on responsibilities beyond those in the role description without discussing it first.
4. You will not represent [[Organisation name]] publicly, sign agreements for us, handle our money or make commitments on our behalf, unless your role description says so and your supervisor has agreed.

## 3. Time and flexibility

1. We hope you can volunteer for about [[number]] hours a [[week / month]], on [[days or times]], for a period of [[number]] months. This is a guide to help us plan, not a requirement.
2. Please let your supervisor know as early as you can if you cannot come, or wish to change your hours. We understand that study, work, family and health come first.
3. We will not ask you to volunteer for more than [[number]] hours in a week, or to work late at night or alone in unsafe situations.
4. If you wish to take a break from volunteering, for example during exams or for family reasons, please tell us. Your place will be kept open where we can.

## 4. What we offer you

1. **Induction:** within your first [[two weeks]], an introduction to our work, team, safety arrangements, Code of Conduct, Safeguarding Policy and Procedure and Data Protection Policy.
2. **Training:** any training you need to do your role safely and well, at no cost to you.
3. **Supervision and support:** a named supervisor, regular check-ins at least every [[month]], and someone to talk to if anything worries you.
4. **Expenses:** reimbursement of actual, agreed out-of-pocket expenses, as set out in clause 5.
5. **Insurance:** cover under our [[public liability / personal accident]] insurance while you are volunteering for us, as described in clause 11.
6. **Recognition:** we will thank you, involve you in decisions that affect your role, and listen to your ideas.
7. **References:** on request, a reference or certificate describing your role and the time you gave, after [[three months]] of volunteering.
8. **A safe and respectful environment:** free from harassment, discrimination and bullying, as set out in our Code of Conduct.

## 5. Expenses

1. We reimburse the actual cost of expenses you incur while volunteering for us, where you have agreed them in advance with your supervisor, for example:
  1. travel by public transport, or by your own vehicle at [[rate per kilometre]] for agreed journeys;
  2. meals when you volunteer for more than [[number]] hours, up to [[amount and currency]]; and
  3. phone or internet costs, materials or other agreed items.
2. Please submit a claim with receipts within [[30 days]], using [[form or system]]. Where a receipt is not available, for example for some local transport, tell your supervisor the actual cost and the reason.
3. We pay claims within [[14 days]] by [[bank transfer / mobile money]].
4. We do not pay flat allowances, per diems, stipends or any amount that is not linked to an actual cost.
5. Expenses follow our Expenses and Per Diem Policy, as it applies to volunteers.

::: adapt Expenses: keep them real
Reimburse actual costs only. A flat daily payment, even if small, is the most common way a volunteer becomes a worker in law. If receipts are hard to obtain, for example for motorbike taxis or local buses, keep a simple log of actual costs signed by the supervisor. Some tax systems allow approved mileage rates or modest meal amounts without tax: check your local tax rules. If you want to provide lunch or transport directly, do so for everyone at the activity, rather than as a payment to each volunteer.
:::

## 6. What we ask of you

1. Follow our Code of Conduct, Safeguarding Policy and Procedure, Data Protection Policy and any other policy your supervisor tells you applies to your role.
2. Treat everyone with dignity and respect: colleagues, other volunteers, partners and the people we serve.
3. Take part in the induction and any training needed for your role.
4. Tell your supervisor if you have any concern about your role, your safety, or the wellbeing of anyone you meet through our work.
5. Declare any conflict of interest, for example if a family member applies for support or a contract from us.
6. Do not accept gifts or payments from anyone you meet through your volunteering, except small tokens worth less than [[amount and currency]].

## 7. Safeguarding

1. Your role [[does / does not]] involve contact with children or adults at risk.
2. Where it does, before you start we will ask for:
  1. [[two]] references, including at least one from someone who knows you in a work, volunteering or study setting;
  2. a self-declaration of any criminal record or safeguarding concern; and
  3. [[a criminal record or police clearance check appropriate to the role]].
3. If a check is not yet complete, you will volunteer only under supervision.
4. If you see, suspect or are told about harm, abuse or exploitation, report it within [[24 hours]] to [[safeguarding lead, contact]]. If someone is in immediate danger, contact emergency services first on [[number]].
5. Please tell us promptly if you are charged with or convicted of any offence that could affect your suitability for your role.
6. If we receive a safeguarding concern about you, we may pause your volunteering while we look into it. Pausing is a protective step, not a finding of wrongdoing.

::: adapt Safeguarding checks for volunteers
Volunteers should meet the same safeguarding standards as staff in equivalent roles. Use criminal record checks only where the role justifies them, and follow your local rules: for example, the UK uses Disclosure and Barring Service checks; Kenya issues a Certificate of Good Conduct; many countries issue police clearance certificates. If a volunteer is under 18, get written consent from a parent or guardian, follow child labour rules on minimum age and hours, and never place them in a role with unsupervised responsibility for others.
:::

## 8. Confidentiality

1. During your volunteering you may learn confidential information about the people we serve, our staff and volunteers, our partners, funders and finances.
2. You agree to keep this information confidential, during and after your volunteering, and to use it only for your volunteer role.
3. This does not stop you from reporting a concern under our Safeguarding Policy and Procedure or Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure, or to the authorities, or from disclosing information when the law requires it.
4. We intend this clause to be binding, as far as the law allows.

## 9. Data protection

1. We will process your personal data, such as your contact details, emergency contact and any check results, only to manage your volunteering, keep you and others safe, and meet our legal obligations, as explained in our Privacy Notice.
2. You agree to handle any personal data you see in your role in line with our Data Protection Policy, including:
  1. accessing only the information you need for your role;
  2. not copying personal data to your own devices, accounts or messaging apps unless your supervisor has approved it;
  3. not putting personal or confidential information into AI tools or other online services we have not approved; and
  4. reporting any loss or accidental sharing of data to [[role]] within [[24 hours]].
3. We intend this clause to be binding, as far as the law allows.

## 10. Intellectual property, photos and stories

1. If you create materials for us as part of your role, such as reports, designs, photographs, training materials or social media content, you [[assign the copyright to us / give us a free, permanent, worldwide licence to use, adapt and publish them]]. Where the law allows, you waive any moral rights that would prevent us adapting them. We will credit you where it is practical.
2. Never take or share photographs, videos or stories of the people we serve unless you follow our Photo and Story Consent Form process and your supervisor has agreed.
3. If you would like us to use your own name, photograph or story in our communications, we will ask for your separate consent, which you can withdraw at any time.
4. We intend sub-clauses 1 and 2 to be binding, as far as the law allows.

## 11. Health, safety, wellbeing and insurance

1. We will tell you about any risks in your role and how to avoid them, and give you any safety equipment you need.
2. Please follow safety instructions, look after yourself and others, and report any accident, near miss or hazard to your supervisor on the same day.
3. Our insurance with [[insurer]] covers volunteers for [[public liability: injury or damage you cause to others / personal accident: injury to you]] while you are volunteering in an agreed role. It does not cover [[your own vehicle / personal belongings / activities outside your role]].
4. If you volunteer away from your usual location, or in another country, we will follow our Travel Safety and Duty of Care Policy and brief you before you go.
5. If volunteering affects your wellbeing, please talk to your supervisor or to [[another named person]]. You can always step back from a task that feels unsafe.

::: adapt Insurance
Check with your insurer that volunteers are covered, and for what. Many policies cover staff but not volunteers unless you ask. Consider public liability (injury or damage caused to others), personal accident (injury to the volunteer) and cover for driving, if volunteers drive. In some countries employers' liability insurance is compulsory for employees but does not automatically include volunteers. If you have no insurance yet, say so honestly, and limit volunteer roles to low-risk activities until you do.
:::

## 12. Equipment and accounts

1. We may lend you equipment or give you access to our systems, such as a laptop, ID badge or email account. Please look after them and use them only for your volunteer role.
2. Do not share passwords, and lock any device that holds our information.
3. When your volunteering ends, please return all equipment and documents within [[seven days]]. We will close your access to our systems on your last day.

## 13. Volunteers from other countries

1. If you are volunteering in a country where you are not a citizen or resident, you are responsible for making sure you have the right visa or permit, and we will provide the letters and information you reasonably need.
2. We will not ask you to volunteer if we know that it would breach your visa conditions.
3. Before you travel, we will brief you on local laws, customs, health and security, and confirm your insurance arrangements.

::: adapt International volunteers
Many countries treat volunteering as work for immigration purposes. A tourist visa often does not allow it, and some countries have special volunteer or charity worker visas. Check the rules of the host country before accepting an international volunteer, and never encourage someone to volunteer on the wrong visa. If you host remote volunteers working from their own country, local volunteering and data protection law there may also apply.
:::

## 14. Problems and complaints

1. If you are unhappy about any aspect of your volunteering, please talk to your supervisor first. Most problems can be sorted out informally.
2. If that does not resolve it, or the problem is about your supervisor, contact [[name, role]] at [[email]]. They will listen, look into the matter and reply within [[14 days]].
3. You can also use our Complaints and Feedback Procedure, or for concerns about wrongdoing, our Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure. Harassment and safeguarding concerns are handled under the relevant policy.
4. If we have concerns about your volunteering, your supervisor will talk to you about them openly, listen to your view and agree how to move forward. We may suggest extra support, a different role or, if needed, ending the volunteering arrangement.
5. If there is a serious concern, such as a safeguarding allegation, fraud or harassment, we may pause your volunteering straight away while we look into it.

## 15. Ending your volunteering

1. You can stop volunteering at any time. We would be grateful if you could give your supervisor [[two weeks']] notice, so that we can plan, and hand over any work in progress.
2. We may stop offering you volunteering opportunities, for example if a project ends, your role is no longer available, or there are concerns that we cannot resolve. Where we can, we will discuss this with you first and give you reasons.
3. When you leave, we will invite you to share feedback on your experience, and confirm what reference we can give.
4. Clauses 8, 9 and 10 continue after your volunteering ends.

## 16. Review

1. We will review your role and this agreement with you after [[three months]], and then at least [[once a year]].
2. We review this template at least [[once a year]] and after any significant change in the law or in how we work with volunteers.
