MODULE 01

Clarity: Strategic Foundation

Vision, mission, values and objectives that guide real choices.

READING 1 · 8 MINPurpose, vision and mission are different jobs+

Purpose names the larger reason the work matters. Vision describes the future condition you hope to help create. Mission defines your organisation’s contribution: whom it serves, what it does and the change it pursues. Treating these as interchangeable creates attractive language but weak direction.

BoardSource’s current nonprofit guidance emphasises that the statements should be distinct but connected. Academic review also cautions that mission statements are common but their value depends on how they are developed and used—not simply whether they exist. A useful test is operational: could a team use the statement to reject an attractive activity that does not fit?

Apply it now

Write three separate sentences: the future you want to see; the contribution your organisation makes; and the boundary that explains what you will not do.

SourcesBoardSource, Mission, Vision, Values and Purpose (2025)Alegre et al., systematic review of mission-statement research (2018)
READING 2 · 7 MINValues must become observable behaviour+

A list of abstract nouns does not yet guide behaviour. A usable value contains a principle, the behaviour it requires and the trade-off it governs. “Inclusion” becomes operational when it specifies who must be involved before a decision and what accessibility standard the organisation will follow.

Purpose-led organisations create an implicit promise to employees and communities. Recent research warns that purpose can backfire when rules, metrics and everyday processes prevent people from acting on it. Values therefore need evidence in recruitment, budgets, meetings, partnerships and performance conversations.

Apply it now

For each value, complete: “We demonstrate this when…” and “We would refuse…” Keep only values that change a decision.

SourcesBoardSource, values as ethical guidelines (2025)Nielsen et al., When Purpose Backfires (HBR, 2026)
READING 3 · 9 MINFrom aspirations to strategic objectives+

Strategic objectives translate identity into a limited set of intended results. External objectives describe change for beneficiaries, customers, communities or systems. Internal objectives build the capacity required to produce that change—people, finance, technology, governance and learning.

Good objectives specify a result and direction without prematurely becoming task lists. Research on organisational effectiveness shows why a balanced view matters: financial and non-financial measures, and the needs of different constituencies, all contribute to effectiveness.

Apply it now

Draft three external and three internal objectives. For each, name the stakeholder, desired change, evidence and time horizon. Remove activity verbs such as “run” or “deliver”.

SourcesBoardSource, Strategic Planning GuideKaur & Sood, systematic review of organisational effectiveness (2023)
MODULE 02

Theory of Change

Explain why activities should contribute to meaningful change.

READING 1 · 9 MINA Theory of Change is an argument, not a diagram+

A Theory of Change explains how an intervention is expected to contribute to a chain of results. The arrows matter only when they represent a plausible causal claim. A diagram without a narrative, assumptions or evidence is a results list—not yet a theory.

Current BetterEvaluation guidance distinguishes change theory—the mechanism through which people, organisations or systems change—from action theory—how activities activate that mechanism. Strong theories also acknowledge context, other actors and unintended outcomes.

Apply it now

Choose one arrow and finish: “We expect A to contribute to B because… Evidence for this is… This may fail if…”

SourcesBetterEvaluation, Manager’s Guide (2026)UNODC Evaluation Handbook (2024 edition)
READING 2 · 8 MINBuild backwards from impact+

Start with the long-term condition you want to influence, then identify the outcomes that must exist immediately before it. Continue backwards until the pathway reaches outputs and activities under your control. Backwards mapping exposes missing preconditions and prevents activity-first planning.

Distinguish outputs—the direct products of activity—from outcomes—changes in behaviour, capacity, practice, relationships or conditions. Your organisation controls delivery but usually only contributes to outcomes and impact.

Apply it now

Ask “what must be true immediately before this?” three times. Mark every condition outside your control and name the partner or contextual factor involved.

SourcesNPC practical guide via BetterEvaluationActKnowledge, Facilitator’s Source Book
READING 3 · 8 MINAssumptions, evidence and revision+

Assumptions are conditions believed necessary for a causal link to hold. They can concern motivation, access, quality, policy, partner behaviour or context. Making them visible turns a static diagram into a testable strategy.

A Theory of Change should be revised as evidence and context evolve. BetterEvaluation recommends drawing on research, prior evaluations, stakeholder mental models and observed results—not workshop opinion alone.

Apply it now

Rank assumptions by importance and uncertainty. High-importance, high-uncertainty assumptions become research, monitoring or pilot priorities.

SourcesOECD, Strengthening Evaluation Practice (2026)BetterEvaluation, evidence and revision guidance (2026)
MODULE 03

Team, Structure & Coordination

Align structure, authority, work and the employee journey.

READING 1 · 8 MINStructure follows strategy and work+

An organigram should not begin with job titles. Begin with work required to deliver the strategy, group related responsibilities, then define roles and reporting relationships. CIPD’s 2025 guidance frames organisation design as aligning structure with strategy and examining systems, people practices, measures, policies, processes and culture together.

Apply it now

List essential work in three groups: mission delivery, enabling work and governance. Create role clusters before assigning people.

SourcesCIPD, Organisation Design (2025)ACNC Governance Standards
READING 2 · 8 MINRoles need outcomes, authority and interfaces+

A useful role description defines results the role owns, decisions it can make, information it needs, relationships it manages and issues it must escalate. Governance sets direction, oversees risk and ensures accountability; management organises people and resources to execute.

Apply it now

For every role, write: three owned outcomes; three decision rights; three essential interfaces; and one escalation threshold.

SourcesACNC Governance HubCIPD, organisation design process (2025)
READING 3 · 7 MINOnboarding is social and strategic+

Onboarding should help a new person understand purpose, relationships, expectations and ways of working—not merely complete administration. CIPD’s 2024 research found that organisations reviewing hybrid onboarding commonly strengthened early introductions, social connection, buddy support and understanding of culture.

Apply it now

Build a 30-day plan with four outcomes: understands the mission, knows key relationships, can perform core work and knows how to ask for help.

SourcesCIPD, Resource and Talent Planning Report (2024)Systematic review of newcomer onboarding tools (2024)
MODULE 04

Organisation Internal Systems

Brand, communication and MEAL as connected operating systems.

READING 1 · 7 MINBrand is a promise made consistent+

A purpose-led brand is not primarily a logo. It is the consistent relationship between identity, claims, behaviour and stakeholder experience. Start with audience needs and values, then define the promise, voice, proof and visual rules that make the organisation recognisable.

Apply it now

Write a one-sentence promise, three proof points and three voice principles. Test whether each public claim has evidence.

SourcesBoardSource, aligning identity and purpose (2025)ACCC, claims guidance
READING 2 · 8 MINCommunication starts with decisions, not channels+

Choose communications by stakeholder decision: what should this person understand, feel or do? Then select message, messenger, format, channel and frequency. Internal communication also needs rules for what is documented, where the authoritative version lives and when feedback is expected.

Apply it now

Map five audiences. For each, name one desired decision, one barrier, one message, one trusted messenger and one feedback route.

SourcesCIPD, operating models and engagement (2024)ACNC Governance Hub
READING 3 · 10 MINMEAL puts evidence into decisions+

Monitoring tracks implementation; evaluation makes systematic judgements; accountability gives affected people voice and recourse; learning converts evidence into changed practice. CRS describes MEAL as a project-cycle system, not a donor-reporting appendix.

Connect each outcome to a small number of indicators, sources, responsibilities and review moments. Collect data only when someone can explain the decision it will inform.

Apply it now

For one outcome, define one quantity indicator, one quality indicator, one feedback channel, one learning question and the meeting where evidence changes a decision.

SourcesCRS, MEAL Policies and Procedures (2024)Science for Africa Foundation, ten MEAL steps (2024)
MODULE 05

Policies & Procedures

Right-sized governance for finance, compliance, risk and data.

READING 1 · 8 MINA policy sets the rule; a procedure makes it repeatable+

A policy states intent, scope, principles, responsibilities and controls. A procedure states the sequence, evidence and escalation needed to perform work consistently. Prioritise by legal obligation, potential harm, financial exposure and operational frequency.

Apply it now

Create a policy register. Score candidates 1–5 for legal duty, harm, financial exposure and frequency. Draft the highest total first.

SourcesISO 37301:2021, Compliance management systemsACNC Governance Standards
READING 2 · 9 MINFinancial control protects mission and trust+

Early-stage controls should separate request, approval, payment and reconciliation where possible. Where headcount prevents full separation, add compensating oversight. Australian charities must keep records explaining receipts, spending, financial position and performance.

Apply it now

Map one payment. Mark who requests, approves, pays, records and reconciles. Where one person performs two roles, add independent review.

SourcesACNC, 2025–26 Regulatory FocusACNC Governance Hub
READING 3 · 10 MINRisk and privacy must be designed in+

ISO 31000 treats risk as an integrated cycle: identify, analyse, evaluate, treat, monitor and communicate uncertainty in relation to objectives. Privacy begins before collection: know what data you hold, why, where, who can access it and when it is deleted.

Apply it now

Choose one critical process. Identify its highest people, financial and data risks; controls; accountable owner; treatment deadline; and evidence.

SourcesISO 31000:2018, current risk guidelineOAIC, Data Breach Preparation and Response (2024)OAIC, NDB guidance (2025)
MODULE 06

Strategy to Action

Activities, owners, milestones, dependencies and useful schedules.

READING 1 · 8 MINDecompose outcomes into deliverable work+

A work breakdown structure organises full scope into deliverables and manageable work packages. PMI emphasises that it should be deliverable-oriented, created with the people doing the work and account for the full scope.

Apply it now

Choose one objective. Define 3–5 observable deliverables. Break each into work packages small enough to estimate, assign and review.

SourcesPMI, current standards libraryPMI, Effective Work Breakdown Structures
READING 2 · 8 MINOwnership is a decision architecture+

Every activity needs one accountable owner, even when several people contribute. Clarify who decides, performs, is consulted and needs information. Pair ownership with acceptance criteria: what evidence shows the deliverable is complete and who accepts it?

Apply it now

For five priority activities, name one owner, contributors, decision authority, completion evidence and escalation date.

SourcesPMI, Managing and Leading Small ProjectsCIPD, alignment of structure and work (2025)
READING 3 · 9 MINA Gantt chart is a conversation about dependencies+

A useful schedule makes duration, sequence, dependencies, milestones and resource constraints visible. Build it only after scope and ownership are understood. Review the critical path and near-term commitments frequently.

Apply it now

Map dependencies before dates. Identify tasks that cannot start until another finishes and the three estimates carrying most schedule risk.

SourcesPMI, Practice Standard for SchedulingJo et al., Gantt-chart task taxonomy (2024)
MODULE 07

Business Model

Connect purpose, value, delivery, cost and income.

READING 1 · 8 MINSeparate beneficiary, customer and funder+

In purpose-led models, the person receiving value may not be the person paying. Name beneficiaries, customers, funders and influential partners separately, then map the value exchange for each. The OECD documents the diversity of legal forms and business models in the social economy.

Apply it now

For each stakeholder, write what they give, what they receive, why they participate and what would make them leave.

SourcesOECD, Social and Solidarity Economy review (2023)OECD, Social Economy Data (2024)
READING 2 · 9 MINDesign the economic engine around impact+

A model links value proposition, delivery, channels, capabilities, partnerships, costs and revenue. Purpose-led design adds: does growth strengthen intended outcomes, or can revenue expand while impact weakens? Build safeguards into the model rather than relying on intention.

Apply it now

Trace one dollar of revenue through delivery to outcome. Mark every assumption and point where financial and impact incentives could diverge.

SourcesUNDP, Growing Your Business methodologyOECD, social-economy model diversity
READING 3 · 8 MINTest the riskiest assumption before building+

A canvas is a set of hypotheses. Rank assumptions by importance and uncertainty, then choose the smallest ethical test that produces decision-quality evidence. Record the decision rule before collecting evidence.

Apply it now

Name your most dangerous assumption. Write a test, target group, evidence threshold, deadline and the decision if it fails.

SourcesUNDP, impact-driven business developmentOECD, business-model opportunities and constraints (2024)
MODULE 08

Fundraising, Sponsors & Partnerships

Build a resilient income portfolio and respectful relationships.

READING 1 · 9 MINDiversification is fit—not maximum variety+

Individual giving, grants, corporate partnerships, membership, sponsorship and B2B income require different capabilities and time horizons. Diversification should reduce concentration risk without scattering scarce capacity. Select strategies based on mission fit, market, net potential, acquisition cost, renewal and ethical risk.

Apply it now

Score each income stream 1–5 on mission fit, readiness, net potential, time to revenue, renewal and concentration risk. Pursue the top two.

SourcesAustralian Productivity Commission, Future Foundations for GivingACNC, Australian Charities Report (2025)
READING 2 · 8 MINRetention begins with the first experience+

Acquisition is not success if supporters do not remain. Research using large-platform data found that first-time donors exposed to a failed fundraising experience were substantially more likely to stop giving. Design the relationship after the gift before launching the ask.

Apply it now

Map the first 90 days: acknowledgement, expectation setting, progress evidence, listening opportunity and next meaningful action.

SourcesKellogg, donor-retention research (2024)Donor capital and donor-experience governance (2025)
READING 3 · 10 MINBuild different cases for different partners+

An institutional donor assesses strategic fit, evidence, governance and compliance. A corporate partner also considers employee, customer and brand value. A B2B buyer needs a defined problem, service, outcome, price and procurement fit. The same generic pitch cannot serve all three.

Apply it now

Select one institutional, corporate and B2B prospect. Write a distinct case for each and name the evidence each needs before saying yes.

SourcesACNC Governance StandardsAustralian Productivity Commission, giving channelsOECD, stakeholder diversity
