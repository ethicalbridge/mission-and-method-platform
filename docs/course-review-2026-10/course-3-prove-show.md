# Course 3 · Prove & Show — panel review

Panel emphasis: MEAL, brand strategy, communications and marketing, ethical storytelling (with learning design and plain-language editing throughout).

## Verdict

Course 3 is a sound, current and well-intentioned course. The MEAL content follows mainstream good practice (MEAL DPro, OECD criteria, CHS), and the communications content keeps coming back to evidence, consent and dignity, which is rare and valuable. Its biggest strength is the evidence thread: honest baselines (6.4), honest reporting (6.11), proof points (7.2, 8.3) and outcome-based communications measurement (8.13) make one argument, "only claim what you can show". Its biggest problem is workload and sequencing, not content. There are 32 taught lessons and 66 required fields at "20 minutes" each. Several exercises ask for full tables typed into a text box (6.3 has a 10-column indicator sheet). Lessons ask learners to define things before they are taught (7.2 asks for personality and key messages; 6.6 assembles a MEAL plan before monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning are covered). A founder at an early stage will either stall or invent answers, which goes against the course's own rule in 6.4: "do not invent it".

Scores 1–5: **Clarity 4** (concepts well defined, few gaps) · **Readability 3** (FRE 46 on average; 6.5, 6.8, 7.7 and 8.12 read like reference text) · **Depth 3** (enough to understand, too thin for the hardest exercises: indicators, evaluation, consent) · **Practicality 3** (good exercises, but time estimates and table fields are unrealistic) · **Flow 3** (M7 and M8 build well; M6 order and cross-module duplication confuse) · **Professional credibility 4** (accurate and ethical; a few over-claims in examples and gaps in consent practice)

## Keep (what works — do not lose this)

- **The "proportionate" principle in 6.1** ("five good indicators reviewed every quarter learns more than one with fifty indicators nobody analyses") and the decision-first exercise ("Decision | who makes it | when | what evidence they need"). This is the best MEAL advice a small organisation can get.
- **6.4 Baselines and targets**: "If you do not know the baseline, do not invent it… Record 'to be established by [date]'" and "Revise targets openly, with a reason, rather than quietly." Excellent, honest and practical.
- **The evidence thread across modules**: 6.11 → 7.2 proof points → 7.7 trust signals → 8.3 proof points → 8.13 outcome measures. It turns MEAL into something communications can use.
- **8.4 Ethical storytelling**, especially "Your organisation, not the person, should take the reputational risk of saying less", the right to withdraw, showing people the final version, and the consent log.
- **Reflect prompts** are consistently sharp: "Which of your indicators could improve while the real situation gets worse?" (6.3), "What would stop a participant from complaining about your organisation?" (6.9), "How would your organisation speak to someone having the worst day of their life?" (7.3), "Which metric do you report that does not change any decision?" (8.13).
- **Weak/strong pairs** that teach one clear point: 6.11 ("transformed hundreds of lives" vs a specific, hedged result with a barrier and a change), 8.1, 8.9, 8.13, 7.8 (approval scaled to risk).
- **Global South awareness** where it appears: WhatsApp and short visits (8.2), low-data notices and internet costs (8.6), legal limits on political activity (8.9), AfrEA principles and Africa No Filter in the resources.
- **The 7.1 distinction** between brand, identity, image, communications and marketing. It is clear and short and clears up a common confusion.

## Module 06 · MEAL: Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability & Learning

### Verdict
Technically the strongest module in the course and credible to an experienced MEAL officer. The problems are order (the integrating MEAL plan comes before half its parts), the heaviest exercises (6.2–6.5) being under-scoped and under-timed, and two dense lessons (6.5 FRE 27, 6.8 FRE 24) that use terms without defining them.

### Keep
- 6.1 proportionality and decision-linked data; the chain "Theory of Change → results framework → indicators → data → learning → decisions".
- 6.3 strong example (numerator/denominator, disaggregation, "reached at follow-up") and the trap list ("double counting the same person").
- 6.4 in full. 6.7 "ask why before reacting… Separate the explanation from the response". 6.10 "Treat negative findings as useful information".
- 6.11 "Distinguish outputs from outcomes, contribution from attribution, and evidence from aspiration."

### Adjust
- **High · Reorder the module.** 6.6 says the MEAL plan "brings everything together: … evaluation plans, accountability mechanisms and reporting", yet 6.7–6.11 come after it. Proposed order: 6.1 System → 6.2 Results framework → 6.3 Indicators → 6.4 Baselines and targets → 6.5 Data collection and ethics → **6.6 Accountability** (feedback is a data source) → 6.7 Monitoring → 6.8 Learning → 6.9 Evaluation → 6.10 Reporting → **6.11 MEAL plan and budget** (assemble) → review.
- **High · 6.5 ethics gaps.** Add the voluntary-consent sentence (course table). Add response bias: "People may tell you what they think you want to hear, especially if they depend on you. Have someone outside the delivery team ask, or offer an anonymous option." Add a sampling rule: "If you work with fewer than about 100 people, try to hear from all of them. If more, ask a random selection (for example every fifth name), not only those easiest to reach." For children, add "a parent's or guardian's consent **and** the child's own agreement".
- **High · 6.3 scope and consistency.** Exercise: "Define three to five indicators for the results in your 6.2 framework, at least one of them qualitative." P3 lists "method" and "direction" but the field hint omits them. Use the same list in both, and put the full list in Excel.
- **Medium · Merge the 6.7 and 6.10 routines.** Both ask for a meeting routine. Make 6.7 "Monitoring and the quarterly review" (dashboard + routine) and 6.8 "Learning and adapting" (after-action review, lessons log, first review record). 6.8 then reuses the 6.7 routine instead of asking for it again.
- **Medium · 6.7 decision rule for traffic lights.** Add: "For example, green: 90% of plan or more; amber: 70–89%; red: below 70%. Agree your thresholds before you see the data."
- **Medium · 6.8 proportionality.** Add a rule: "If you are under two years old or have no large funder requirement, plan a light internal review. Commission an external evaluation when a funder requires it or a big decision (scale up, stop, replicate) depends on it."
- **Medium · 6.9 inclusion.** Add one sentence: "Check each channel against literacy, language, disability, phone access and gender. Some women will only raise concerns with a woman." The exercise covers feedback but not participation, so add to the "loop" hint: "one decision participants will help make this year".
- **Medium · 6.11 baseline ambiguity.** "52%… (baseline 31%)": 31% of whom? At intake nobody is in work. Rewrite as "(previous cohort: 31%)".
- **Low · 6.6** "MEAL budget of 7% of programme costs": add "a common rule of thumb is 5–10%".

### Add / cut / merge / move
- Move 6.6 to the end and 6.9 to sixth position (above). Merge the routine fields of 6.7/6.10.
- Add one social-enterprise example (6.3 or 6.5), for example "Share of solar-lamp customers who stopped buying kerosene, by SMS survey at three months".

### Lesson notes
- **6.1 P2** — "product analytics": add "(how customers use your product)".
- **6.2** — add the "No Theory of Change yet?" fallback; P3 rewrite: "Check two things. Up the chain: if we deliver these outputs and our assumptions hold, will the outcomes follow? Across each row: can we actually get evidence for this?"
- **6.3 P1** — "sensitive to change" → "likely to move when the real situation changes".
- **6.5 P3** — rewrite (only three of the "five checks" are defined now): "Check data quality in five ways. *Valid*: it measures what it claims to. *Reliable*: different people collect it the same way. *Timely*: it arrives in time to use. *Precise*: detailed enough for your decision. *Intact*: nobody has changed or lost it."
- **6.8 P1** — rewrite: "Monitoring asks 'Are we doing what we planned?' Evaluation asks harder questions: did things change, for whom, why, at what cost, and what happened that we did not intend? Many evaluators use six questions (the OECD criteria): Is it the right thing to do? Does it fit with other work? Does it work? Is it good use of money and time? What difference does it make? Will it last?"
- **6.8 P2–P3** — define "contribution story" ("a reasoned account of how your work helped, alongside other factors"), "terms of reference" ("the brief for the evaluator") and "management response" (strong example). Field "approach" says "Formative or summative" but P2 names four types; use all four.
- **6.11 P2** — define in place: "Attribution means claiming your work alone caused a change; contribution means showing it was one of the reasons."

## Module 07 · Brand, Identity & Image

### Verdict
Clear, short and right-sized. It treats brand as trust and experience, not logos, which suits purpose-led organisations. The main problems are a field that asks for content taught later (7.2), overlap between 7.6 and 7.8, and missing safety and multilingual points that matter in the Global South.

### Keep
- 7.1 definitions and "A strong brand is built more by consistent experience than by a logo."
- 7.2 "Promises we will not make" field: unusual and valuable.
- 7.3 "this, not that" pairs and the people-first word list.
- 7.5 accessibility as part of identity, and "A simple, consistent system applied everywhere beats a sophisticated one used inconsistently."
- 7.7 "Never overstate affiliations, endorsements or results", with a verifiable strong example.
- 7.8 approval scaled to risk.

### Adjust
- **High · 7.2 field asks for later content.** "Purpose | promise | audiences | personality | key messages | proof points": personality is 7.3, audiences 8.2, messages 8.3. Change it to "Purpose (from 1.1) | promise | proof points". The review assembles the full platform.
- **High · 7.6 co-branding safety.** Add: "In some places, showing a foreign funder's or partner's logo can put staff or participants at risk. Agree low-visibility or no-visibility options with funders in advance; many accept them."
- **Medium · 7.1 exercise/field mismatch.** The exercise says "Describe how your brand, identity, image and communications differ", but the fields are "Current image" and "Desired brand". Rewrite the exercise: "Write what people think of you now, with evidence, and what you want them to expect." Add a quick method to the hint: "Ask five people from different groups: What do we do? Who is it for? What one word describes us?"
- **Medium · 7.5 imagery consent duplicates 8.4.** Field "how consent is obtained" → "what your images show and what you will never show; consent follows your 8.4 rules". Also add multilingual identity: "If you work in more than one language or script, test your logo and typeface in each."
- **Medium · 7.4 is premature for many founders.** Open P1 with: "If you have one name and no separately named programmes, write 'Single brand' and note when you would revisit this." Add to the naming checks: "not already used by a registered organisation in your country".
- **Medium · 7.7 readability (FRE 20).** Turn P2 into a checklist. Add an alternative for informal groups without registration: "fiscal host, named community referees, published annual summary".

### Add / cut / merge / move
- **Merge 7.6 and 7.8** into "Brand guidelines and governance". Both cover a shared folder, owner and templates; 7.8 has no resources. Fields: outline, templates and owners, co-branding, approval rules. This saves one lesson (~20 min).
- Add the "which statement is which" table to 7.2 (see Module 8).

### Lesson notes
- **7.2 strong example** — write it as a promise: "Bring it in and we will tell you honestly, before we start, whether it is worth fixing and what it will cost."
- **7.3 strong example** — "care leavers in crisis" is UK usage; add "(adapt to the terms your community uses)".
- **7.5 P2** — "codes for print and screen" → "colour codes for screens (HEX) and for printing (CMYK)"; add "logo files in SVG and PNG, and a version that reads clearly as a small WhatsApp profile image".
- **7.6 P2** — "do's and don'ts" → "dos and don'ts".
- **Module promo** on 7.8 offers comms tools (Planner, Persona Builder) for a brand lesson. Move the promo to 8.x and drop it from Module 7, or reframe it as "templates".

## Module 08 · Marketing & Communications

### Verdict
Comprehensive, modern and ethical, with a strong start (objectives linked to strategy, evidence-based personas, proof points) and a strong finish (outcome measurement). At 13 taught lessons and ~4.6 hours it is the longest module in the course. Overlapping lessons (social/content, social rules/policy) and lessons not every founder needs yet (community, media) make it feel like a list of channels rather than a plan.

### Keep
- 8.1 "Start from your objectives, not from channels" and the strong example with audience, action and a strategy link.
- 8.2 personas "based on conversations and data, not stereotypes".
- 8.4 in full (see Adjust for additions).
- 8.7 the three-email welcome journey: concrete and immediately usable.
- 8.9 campaign brief and the one-action call to action; legal limits on advocacy.
- 8.13 the three levels (outputs, engagement, outcomes) and the decision-making strong example.

### Adjust
- **High · 8.4 consent depth.** Add a consent checklist to P2 or the field hint. "Before you record or publish, the person knows: what you will use; where (website, social media, funder reports, media); for how long; that saying no will not affect their support; how to withdraw, and that content already shared online may not be fully removable; who to contact. Ask in their language; record verbal consent if written is not suitable. For children, get a guardian's consent and the child's own agreement." Also add to P1: "Make the person or community the main actor in the story, not your organisation or donors."
- **High · 8.3 proof point over-claims.** "Proof point: 52% in work or training at six months" drops the hedge used in 6.11 ("of those we reached"). Rewrite: "52% of graduates we reached six months later were in work or training." This is the module that teaches honest claims, so its own example must be honest.
- **Medium · Statement confusion.** Add to 8.3: a table with Mission (what we do, for whom, why; built in 1.5) · Positioning (why us; 1.8) · Brand promise (what people can always expect; 7.2) · Core message (how we explain ourselves in one breath; 8.3) · Case for support (why fund this now; 11.17).
- **Medium · 8.6 strong example does not match the exercise.** It describes partners and a notice, not social channels. Replace it: "WhatsApp Channel for participants (weekly reminders; programme assistant replies within 24 hours); LinkedIn for employers (two posts a month); Facebook page kept with a pinned post pointing to WhatsApp." Add account security: "two admins and two-step login".
- **Medium · 8.7 relevance.** Retitle "Email, messaging and supporter journeys". Add: "In many places WhatsApp or SMS reaches more people than email; the same consent and opt-out rules apply." Cross-reference 11.19 Stewardship.
- **Medium · 8.12 timescales and forward link.** In the strong example, "within 24 hours" → "within the time your regulator and agreements require". Add to P2: "Coordinate any statement with your safeguarding lead; never name or blame individuals publicly." Add "You will add these scenarios to your risk register in Module 10."
- **Low · 8.1 strong example** objective 2 lacks a date and action. Rewrite: "by December, 300 people receive our monthly update and 40 give monthly".

### Add / cut / merge / move
- **Group into parts** (already supported by `part:` in kit, as used in M10/M11): A. Plan (8.1–8.4) · B. Channels (8.5–8.8) · C. Reach and relationships (8.9–8.11) · D. Protect and measure (8.12–8.13).
- **Merge 8.6 and 8.8** into "Social media and your content calendar". Channel plan + pillars + two weeks of calendar. Saves one lesson and removes the duplicate "content types/frequency".
- **Move the social media rules** field from 8.6 to 8.12 so social media policy lives in one place.
- **8.10 Community**: keep it, but open with "Most useful once you have a group of past participants, alumni or regular supporters", and link it to 6.9 (participation) and 4.8 (volunteers).

### Lesson notes
- **8.2 P1** — make the audience list a bullet list; FRE 38.
- **8.5 title/P3** — define the acronym: "search engine optimisation (SEO): helping people find you through search". Add "a free business profile on map services". Add "a privacy notice on every form".
- **8.8 calendar field** — "One month of content" → "Your next two weeks of content".
- **8.9 P2** — define "insight": "what you know about the audience that explains why they have not acted yet". Add "Fundraising appeals and their budgets: see 11.10."
- **8.11 strong example** — keep it; add "brief and support any participant spokesperson before and after the interview".
- **8.12 P1** — split the long risk list into bullets; FRE 31.
