# Course 1 · Clarify — panel review

## Verdict
Clarify is a sound, honest and genuinely useful start: the sequence (profile → purpose → context → stakeholders → vision/mission/values → form → positioning → objectives → Theory of Change) is the right one, the weak/strong examples are sharp, and the tone ("not a test", "contribution, rather than attribution", "take qualified local advice") matches the brand. The single biggest strength is that every lesson ends in a concrete, saved decision that later modules reuse (objective codes ESO1/ISO1, one pathway per external objective, the health check repeated in 13.9). The single biggest problem is conceptual load for a first-time founder: the course asks for five overlapping "big statements" (purpose, vision, mission, impact, external objectives) and up to nine objectives — each needing its own Theory of Change pathway — without ever showing one complete worked example or a rule for telling these statements apart. Module 00, the free first impression, also contains factual errors about the course structure and promises (videos, personalised examples) that the product does not yet deliver. All of this can be fixed with editing, not rewriting.

**Scores 1–5:** Clarity 3 (good definitions, but purpose/vision/mission/impact blur) · Readability 3 (FRE 40–58, long list-sentences, undefined acronyms such as MEAL in Module 00) · Depth 3 (Module 1 is enough; 2.3, 2.5–2.7 are too thin for 20 minutes and there is no complete ToC example) · Practicality 4 (decision-oriented exercises, good field hints) · Flow 3 (right overall order; duplication between 1.5/1.8 and 1.1/1.2/2.2; workload explodes in Module 2) · Professional credibility 4 (current sources, contribution logic, legal caution; a few over-simplifications and Global South gaps).

## Keep (what works — do not lose this)
- **Decision-first exercises:** "Name one evidence gap" (1.1), "What will you postpone" (1.9), "decision if false" (2.8). This separates the course from a template pack.
- **The weak/strong pairs**, e.g. "We will register as an NGO because funders like NGOs" (1.7) or "Version 1.2, March: after testing with six participants…" (2.9). They teach in one glance.
- **Honesty about limits.** "Contribution, rather than attribution" (2.3 P3); "Your claims should become more modest as you move outwards" (2.1 P3); "avoid inventing numbers you cannot yet support" (1.9 P4); "Do not register an entity only because a template or funder suggests it" (0.2 P4). Experienced NGO managers will find this credible.
- **External vs internal objectives with codes** (1.9) and the rule "Internal objectives do not need their own pathway, but they appear as inputs and conditions" (2.1 P4). This is the spine of the whole Planning System.
- **Early risk flags:** 0.2 'activities' hint — "Note any work with children, vulnerable adults, personal data or money from the public."
- **The health-check scale** (0.3 P2), "Many organisations have documents that nobody practises", and its repeat in 13.9 — a before/after arc for the whole programme.
- **Module 2's method:** backward mapping, the three spheres (2.1 P3), the 'unintended' field (2.4) and the review-page pathway strip.
- **Resource selection** is strong (ODI, BetterEvaluation, Hivos, CIVICUS Monitor, Bertha Centre and ASEAN legal-form guides — good Global South spread).

## Module 00 · Start Here: Your Organisation Journey
### Verdict
The content of 0.2 and 0.3 is excellent and sets the honest, practical tone of the brand. As a first impression, though, the module opens with 230 words about course structure (with factual errors), an undefined acronym and a subscription upsell, and ends without telling the learner what their health check means for them. It needs a warmer, shorter opening and a clear "your next step" ending.

### Keep
- 0.2's four fields, especially 'activities' (safeguarding/data flags) and 'questions' ("Mark anything undecided as an open question rather than guessing").
- 0.3's "It is not a test and there is no passing score", the scale definitions, and "Involve at least one other person… Differences in ratings are useful information."

### Adjust
- **High — 0.1 P2 course structure is wrong.** Use the five real course names (see course table).
- **High — Upsell on the very first lesson of the free module.** `promo.lesson:'journey'` puts a four-tool subscription box on 0.1, and the Module 00 page shows the same four tools twice ("Impact Tools · optional subscription" and "Tools for this module"). Move the promo to 0.4 (review) or remove it from Module 00. Show the tools once.
- **High — 0.1 has no welcome or promise of what the learner will have at the end.** Add a new first paragraph: "By the end of the five courses you will have a written foundation for your organisation: a strategy, a Theory of Change, a structure, core policies, a budget, a MEAL plan and a one-year operating plan. Each lesson takes 20–40 minutes and saves your answers as you go." Also add one sentence on saving: "Your work is saved in this browser. Use 'Export course backup' before changing device."
- **High — 0.3 health-check areas omit safeguarding,** although P3 and the strong example both use it. Add "safeguarding" and "data protection" to the 'ratings' hint list (and to the 13.9 repeat).
- **Medium — 0.1 exercise asks newcomers to judge modules by name.** A first-time founder cannot yet choose between "MEAL" and "Internal systems". Replace the 'priority' hint with a short route table in P3: "If funders are asking for evidence → Courses 1 then 3. If you are about to hire → Courses 1 then 2. If you are about to register or handle money → Courses 1 then 4." This also answers the honest question of learners who buy only one course: name the minimum prerequisites (0.2, 1.9 and 2.1–2.4).
- **Medium — 0.1 weak/strong example lectures the learner** ("copy a template"). Replace with an organisation example: weak "We need a fundraising strategy now"; strong "We need funding, but first we must agree what change we are funding (Module 1) and how it happens (Module 2); we'll reach Module 11 in about six weeks."
- **Medium — 0.3 stage label "Review"** is confusing on day one. Use "Understand" (it is a baseline), or label it "Review (baseline)".
- **Medium — 0.4 review gives no interpretation.** Add a closing prompt to the review page: "Your three priorities point to these courses: …" (static text mapping areas → courses is enough). This is the natural, honest bridge from the free module to Course 1.

### Add / cut / merge / move
- **Add** a "Words we use" glossary (one screen) as part of 0.1 or a short unnumbered page — this is where second-language readers will decide whether the course is for them.
- **Cut** 0.1 'rhythm' as a required field, or make it optional: it is useful but not a quality output.

### Lesson notes
- 0.1 P1: split the 55-word list sentence: "The course follows the order in which organisations make decisions. First, why you exist and what change you pursue. Then who does what and how you run. Then how you show results, stay solvent and compliant, find resources and partners. Last, one plan you run and review."
- 0.2 P2: "a company has directors and shareholders" — add "(a nonprofit company usually has members, not shareholders)" to avoid confusion with 1.7's nonprofit company.
- 0.3 P3: "A trained focal person, a referral route people know about" → "a named, trained person people can go to, and a known way to pass concerns to the right local service".
- 0.3 'ratings': 14 lines in one box. Hint should say "Use the Health check sheet if easier; paste only areas rated 'not started' or 'informal' here, with evidence."

## Module 01 · Strategic Foundations
### Verdict
The strongest module of the course: well-chosen lessons, clear decision filters and practical fields. Its weaknesses are overlap between statements (purpose/vision, mission/positioning), an inconsistency in the stakeholder grid, a 9-objective ceiling that is too high for small organisations, and SWOT placed where idea-stage founders have little to say.

### Keep
- 1.1 P2 "Separate the need from your preferred solution… 'We need an app' names a solution."
- 1.3 P2 "who benefits, who pays, who decides and who influences" — a genuinely useful distinction, especially for social enterprises.
- 1.7's four questions (control, surplus, funding, reporting) and the strong example that reasons from resourcing.
- 1.8 "Be honest: 'passion' and 'innovation' are not advantages unless you can show what they produce."

### Adjust
- **High — 1.1 vs 1.4 examples are near-duplicates.** Make 1.1's strong example about *why this organisation*: "We exist because women in remote districts cannot reach legal help, and we have the trusted paralegal network to bring it to them."
- **High — 1.9 objective count.** Change P3 to "One or two external and two or three internal objectives is enough for most small or new organisations; larger organisations rarely need more than four of each." Add a decision rule: "If an objective would not change what you do next year, it is not strategic."
- **High — 1.3 P3 contradicts its own example.** The grid rule says high-interest/low-influence groups are "kept informed", but the strong example says young job-seekers (high interest, low influence) are "involve in design". Rewrite: "…keep informed those with high interest and low influence — **except the people you serve: involve them, even if they have little formal power.**"
- **Medium — 1.5 and 1.8 duplicate the "no" list.** 1.5 field 'boundaries' ("What we will not take on") and 1.8 field 'choices' ("What we will not do") ask the same thing. Keep it in 1.8 only; in 1.5 replace 'boundaries' with a missing 'change' field ("What changes for them?") so the fields match the formula "We help [people] achieve [change] through [approach]".
- **Medium — 1.2 is overloaded and SWOT is misplaced.** 1.2 asks for PESTLE + actors + SWOT in 20 minutes. Keep 1.2 external (PESTLE, actors, opportunities/threats); move strengths/weaknesses to 1.8 as the evidence base for comparative advantage.
- **Medium — 1.7 misses Global South realities.** Add to P4: "In some countries NGO registration, foreign funding or advocacy are restricted, and rules change quickly. Check current rules before choosing a form (see the CIVICUS Monitor from Lesson 1.2)." Also soften P2: "A company is usually not eligible for charitable grants or tax-deductible donations, though some impact funders and public programmes do fund companies."
- **Medium — Consultation is required but not taught.** 1.1 P3, 1.3, 1.4 'visionTest' all ask learners to talk to affected people. Add a 4-line box in 1.1: "Explain why you are asking and what you will (and won't) do with answers; get consent; don't promise services you can't yet offer; respect people's time — offer refreshments or travel costs; don't collect personal details you don't need."
- **Low — 1.7 title** "Organisational form and identity": identity is Module 7. Rename "Organisational form".

### Add / cut / merge / move
- **Move** 1.7 Form to after 1.9 Objectives: its own reflect question ("Where will most of your resources come from in three years?") depends on the objectives. New order: Purpose, Context, Stakeholders, Vision, Mission, Values, Positioning, Objectives, Form.
- **Add** to 1.10 review a one-page "Strategy on a page" view (vision, mission, values, position, objectives) instead of only the raw field dump from `output()`. This is the deliverable founders will actually share with boards and funders.

### Lesson notes
- 1.1 P4: "names the change that motivates you without promising more than you can influence" → "says what change you care about. Do not promise more than you can influence."
- 1.2 strong example: add a date and source to keep the rule it teaches: "…40% of young people in our 2026 survey of 120 (n=120) lack reliable internet."
- 1.8 'position' template: "unlike [alternatives]" reads as competitive in a collaborative sector; use "where others [what they do]".

## Module 02 · Theory of Change
### Verdict
Technically credible and current (spheres, backward mapping, contribution analysis, testing and versioning), and the assumptions and testing lessons (2.8, 2.9) are above the standard of most founder courses. But the middle of the module is thin and repetitive — four lessons of 77–98 words for 20 minutes each — and learners never see what a finished ToC looks like. Fix the depth gap with one complete example and a merged lesson, not with more text.

### Keep
- 2.1 P3 three spheres and "Your claims should become more modest as you move outwards."
- 2.2 problem tree with "Do this with people affected, not for them" and the data-caution sentence in P3.
- 2.4 'unintended' field ("Who might be excluded, burdened or affected differently?").
- 2.8 field structure "Connection | assumption | current evidence | test | decision if false" and the strong example with a low-cost test.
- 2.9 versioning example and "Who is best placed to tell you your pathway is wrong?"

### Adjust
- **High — Worked pathway and one-pathway-first** (see course table). The example should show problem → 2 activities → 3 outputs → 3 outcomes → impact, with 3 assumptions on the arrows and a 150-word narrative. Rewrite the intro and 2.1 P4: "Build your first pathway in full for ESO1. Add others in the workbook once it has been tested."
- **High — Impact vs long-term outcome vs ESO vs vision is unclear.** 2.4 sequences "short-term, medium-term and long-term outcomes" and 2.3 separately defines impact. Add to 2.3 P2: "Your vision is the whole future. Impact is the part of it this pathway contributes to. Your external objective is usually a medium- or long-term outcome you commit to reach in 3–5 years."
- **Medium — Name the logframe.** Experienced managers and funders will ask. Add to 2.1 P1: "Funders may ask for a logframe or results framework. These are tables built from your Theory of Change; you will create one in Module 6."
- **Medium — Outcome-writing rule missing (2.4).** Add a sentence pattern: "[Who] [now does / can / has] [what change]", e.g. "Partner employers accept applications without online-only registration." Link to 1.3: "Use your stakeholder map: whose behaviour must change?"
- **Medium — Align the running example with Module 1.** 2.2 and 2.3 target "young adults in one district"; 1.8/1.9 target young people leaving care and refer general job-seekers elsewhere. Use care leavers throughout — and point out that this is the positioning choice at work.
- **Medium — 2.9 testing ethics and time.** Add: "Use plain language, offer to cover participants' travel or time, and tell them what changed because of their input." Split the 20 minutes into "draft narrative (30 min)" and an offline step "test with 3–6 people (1–2 weeks)".
- **Low — 2.8 stage "Manage"** does not fit drafting assumptions; use "Design". Add a one-line prompt in 2.4–2.7 reflect: "Note any 'this must be true' thought — you'll collect them in 2.8."

### Add / cut / merge / move
- **Merge 2.5 Outputs, 2.6 Activities and 2.7 Inputs** into one lesson "Outputs, activities and inputs: the delivery chain" (~250 words, one table field "Output | quantity, reach, quality check | activities that produce it | inputs needed | available or gap"). It removes 40 minutes of repetition and makes the backward chain visible in one place. Implementation note: `pathway()` in learning.js reads lesson ids 'inputs', 'activities', 'outputs'; it must be updated if lessons are merged.
- **Add** "0. Problem" (2.2 'problem') as the first box of the review-page pathway strip, so the learner sees problem → impact.

### Lesson notes
- 2.1 why: "It names an additional condition between training and employment." → "It names what else must happen (employers changing how they recruit) and says 'may', because it is a hypothesis."
- 2.1 field 'logic': add example to hint: "If [people] [gain/do X], then [change], because [reason]."
- 2.2: open with "Start from the need you wrote in 1.1." to show it builds on, not repeats, 1.1.
- 2.3 P3: define attribution: "Attribution means claiming your work alone caused a change; contribution means showing it helped."
- 2.5 weak example "People are empowered" is an outcome-type error, not a vague output; use "Training delivered" (no quantity, reach or quality).
- 2.9 field 'narrative': hint should give a 5-heading skeleton (Problem · Change we seek · How we get there · What must be true · Our evidence and gaps), ~300 words.
