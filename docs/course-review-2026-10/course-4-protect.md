# Course 4 · Protect — panel review

Modules 09 Financial Management (`course/m09-financial-management.js`) and 10 Legal, Compliance, Policies & Risk (`course/m10-legal-compliance-risk.js`). 23 taught lessons plus 2 reviews, about 8 hours.

## Verdict

Protect covers the right ground in the right order for a founder: money first (budget, restricted funds, cash, controls, reporting, oversight), then duties, policies and risk. The content is accurate, and its caution about legal limits is better than in most paid founder courses. Both module intros say plainly that this is not an accounting qualification or legal advice. **Biggest strength:** concrete decision rules and honest prompts, such as the runway trigger in 9.5, compensating controls for small teams in 9.6, the now/soon/good-practice policy triage in 10.2, and reflect questions like "What if the concern is about the director?" (10.7). **Biggest problem:** the hardest practical tasks rest on very little teaching and an unworkable input format. A 12-month cash flow (9.5), a full budget (9.2), a legal map (10.1) and a data inventory (10.6) are each given about 130 words, 20 minutes and a pipe-delimited textarea. The Excel workbooks are still "being prepared" and the Excel panel appears below the form. Readability is the weakest of the five courses (average FRE 44; 10.3 Contracts scores 14). For a global audience there are also notable gaps: the module is centred on UK concepts, and safeguarding against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEAH), foreign-funding and NGO-registration laws, sanctions and counter-terrorist-financing checks, and cash or mobile-money controls are missing.

**Scores 1–5:** Clarity **4** (clear structure, most terms defined) · Readability **2** (long list-sentences; five lessons below FRE 33) · Depth **3** (right topics; 9.6, 10.5 and 10.9 too thin for their exercises) · Practicality **3** (good registers and triggers, but tabular work forced into textareas and unrealistic times) · Flow **3** (M9 logical; M10 uses the risk register before it is built, and the policy register is built twice) · Professional credibility **4** (accurate and cautious; missing PSEAH/SEAH, AML/CTF and foreign-funding rules; a UK-centric layer)

## Keep (what works — do not lose this)

- **9.1:** the three layers (bookkeeping / accounting / management finance) and "Separating these roles is itself a control." The reflect question ("If the person who manages your money was away for two months, who could pay the bills?") is excellent.
- **9.2:** income split into confirmed / likely / possible, plus a "reduced-spend scenario" field. This is the single most useful budgeting habit for small NGOs.
- **9.3:** full cost recovery and the overhead myth, supported by Humentum's global sources rather than UK ones.
- **9.5 and 9.7:** decision rules, not just definitions. "Below three months the director presents a recovery plan"; variances classified as timing, price or volume, then "accept, correct or re-forecast".
- **9.6:** "Small teams should identify compensating controls … rather than pretend that separation exists." Realistic and credible.
- **10.1:** the "confirmed by adviser?" column and the open-questions field turn "get legal advice" into a task.
- **10.2:** "A large folder of copied policies does not prove that people are protected", plus the now / soon / good-practice sort.
- **10.5:** "Do not place confidential case details in this learning workspace", and the reflect question about the newest volunteer.
- **10.11:** cause → event → consequence, inherent and residual scores, the laptop example, and "Scores support discussion; they do not make a judgement automatically correct."
- **10.12:** low risk appetite for safeguarding, fraud and legal integrity, with 48-hour escalation to the chair.
- **M10 resource curation:** global and multilingual (Safeguarding Resource and Support Hub in 18 languages, CHS Alliance, ICRC, UNCTAD, ICNL, TrustLaw).

## Module 09 · Financial Management

### Verdict
This is a sound, well-ordered module that follows the finance cycle (plan → record → control → report → review), and its examples carry real decision rules. Its weakness is depth at the two points where founders most often get into trouble: controls (9.6) and cash (9.5). It also has a few definitions an accountant would query: runway, reserves, and 'deferred' treated as a fund type.

### Keep
The lesson order; 9.3's "hidden or donated costs" field; 9.10's measures with targets ("Largest funder: 46% of income (limit 50%)"); everything listed under Keep above.

### Adjust
- **High · 9.6 Controls is too thin for the "in depth" that 5.6 promises.** Add a paragraph on cash and mobile money: "Cash and mobile money need extra care. Set a petty cash limit. Have two people count cash and sign for it. Get a signed or thumb-printed receipt for cash given to participants. Use mobile-money accounts in the organisation's name, never personal ones, and download the statements monthly." Add one line on funder screening: "Some funders require you to check suppliers and partners against sanctions lists; ask, and keep the record." Replace the strong example with illustrative thresholds: "Under US$300: one quote. US$300–3,000: three written quotes. Above US$3,000: director plus treasurer approve. Every payment has two approvers; the treasurer reviews the bank and mobile-money reconciliation each month (illustrative; set your own limits with your accountant)."
- **High · 9.5 Runway definition is ambiguous, and as written it overstates runway.** "With current cash and expected income if nothing new arrives" is self-contradictory, and it counts restricted cash. Rewrite P2: "Runway is the number of months you could keep going if no new money arrived. Take the cash you are free to use, add income you are sure of, and divide by your monthly core costs. Do not count restricted grant money: it can only pay for its own project." Change "seek a short-term facility" to "arrange a short-term loan or overdraft".
- **Medium · 9.2 is missing costs often left out in the Global South.** Add to P2: "Include staff on-costs (employer social security and any end-of-service or severance pay the law requires). If grants arrive in a foreign currency, note the exchange rate and inflation you assumed."
- **Medium · 9.4 Funds register mixes categories.** 'Deferred' is about timing, not a type of fund. Split the hint into "type (restricted, unrestricted, designated)" and "received in advance? (yes/no)". Add: "Tracking separately means a separate code in your accounts. You usually do not need a separate bank account unless the funder requires one."
- **Medium · 9.9 Reserves should mean free reserves.** Add: "Count only free reserves: unrestricted money you could actually spend, not designated funds or money tied up in buildings and equipment." Add: "Act on the auditor's or examiner's management letter: give each point an owner and a date."
- **Medium · Reserves, runway and months of reserves are used in three lessons and never linked** (9.5, 9.9, 9.10). Add to 9.10 P2: "Reserves are what you have saved; runway is how long your cash lasts. Track both."
- **Low · 9.8 stage label.** Reporting is routine work, so change it from Review to Manage. Seed the field from the 5.4 calendar ("Start from your Module 5 calendar and add every finance deadline").

### Add / cut / merge / move
- **Add** to the 9.11 review: a one-page **Finance Manual outline** generated from 9.1, 9.4, 9.6 and 9.9 (roles, approval limits, funds rules, reserves policy), plus the Adviser brief. Many funders' due-diligence checks ask for a finance manual, and the module currently produces only fragments.
- No cuts. If time must be trimmed, 9.8 could fold into 9.9 as "External reporting, audit and board oversight".

### Lesson notes
- **9.1 P1** (FRE 30): "Finance work has three layers. Bookkeeping records every payment and receipt. Accounting turns those records into official reports, such as annual accounts and tax returns. Management finance uses the numbers to plan and decide." Define "statutory accounts (the annual accounts the law requires)" on first use. Add tax and payroll registration to P4.
- **9.3 P3:** define the term: "Your indirect cost rate is your shared costs as a percentage of direct costs (or of total costs; funders differ, so say which)."
- **9.5 exercise:** "Draft a twelve-month cash flow forecast in the workbook sheet; here, record your lowest month, your runway and your trigger."
- **9.6 P4** (reads oddly before the learner has seen the resource): "Keep your rules proportionate to your size, and check them with an accountant who knows your legal form and country. The UK guidance below is a useful model, not a list of your legal duties."
- **9.7:** add one line: "If your books record only cash in and out, add unpaid bills and money owed to you, or your position will look better or worse than it is."
- **9.8 strong:** "annual accounts to the companies registry within nine months" (name the body, so the example does not suggest a universal deadline).

## Module 10 · Legal, Compliance, Policies & Risk

### Verdict
This is the most ambitious module in the course: 13 taught lessons, about 4.6 hours. It is careful, and it is honest that local advice is needed. Its three problems are readability (FRE 14–36 in seven lessons), sequencing and duplication (10.2 uses a risk register built in 10.11–10.12; a policy register is built in both 10.2 and 10.10; 10.8 and 10.9 repeat Modules 4 and 5), and gaps an international NGO reviewer would notice first: SEAH, foreign-funding laws, and sanctions and counter-terrorist-financing checks.

### Keep
The contracts register (10.3), the reporting-channel design (10.7) and the three risk lessons (10.11–10.13), plus everything listed under Keep above.

### Adjust
- **High · 10.5 Safeguarding: add SEAH and a survivor-centred approach.** Insert after P1: "This includes sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (SEAH) by anyone connected to your organisation, against the people you serve, staff or volunteers. Many funders and UN agencies require PSEAH (prevention of SEAH) measures before they fund you." Replace "referring to local authorities" with: "deciding on referral with the safeguarding lead, putting the survivor's safety and wishes first. For children, or where the law makes reporting compulsory, follow the local legal duty; in some places a referral can put an adult survivor at risk." Add a safer-recruitment point for contexts where police checks are unreliable: "Use references that ask about misconduct, a self-declaration and, where available, the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme." Define "adults at risk" on first use.
- **High · 10.1 Legal map: add the rules that can close an NGO.** Add P3: "In many countries NGOs must renew registration with an NGO authority, obtain approval to receive foreign funds, or report foreign income (for example India's FCRA). Anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing rules also affect how you move money and vet partners. Check these first." Promote the ICNL Civic Freedom Monitor from Go deeper to Core resource, and cross-reference TrustLaw here (it currently appears only in 10.3). Add "national NGO umbrella bodies and university law clinics" as low-cost sources of advice.
- **Medium · Sequence: move 10.11 Identifying and assessing risk to second place** (after the legal map). 10.2 P2 says "Then add the risks from your risk register" before any register exists, and 10.11 already draws on "your legal map". The register and monitoring lessons (10.12–10.13) stay at the end.
- **Medium · 10.4: define the term and add the realistic dilemma.** "Facilitation payments are small unofficial payments to make officials do routine work faster. They are illegal in many countries even where they are common. If someone's safety is threatened, safety comes first: pay, then report it at once." Add: "Financial crime also covers money laundering and funding of terrorism; screen partners and large suppliers if your funders require it." Link the conflict-of-interest and board interest declaration templates.
- **Medium · 10.6: define lawful basis and fix the special-category sentence.** "Lawful basis is the legal reason you may use someone's data, such as consent, a contract, a legal duty or legitimate interests; the names differ by country." "Sensitive data, such as health, ethnicity, religion, sexual life or biometrics, and data about children need extra protection." Add one line: "Cloud drives and messaging apps often store data in another country; check whether your law allows this." For concreteness, name a few laws (Kenya Data Protection Act 2019, Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, South Africa POPIA, Brazil LGPD, India DPDP Act 2023). Relabel field 'privacy' from "Information and safeguarding actions" (a mismatch) to **"Privacy actions"**.
- **Medium · Three policy registers.** 10.2 'policies' (owner, approval, review date), 10.4 'policies' (owner, review date) and 10.10 'register' collect the same columns, and 10.4 and 10.10 even share the sheet "Policies". Make 10.2 the needs list (policy, reason, priority, advice needed), have 10.4 add only the content of the core policies, and have 10.10 add the lifecycle columns to the same register.
- **Medium · 10.7:** "in good faith" is dated wording (UK law dropped the test in 2013). Use "who raise concerns honestly and with reasonable belief". State "working days" in the strong example. Add to the 'external' hint: "e.g. data breaches: some laws require notice within 72 hours (EU GDPR)". Start with "Participant feedback channels were designed in 6.9; here, add the routes for serious concerns."
- **Medium · 10.12:** add to the "transfer" response: "Insurance and contracts move the financial cost, not your responsibility, especially for safeguarding. Do not push risks onto smaller local partners without the resources to manage them." Then list the insurance to consider: public liability, employer's liability, professional indemnity, trustee or directors' liability, property, travel and medical evacuation, cyber.
- **Low · 10.11:** give scoring anchors in a hint or the sheet: Likelihood 1 = rare … 5 = expected this year; Impact 1 = minor inconvenience … 5 = serious harm to a person, closure, or loss of more than three months' income.

### Add / cut / merge / move
- **Merge 10.8 HR policies into 10.2.** Its unique content is the list of typical HR policies plus "check it legally for your country". Make that a checklist category in the 10.2 needs assessment; the design work is already in Module 4. This saves 20 minutes and reduces M10 to 12 taught lessons.
- **Refocus 10.9 Procedures** (83 words, the thinnest lesson) on a policy procedure, and say that it builds on 5.3. Replace the purchase example, which belongs to 9.6, with: "Lost phone with participant contacts: 1. Report to the data lead within one hour. 2. Lead changes passwords and wipes the device remotely. 3. Record it in the incident log. 4. Within 72 hours, decide with advice whether the regulator or the people affected must be told." Link the incident-reporting procedure template.
- **Add** to 10.13 P3: "For your top three risks, write a one-page 'if it happens' plan: who leads, who must be told, and the first three actions." This gives basic crisis and continuity planning without a new lesson.
- **Shorten** the lifecycle in 10.10 P1 from nine steps to five: draft and consult → approve → communicate and acknowledge → monitor → review.

### Lesson notes
- **10.1 P1** (FRE 32): "Your legal duties come from four places: your legal form (company, charity, association, foundation, cooperative), your activities (for example working with children, handling personal data, employing people, trading), each country where you work, and your funders, because grant conditions can be legally binding."
- **10.2 strong** (does not match the weak example or the exercise): "Policy register for a six-person youth sports NGO in Ghana. Now: safeguarding (we work with children), data protection (we hold participant records), anti-fraud (a grant condition). Soon: travel safety (field trips start next year). Good practice: environmental policy. Each has an owner; safeguarding and data protection checked by a local adviser."
- **10.3 P2** (FRE 14): "Before anyone signs, check: 1. Who are the parties, and may our signatory sign? 2. What must each side do, and by when? 3. When are we paid, and what must we report? 4. Who owns what we create, and how are data and confidential information protected? 5. If something goes wrong, who pays (liability and indemnity, a promise to cover the other side's losses), and what insurance is needed? 6. How can either side end it? 7. Which country's law applies? 8. Does everyone signing understand the language it is written in?" Point 8 matters for second-language founders. Then the 'checklist' field becomes "Adapt this checklist".
- **10.12 field 'register':** add "residual score" so the escalation rule in the strong example ("15 or more reported to the board") can be applied from the register itself.
