# The one example used across the entrepreneurs' courses

Every "More useful" example, every example table and every "e.g." placeholder in the entrepreneurs' courses
(course/eNN-*.js, course/founder-splits.js) follows the same fictional business, so learners watch one idea grow
from Module 00 to Module 10. It is not a real organisation; keep it unnamed ("our bakery", "the bakery").

## The bakery
- **What it is:** a neighbourhood bakery that trains young people. A social business: it sells bread and cakes, and
  uses the bakery to give young people who left school early a paid traineeship and a first job.
- **Founders:** two co-founders. One is a baker with ten years' experience in other people's bakeries; the other has
  worked with young people for six years (youth centre). No board yet; they plan an advisory group of three
  (an accountant, a youth-centre manager, a local shop owner).
- **Where:** one neighbourhood of a mid-sized city, near a weekly market. No country is named; money in US$.
- **Stage:** idea and pilot. Year 1: a Saturday market stall and a rented slot in a shared kitchen, four trainees in
  the pilot. Plan: a small shop of its own in year 2.
- **Customers:** neighbours and market shoppers; two local cafés and one office canteen buy wholesale.
- **Trainees:** young people aged 17–24 who left school early, referred by the youth centre; six-month paid
  traineeships (four a year at first), with a mentor and a basic food-hygiene certificate.
- **Form:** leaning towards a company with a social purpose (profits reinvested, social purpose written into its
  rules); staying informal for the first three months of the pilot. Check local rules with someone with a legal
  background before registering.
- **Money (year 1, illustrative):** costs about US$48,000 (kitchen rent, ingredients, trainee pay, stall, equipment);
  income from sales about US$38,000; US$6,000 from a local youth fund; US$4,000 of the founders' own savings.
- **Purpose:** "We exist so that young people who left school early can build skills and a working life, and our
  neighbourhood has good bread at a fair price."
- **Vision:** "A neighbourhood where every young person has a way into good work, and good food is affordable."
- **Mission:** "We bake and sell good bread, and train young people who left school early, through paid traineeships
  in a real bakery, starting with one market stall."
- **Values:** respect, honesty, openness, learning (each with what it means in practice).
- **Objectives:** ESO1 Position ourselves as the neighbourhood's trusted bakery for fresh, affordable bread.
  ESO2 Young people who left school early gain baking skills and a first paid job. ESO3 Local families buy good bread
  at a fair price, and less food is wasted. ISO1 Governance and legal. ISO2 Financial sustainability (sales cover core
  costs within 18 months). ISO3 People and skills (a small trained team and a safe training programme). ISO4 Systems
  and visibility (orders, money, stock; a brand people recognise).
- **Typical facts to reuse:** 4 trainees in the pilot; 3 of 4 finish; 2 find a job within three months; 300 loaves a
  week at the stall; food waste measured weekly; safeguarding matters because some trainees are 17.

Use other businesses only when a lesson genuinely needs a contrast (and say so). Never use the organisation
examples from the organisation courses (Imara, Pan del Barrio, Vanita Weavers, Riverside Repair Hub, Tide Line).
